Danantara Posts Rp259.6 Trillion Net Profit and Discloses US$634 Billion Asset Base in First Audited Annual Report

25–27 November 2026. Daya Anagata Nusantara (Danantara) / Ministry of State-Owned Enterprises / Bloomberg / Tempo / Antara / Jakarta Post.

Verified reporting

Indonesia's sovereign wealth fund Daya Anagata Nusantara (Danantara) has released its first audited financial report, disclosing a total asset base of approximately US$634 billion and net profit of Rp259.6 trillion (US$14.2 billion) for the 2025 fiscal year, according to filings reported by Bloomberg on 25 November. The report marks the first independent audit of the fund since its establishment in early 2025 under President Prabowo Subianto's economic programme to consolidate state-owned enterprise holdings under a single investment vehicle.

Revenue surged approximately 400 per cent compared to the aggregate pre-consolidation baseline of the constituent SOEs, according to data cited by Antara. The revenue increase reflects the consolidation of earnings from major state enterprises including Bank Mandiri, Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BRI), Telkom Indonesia, Pertamina, and other strategic state-owned companies whose equity stakes were transferred to Danantara's management. CEO Rosan Roeslani presented the results at an investor briefing, stating the fund aims to establish itself as a world-class sovereign wealth fund benchmarked against Singapore's GIC and Temasek by 2028 to 2029.

For the first nine months of 2026, Danantara reported net profit of Rp203.4 trillion, suggesting the fund is on track to exceed the full-year 2025 result. The 2026 performance has been driven by strong earnings at the banking subsidiaries — BRI and Bank Mandiri are among Indonesia's most profitable banks — as well as improved commodity prices benefiting Pertamina and mining-related SOEs.

President Prabowo highlighted the financial results as evidence that the consolidation strategy is delivering returns, crediting Danantara for gains across state firms. However, the Jakarta Post reported that transparency questions remain, with analysts noting the audit's scope, methodology, and the degree of independent oversight have not been fully disclosed. The fund's governing structure — specifically the degree of operational independence from the Ministry of State-Owned Enterprises and the presidential office — continues to attract scrutiny from institutional investors and governance watchdogs.

Our analysis

Danantara's first audited accounts represent a critical institutional milestone — the difference between a sovereign wealth fund that claims performance and one that can demonstrate it to international standards. The US$634 billion asset base places Danantara among the world's largest sovereign wealth funds by assets under management, comparable to Abu Dhabi's ADIA and significantly larger than Malaysia's Khazanah or the Philippines' Maharlika Fund. However, comparison requires caution: Danantara's assets are overwhelmingly equity stakes in operating SOEs rather than liquid financial assets, making the comparison with GIC or Temasek — which manage diversified global portfolios — structurally different. The Rp259.6 trillion net profit is impressive in absolute terms but needs contextualisation. The profit is largely a consolidation of earnings that the constituent SOEs were already generating — BRI alone reported net profit exceeding Rp60 trillion in 2025, and Bank Mandiri contributed a similar order of magnitude. The 400 per cent revenue surge cited by Antara is an artefact of the consolidation accounting: Danantara is now reporting the combined revenues of entities that previously filed separately. The more meaningful test of value creation is whether Danantara can generate returns above what the SOEs would have delivered independently — through better capital allocation, coordinated investment strategies, or operational improvements. The governance question is the central risk factor. Indonesia's history with state-managed investment vehicles includes both successes and costly failures, and the Danantara model concentrates enormous economic power in a single entity with direct connections to the presidency. The 2028-2029 timeline to achieve world-class status requires not just financial performance but transparent governance, independent board oversight, and demonstrated resistance to political interference — none of which can be established quickly. International co-investors — pension funds, endowments, and other sovereign wealth funds — will be watching the governance framework as closely as the financial returns before committing capital alongside Danantara.

What we don't know yet

The full scope of the audit — which accounting firm conducted it, what standards were applied, and whether it covers all consolidated entities. The breakdown of Danantara's US$634 billion asset base by entity and asset class. Whether the fund uses external leverage or operates entirely from its equity base. The return on equity and return on assets metrics that would allow comparison with GIC and Temasek as stated benchmarks. The fund's investment pipeline for 2027 and the allocation between domestic strategic investments and international diversification. Whether the governance structure provides sufficient independence from political direction to attract meaningful international co-investment. How the fund's performance accounting adjusts for the fact that many constituent SOEs benefit from regulatory advantages, monopoly positions, or government contracts that inflate returns relative to commercial benchmarks.

达南塔拉首份经审计年报披露净利润259.6万亿印尼盾及6340亿美元资产规模

2026年11月25–27日。国家未来力量（达南塔拉）/ 国有企业部 / 彭博社 / Tempo / 安塔拉 / 雅加达邮报。

经核实的报道

根据彭博社11月25日报道的文件，印尼主权财富基金"国家未来力量"（Daya Anagata Nusantara，简称达南塔拉）已发布其首份经审计财务报告，披露总资产规模约6,340亿美元，2025财年净利润259.6万亿印尼盾（142亿美元）。该报告是该基金自2025年初在普拉博沃·苏比安托总统经济计划下成立以来的首次独立审计，该计划旨在将国有企业持股整合到单一投资载体之下。

根据安塔拉引用的数据，收入较合并前各组成国企的基线水平飙升约400%。收入增长反映了主要国有企业收益的合并，包括曼迪利银行、印尼人民银行（BRI）、印尼电信（Telkom）、国家石油公司（Pertamina）以及其他股权转移至达南塔拉管理的战略性国有企业。首席执行官罗桑·鲁斯拉尼在投资者简报会上展示了业绩，表示该基金旨在2028至2029年建立与新加坡GIC和淡马锡对标的世界级主权财富基金。

2026年前九个月，达南塔拉报告净利润203.4万亿印尼盾，表明该基金有望超过2025年全年业绩。2026年表现由银行子公司的强劲盈利驱动——BRI和曼迪利银行是印尼最赚钱的银行之一——以及大宗商品价格改善使Pertamina和矿业相关国企受益。

普拉博沃总统将财务业绩视为合并战略正在产生回报的证据，将国有企业的收益归功于达南塔拉。然而，雅加达邮报报道透明度问题仍然存在，分析师指出审计的范围、方法论和独立监督程度尚未充分披露。该基金的治理结构——特别是相对于国有企业部和总统办公室的运营独立程度——继续受到机构投资者和治理监督机构的审视。

原创分析

达南塔拉的首份经审计年报代表着关键的制度里程碑——它区分了一个声称有业绩的主权财富基金和一个能够按国际标准证明业绩的基金。6,340亿美元的资产规模使达南塔拉跻身全球最大的主权财富基金之列，与阿布扎比的ADIA相当，远大于马来西亚的国库控股或菲律宾的马哈利卡基金。然而，比较需要谨慎：达南塔拉的资产绝大多数是运营国企的股权而非流动金融资产，使得与管理多元化全球投资组合的GIC或淡马锡的比较存在结构性差异。259.6万亿印尼盾的净利润在绝对值上令人印象深刻，但需要背景化理解。利润很大程度上是各组成国企本已产生的收益的合并——仅BRI一家2025年净利润就超过60万亿印尼盾，曼迪利银行贡献了类似量级。安塔拉引用的收入飙升400%是合并会计的产物：达南塔拉现在报告的是此前分别申报的实体的合并收入。更有意义的价值创造检验是达南塔拉能否产生超过国企独立运营时的回报——通过更好的资本配置、协调投资策略或运营改进。治理问题是核心风险因素。印尼国家管理投资载体的历史包括成功和昂贵的失败，达南塔拉模式将巨大的经济权力集中在与总统直接关联的单一实体中。2028-2029年达到世界级地位的时间表不仅需要财务业绩，还需要透明治理、独立董事会监督和证明抵御政治干预的能力——这些都无法快速建立。国际联合投资者——养老基金、捐赠基金和其他主权财富基金——在承诺与达南塔拉并肩投资之前，将像关注财务回报一样密切关注治理框架。

未知事项

审计的完整范围——由哪家会计师事务所执行、适用何种标准、是否覆盖所有合并实体。达南塔拉6,340亿美元资产基础按实体和资产类别的分解。该基金是否使用外部杠杆还是完全从其股权基础运营。允许与GIC和淡马锡作为声明基准进行比较的股本回报率和资产回报率指标。该基金2027年的投资管线以及在国内战略投资和国际多元化之间的配置。治理结构是否提供足够的独立性以吸引有意义的国际联合投资。该基金的业绩核算如何调整许多组成国企受益于监管优势、垄断地位或政府合同这一事实，这些因素相对于商业基准会抬高回报。

Nvidia Halts Server Shipments to Malaysia as New US AI Chip Export Controls Threaten Southeast Asia's Data Centre Boom

25–27 November 2026. US Department of Commerce / Nvidia / Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry / The Star / The Edge / Rakyat Post / Scoop.

Verified reporting

Nvidia has halted shipments of AI server systems to Malaysia and other Southeast Asian countries following the implementation of expanded US export controls on advanced AI chips, according to reports from The Star and Scoop on 26 November. The suspension affects shipments of complete server systems containing Nvidia's latest-generation AI accelerators, including the H200 and B200 series, which require individual export licences under the new regulatory framework.

The expanded controls, announced by the US Department of Commerce in November 2026, extend the existing AI chip export restrictions — previously focused primarily on China — to cover Southeast Asian destinations including Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, and Indonesia. The stated rationale is to prevent China from circumventing chip restrictions by accessing advanced AI computing capacity through data centres in neighbouring countries. The new rules require US companies to obtain individual validated licences for shipping advanced AI chips to ASEAN countries, replacing the previous general authorisation that allowed most commercial shipments.

The impact on Malaysia is particularly significant given the country's position as Southeast Asia's leading data centre hub. Malaysia has approved over 117 data centre projects with combined investment commitments exceeding US$50 billion, many of which depend on access to Nvidia's latest AI accelerators. Investment and Trade Minister Tengku Zafrul confirmed that the Malaysian government is in active discussions with US counterparts to negotiate a framework that would allow continued shipments to verified commercial operators while addressing US national security concerns about Chinese access to computing infrastructure.

Share prices of data centre-related companies across Southeast Asia fell sharply following the announcement, with Malaysian-listed YTL Power, Tenaga Nasional, and data centre REITs all declining. The Edge reported that the uncertainty has caused several planned data centre investments to pause pending clarity on the licensing regime. Industry analysts noted that the controls could redirect data centre investment from ASEAN toward US-allied destinations with pre-approved chip access, such as Japan, South Korea, and Australia.

Our analysis

The expansion of US AI chip export controls to Southeast Asia represents the most significant geopolitical disruption to the region's technology sector since the original China-focused restrictions began in 2022. For Malaysia specifically, the timing is devastating: the country has spent two years positioning itself as a global data centre hub, attracting commitments from Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Nvidia itself, and ByteDance, and the October trade data showing RM436.61 billion in monthly trade — driven overwhelmingly by E&E exports — was celebrated as validation of that strategy just days ago. The core tension is that Malaysia's data centre proposition was built on two pillars that are now in conflict: cost-competitive access to advanced chips, and openness to investment from all sources including Chinese technology companies. The US controls are explicitly designed to prevent Chinese entities from accessing advanced AI computing capacity through third-country data centres. If Malaysia cannot demonstrate to US satisfaction that Chinese operators are not accessing restricted computing power — either through direct investment, cloud services, or remote access arrangements — the licensing framework could become permanently restrictive. The practical impact depends on how the licensing regime is administered. If individual validated licences are granted efficiently for verified commercial operators — hyperscale cloud providers, sovereign AI projects, enterprise data centres — the disruption may be manageable. If the process is slow, opaque, or politically contingent, the competitive damage to Malaysia's data centre sector could be lasting. The comparison with Malaysia's semiconductor position is instructive: testing and packaging operations are less affected because they handle chips after manufacture, while data centres are consuming the chips that the controls target. This means the same AI supply chain that is driving Malaysia's record trade performance is now bifurcated — the manufacturing segment continues to boom, but the deployment segment faces a regulatory chokepoint controlled by Washington.

What we don't know yet

The specific licensing criteria the US will apply to Southeast Asian data centre operators, and the expected processing timeline for individual validated licences. Whether Nvidia's shipment halt is a temporary precaution pending licensing clarity or reflects a longer-term supply disruption. The scale of Chinese investment in Malaysian data centres and the specific arrangements — joint ventures, cloud access, remote computing — that triggered US concerns. Whether Microsoft, Google, and Amazon's Malaysian data centre projects are affected, given that US hyperscalers may receive expedited licensing treatment. How Malaysia will balance its economic interest in maintaining data centre investment from all sources against the US requirement to restrict Chinese access. Whether competing data centre destinations — particularly Singapore, Japan, and Australia — will gain share as a result of the regulatory uncertainty. The impact on Malaysia's projected 2027 trade performance if data centre equipment imports are delayed or redirected.

美国AI芯片出口管制新规下英伟达暂停对马来西亚服务器发货，威胁东南亚数据中心繁荣

2026年11月25–27日。美国商务部 / 英伟达 / 投资贸易及工业部 / 星报 / The Edge / Rakyat Post / Scoop。

经核实的报道

据星报和Scoop 11月26日报道，在美国扩大对先进AI芯片出口管制后，英伟达已暂停向马来西亚和其他东南亚国家发运AI服务器系统。暂停影响包含英伟达最新一代AI加速器的完整服务器系统，包括H200和B200系列，这些产品在新监管框架下需要单独的出口许可证。

美国商务部于2026年11月宣布的扩大管制将现有AI芯片出口限制——此前主要针对中国——扩展到包括马来西亚、泰国、越南和印尼在内的东南亚目的地。所述理由是防止中国通过邻国数据中心获取先进AI计算能力来规避芯片限制。新规要求美国公司获得单独验证许可证才能向东盟国家运送先进AI芯片，取代了此前允许大多数商业发货的通用授权。

鉴于马来西亚作为东南亚领先数据中心枢纽的地位，对该国的影响尤为显著。马来西亚已批准超过117个数据中心项目，合计投资承诺超过500亿美元，其中许多依赖于获取英伟达最新AI加速器。投资贸易及工业部长东姑扎弗鲁确认，马来西亚政府正在与美国对等方积极讨论，以谈判一个框架，允许继续向经验证的商业运营商发货，同时解决美国对中国获取计算基础设施的国家安全担忧。

公告发布后，东南亚数据中心相关公司股价大幅下跌，马来西亚上市的杨忠礼电力、国家能源和数据中心REITs均有所下滑。The Edge报道不确定性已导致数个计划中的数据中心投资暂停，等待许可制度的明确。行业分析师指出，管制可能将数据中心投资从东盟转向具有预先批准芯片获取渠道的美国盟友目的地，如日本、韩国和澳大利亚。

原创分析

美国AI芯片出口管制扩展到东南亚是自2022年最初针对中国的限制以来对该地区科技行业最重大的地缘政治干扰。对马来西亚而言，时机极为不利：该国花了两年时间将自己定位为全球数据中心枢纽，吸引了微软、谷歌、亚马逊、英伟达本身和字节跳动的投资承诺，而就在数天前，10月贸易数据显示月度贸易额达4,366.1亿令吉——主要由电子电气出口驱动——被誉为该战略的验证。核心矛盾在于马来西亚的数据中心价值主张建立在两个现在相互冲突的支柱上：具有成本竞争力的先进芯片获取渠道，以及对包括中国科技公司在内的所有来源投资的开放性。美国管制明确旨在防止中国实体通过第三国数据中心获取先进AI计算能力。如果马来西亚无法向美国证明中国运营商没有通过直接投资、云服务或远程访问安排获取受限计算能力，许可框架可能变成永久性限制。实际影响取决于许可制度的执行方式。如果为经验证的商业运营商——超大规模云提供商、主权AI项目、企业数据中心——高效授予单独验证许可证，干扰可能是可控的。如果流程缓慢、不透明或受政治因素影响，对马来西亚数据中心行业的竞争损害可能是持久的。与马来西亚半导体地位的比较具有启示性：测试和封装业务受影响较小，因为它们在制造后处理芯片，而数据中心正在消耗管制所针对的芯片。这意味着推动马来西亚创纪录贸易表现的同一AI供应链现在被分割——制造环节继续繁荣，但部署环节面临华盛顿控制的监管瓶颈。

未知事项

美国将对东南亚数据中心运营商适用的具体许可标准，以及单独验证许可证的预期处理时间。英伟达的发货暂停是等待许可明确期间的临时预防措施还是反映更长期的供应中断。中国在马来西亚数据中心的投资规模以及触发美国担忧的具体安排——合资企业、云访问、远程计算。微软、谷歌和亚马逊的马来西亚数据中心项目是否受到影响，鉴于美国超大规模运营商可能获得加速许可处理。马来西亚将如何平衡维持来自所有来源的数据中心投资的经济利益与美国限制中国获取的要求。竞争性数据中心目的地——特别是新加坡、日本和澳大利亚——是否会因监管不确定性而获得份额。如果数据中心设备进口被延迟或转移，对马来西亚2027年预计贸易表现的影响。

S&P Slashes Philippines Growth Forecast to 2.4% Through 2028 as Deutsche Bank Warns of Prolonged Stagflation

23–27 November 2026. S&P Global Ratings / Deutsche Bank / Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas / Philippine Statistics Authority / BusinessWorld / PhilStar.

Verified reporting

S&P Global Ratings has slashed its Philippines economic growth forecast to 2.4 per cent for 2026, down from its previous estimate of 4.1 per cent set in June, with the downgrade extending through 2028, according to a report published by BusinessWorld on 23 November. The revision represents the sharpest growth downgrade S&P has applied to any Asian economy this year, reflecting the severity of the Philippines' stagflation challenge.

Deutsche Bank joined the downgrade wave on 24 November, cutting its own Philippines growth forecast and warning that the country faces prolonged stagflation that could persist through 2028, as reported by PhilStar. The twin downgrades from two of the world's most prominent financial institutions underscore a growing consensus that the Philippines' economic difficulties are structural rather than cyclical.

The downgrades follow third-quarter GDP data showing growth of just 2.1 per cent year-on-year — the weakest quarterly performance since the pandemic recovery — while October headline inflation remained elevated at 7.8 per cent. The combination of sub-3 per cent growth with near-8 per cent inflation places the Philippines as the worst-performing major economy in ASEAN on both metrics simultaneously. S&P cited weakening government spending, persistent supply-side inflation in food and energy, and tightening financial conditions as the primary drivers of the downgrade.

The BSP's benchmark overnight reverse repurchase rate stands at 5.50 per cent after cumulative hikes of 125 basis points in 2026, yet real interest rates remain deeply negative at approximately minus 2.3 per cent when adjusted for headline inflation. Governor Eli Remolona has signalled that the pace of rate hikes may moderate, but the central bank has explicitly ruled out rate cuts while inflation remains above its 2 to 4 per cent target band. The Philippine Stock Exchange Index has declined approximately 28 per cent year-to-date with net foreign portfolio outflows of PHP 123 billion.

Our analysis

The S&P and Deutsche Bank downgrades are significant not only for their magnitude — cutting from 4.1 per cent to 2.4 per cent in five months is a severe revision — but for the signal they send about international institutional confidence in the Philippines' economic trajectory. When two of the most influential providers of economic forecasts independently reach the same conclusion within 48 hours of each other, it typically reflects a consensus view among the analytical community that the data has moved decisively against the country. The extension of the downgrade through 2028 is the most concerning element. This is not a forecast of a cyclical dip followed by recovery; it is a projection that the Philippines will underperform ASEAN peers for at least two more years. The implication is that the stagflation is being viewed as a structural trap — high food and energy inflation driven by supply-side factors that monetary tightening cannot resolve, combined with demand weakness driven by high interest rates, weakening government spending, and deteriorating business confidence. Thailand Launches TouristDigiPay Sandbox Allowing Foreign Visitors to Spend Stablecoins as Baht via QR Codes

13–27 November 2026. Securities and Exchange Commission Thailand / Bank of Thailand / Bloomberg / Blockhead / CoinTelegraph.

Verified reporting

Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has launched the TouristDigiPay regulatory sandbox, allowing foreign tourists to convert cryptocurrency — primarily USDT stablecoins — into Thai baht for retail spending through QR code payments, according to Bloomberg and multiple industry publications. The sandbox, which went live in November 2026, represents one of the most ambitious regulatory experiments in ASEAN linking digital assets to mainstream retail payments.

The first approved application operating within the sandbox is WORPT, a mobile wallet that enables tourists to load USDT (Tether) stablecoins and spend them at participating merchants through Thailand's PromptPay QR code infrastructure. When a tourist scans a merchant's QR code, WORPT instantly converts the stablecoin to Thai baht at the prevailing exchange rate, and the merchant receives settlement in baht — eliminating crypto volatility risk for retailers. The process mirrors the familiar experience of paying with a foreign currency card, but uses stablecoins as the funding source instead of a traditional bank account.

The SEC's sandbox framework establishes strict parameters for the pilot: only foreign passport holders can use the service, only stablecoins pegged to major currencies are accepted as funding sources, and all transactions are subject to anti-money-laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) requirements. Participating merchants receive payments in baht through existing banking channels, requiring no cryptocurrency infrastructure on their end. The pilot initially covers merchants in major tourist areas including Bangkok, Phuket, and Chiang Mai.

The initiative aligns with the Thai government's efforts to revive tourism spending, which has been declining — total arrivals for January to October 2026 reached 25.46 million, down 4.59 per cent year-on-year, with the revised full-year target lowered to 31.5 million from 2025's actual 32.97 million. The TouristDigiPay sandbox is designed to attract crypto-native travellers — a demographic that skews younger and higher-spending — by removing the friction of traditional foreign exchange while maintaining regulatory oversight.

Our analysis

Thailand's TouristDigiPay sandbox is a genuinely innovative regulatory approach that positions the country at the intersection of two important trends: the mainstreaming of stablecoin payments and the competition among tourist destinations for high-spending digital-native travellers. The design is clever in several respects. By using Thailand's existing PromptPay QR infrastructure for settlement, the sandbox avoids the need to build parallel crypto payment rails — merchants receive baht through familiar channels, lowering the adoption barrier to near zero on the retail side. By restricting the service to foreign tourists with passport verification, the SEC limits the domestic regulatory complexity and frames the initiative as tourism promotion rather than a broad deregulation of crypto payments. The choice of USDT as the primary stablecoin is pragmatic rather than ideological — Tether is the most widely held stablecoin globally, and crypto-native travellers are most likely to hold USDT balances. However, this choice exposes the system to Tether's specific risks, including ongoing questions about reserve backing and regulatory status. The connection to Thailand's tourism challenge is direct. The 4.59 per cent decline in tourist arrivals and the reduction of the full-year target to 31.5 million — well below the pre-pandemic peak of 40 million — reflects intensifying competition from Vietnam, Japan, and other destinations. TouristDigiPay is unlikely to reverse the tourism decline on its own, but it could attract a specific segment of high-value travellers: crypto professionals, digital nomads, and younger travellers who hold significant stablecoin balances and prefer to spend directly rather than converting through traditional foreign exchange. The broader significance is regulatory. Thailand's SEC is effectively creating a template for supervised crypto-to-fiat retail payments that other ASEAN regulators may study. If the sandbox demonstrates that stablecoin payments can operate within AML/KYC frameworks without creating systemic risks, it could inform regulatory approaches across the region.

What we don't know yet

The number of merchants currently participating in the pilot and the geographic coverage beyond the initial tourist areas. 原创分析

泰国的TouristDigiPay沙盒是一种真正创新的监管方法，将该国定位在两个重要趋势的交汇处：稳定币支付的主流化和旅游目的地之间争夺高消费数字原生旅客的竞争。设计在几个方面很巧妙。通过使用泰国现有的PromptPay二维码基础设施进行结算，沙盒避免了建设平行加密支付轨道的需要——商户通过熟悉的渠道接收泰铢，将零售端的采用门槛降至接近零。通过将服务限制为持护照验证的外国游客，SEC限制了国内监管复杂性，将该倡议定位为旅游推广而非加密支付的广泛放松监管。选择USDT作为主要稳定币是务实的而非意识形态的——泰达币是全球持有最广泛的稳定币，加密原生旅客最有可能持有USDT余额。然而，这一选择使系统暴露于泰达币的特定风险，包括关于储备支持和监管地位的持续问题。与泰国旅游挑战的联系是直接的。游客入境人数4.59%的下降和全年目标降至3,150万——远低于疫情前4,000万的峰值——反映了来自越南、日本和其他目的地日益激烈的竞争。TouristDigiPay不太可能独自扭转旅游下滑，但可以吸引特定的高价值旅客群体：加密专业人士、数字游民和持有大量稳定币余额且倾向直接消费而非通过传统外汇兑换的年轻旅客。更广泛的意义在于监管。泰国SEC实际上正在创建一个受监督的加密货币到法定货币零售支付模板，其他东盟监管机构可能会研究。如果沙盒证明稳定币支付可以在AML/KYC框架内运营而不产生系统性风险，它可能为整个地区的监管方法提供参考。

未知事项

目前参与试点的商户数量以及初始旅游区以外的地理覆盖范围。沙盒上线以来的交易量和平均交易规模。SEC是否计划将沙盒扩展到包括USDT以外的其他稳定币，以及USDC或其他受监管稳定币是否会被优先考虑。决定使TouristDigiPay永久化或延长沙盒期的时间表。泰国国内居民最终是否会被允许使用类似的稳定币支付服务。泰国银行对沙盒的看法——特别是央行是否将稳定币支付视为与其数字泰铢CBDC开发互补还是竞争。该计划产生了多少增量旅游消费，以及加密原生旅客的目标群体是否对该倡议作出回应。

AI-Related Boom Drives Record Rise in ASEAN Manufacturing New Orders as Singapore Factory Output Jumps 29.1%

17–27 November 2026. S&P Global Market Intelligence / Economic Development Board / SIPMM / Straits Times / AsiaOne.

Verified reporting

AI-related demand has driven a record rise in ASEAN manufacturing new orders, according to S&P Global Market Intelligence research published on 17 November. The research, based on the ASEAN Manufacturing Purchasing Managers' Index survey data, found that new order growth across the ASEAN manufacturing sector reached its highest level on record, with the AI semiconductor and data centre equipment supply chain identified as the primary driver of the expansion.

Singapore's manufacturing sector has been a principal beneficiary of the AI-driven boom. The Economic Development Board reported that October factory output jumped 29.1 per cent year-on-year, eclipsing consensus forecasts, according to the Straits Times. The electronics cluster led the surge, with semiconductor and precision engineering sub-sectors posting particularly strong gains driven by global demand for AI chips, advanced packaging, and data centre components.

The Singapore Manufacturing PMI registered 51.4 in October, moderating slightly from 51.7 in September but maintaining expansion territory for a sustained period. The dip from September reflected a slight normalisation in delivery times rather than weakening demand — new orders and output sub-indices remained firmly in expansion. Industry executives surveyed by SIPMM remained upbeat on AI demand despite geopolitical clouds, with order backlogs at elevated levels and production capacity utilisation running above 80 per cent across the semiconductor segment.

Across the broader ASEAN region, manufacturing PMI readings reflected a broad-based expansion. The S&P Global research noted that the AI investment cycle has created a multiplier effect across the ASEAN supply chain: advanced chip production in Singapore feeds into testing and packaging operations in Malaysia, component manufacturing in Vietnam and Thailand, and assembly operations across the region. The report estimated that AI-related manufacturing activity now accounts for approximately 15 to 20 per cent of total ASEAN manufacturing new orders, up from less than 5 per cent two years ago.

Our analysis

The S&P Global finding that ASEAN manufacturing new orders have reached a record high — driven overwhelmingly by AI demand — confirms that the AI investment cycle has become the dominant growth engine for Southeast Asian manufacturing. This is a structural shift of historical significance. For decades, ASEAN manufacturing was primarily driven by consumer electronics, automotive parts, and commodity processing. The AI supply chain has introduced a new, higher-value layer that is transforming the region's industrial profile. Singapore's 29.1 per cent jump in October factory output is an extraordinary number that reflects the country's unique positioning at the apex of the ASEAN semiconductor supply chain. Singapore's factories produce the highest-value components — advanced semiconductors, precision engineering parts, and sophisticated manufacturing equipment — that feed into the broader regional production network. The 29.1 per cent growth rate, while partly amplified by base effects, represents a genuine acceleration in output volumes driven by hyperscale data centre buildout programmes from Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta. The record ASEAN new orders data needs to be read alongside the Malaysia chip controls story. The US export control expansion threatens to disrupt precisely the AI supply chain that is driving the ASEAN manufacturing boom. If Nvidia server shipments remain suspended and licensing requirements create delays, the downstream effects could ripple through the entire ASEAN semiconductor ecosystem — from Singapore's wafer fabrication to Malaysia's testing operations to Vietnam's component assembly. The concentration risk is significant. When AI-related activity accounts for 15 to 20 per cent of total ASEAN manufacturing new orders — and is growing rapidly — the region's manufacturing sector becomes increasingly leveraged to the AI investment cycle. A correction in AI capital spending, whether from overbuilding of data centre capacity, a reassessment of AI returns, or export control disruptions, would have proportionally larger effects on ASEAN manufacturing than on the United States or China, where domestic demand provides a larger buffer.

What we don't know yet

The November ASEAN PMI data and whether the record new orders trend has continued or moderated following the US chip export control announcement. The specific breakdown of AI-related versus non-AI manufacturing growth across individual ASEAN countries. Whether Singapore's 29.1 per cent output jump is sustainable or represents a temporary surge driven by front-loading of orders. The impact of US export controls on ASEAN new order pipelines — specifically whether orders have been cancelled, deferred, or redirected. How much of the AI-driven manufacturing boom is generating domestic employment and wage growth versus accruing primarily to foreign-invested enterprises. Whether the ASEAN manufacturing expansion is broadening beyond the AI semiconductor supply chain to include other technology segments such as EV components and renewable energy equipment. The capacity constraints facing the ASEAN semiconductor supply chain and whether bottlenecks in power supply, water availability, or skilled labour are emerging in key manufacturing zones.

AI热潮推动东盟制造业新订单创纪录增长，新加坡工厂产出飙升29.1%

2026年11月17–27日。标普全球市场情报 / 经济发展局 / 新加坡制造商联合会 / 海峡时报 / AsiaOne。

经核实的报道

据标普全球市场情报11月17日发布的研究，AI相关需求推动东盟制造业新订单创纪录增长。该研究基于东盟制造业采购经理指数调查数据，发现东盟制造业新订单增长达到历史最高水平，AI半导体和数据中心设备供应链被认定为扩张的主要驱动力。

新加坡制造业是AI驱动繁荣的主要受益者。据海峡时报报道，经济发展局报告10月工厂产出同比飙升29.1%，远超市场预期。电子集群引领了飙升，半导体和精密工程子行业在全球对AI芯片、先进封装和数据中心组件需求的驱动下录得特别强劲的增长。

新加坡制造业PMI在10月录得51.4，较9月的51.7略有放缓但维持在扩张区间。从9月的回落反映的是交货时间的轻微正常化而非需求减弱——新订单和产出分项指数仍牢牢处于扩张区间。新加坡制造商联合会调查的行业高管对AI需求保持乐观，尽管存在地缘政治阴影，订单积压处于高位，半导体领域产能利用率运行在80%以上。

在更广泛的东盟地区，制造业PMI读数反映了广泛的扩张。标普全球研究指出，AI投资周期在东盟供应链中创造了乘数效应：新加坡的先进芯片生产输入马来西亚的测试和封装运营、越南和泰国的组件制造，以及整个地区的组装运营。该报告估计AI相关制造活动现在约占东盟制造业新订单总量的15%至20%，两年前不到5%。

原创分析

标普全球发现东盟制造业新订单达到创纪录高位——压倒性地由AI需求驱动——确认AI投资周期已成为东南亚制造业的主导增长引擎。这是具有历史意义的结构性转变。几十年来，东盟制造业主要由消费电子、汽车零部件和大宗商品加工驱动。AI供应链引入了一个新的、更高价值的层面，正在改变该地区的工业面貌。新加坡10月工厂产出29.1%的飙升是一个非凡的数字，反映了该国在东盟半导体供应链顶端的独特定位。新加坡工厂生产最高价值的组件——先进半导体、精密工程零件和精密制造设备——为更广泛的区域生产网络提供输入。29.1%的增长率虽然部分受基数效应放大，但代表着产出量的真正加速，由微软、谷歌、亚马逊和Meta的超大规模数据中心建设项目驱动。东盟新订单创纪录的数据需要与马来西亚芯片管制的报道对照阅读。美国出口管制扩展有可能干扰正是驱动东盟制造业繁荣的AI供应链。如果英伟达服务器发货持续暂停且许可要求造成延迟，下游效应可能波及整个东盟半导体生态系统——从新加坡的晶圆制造到马来西亚的测试运营到越南的组件组装。集中度风险显著。当AI相关活动占东盟制造业新订单总量的15%至20%——且快速增长时——该地区的制造业越来越依赖AI投资周期。AI资本支出的修正——无论是数据中心产能过剩、对AI回报的重新评估还是出口管制干扰——对东盟制造业的影响将按比例大于对美国或中国的影响，因为后者有更大的国内需求缓冲。

未知事项

11月东盟PMI数据以及创纪录的新订单趋势在美国芯片出口管制宣布后是否继续或有所放缓。AI相关与非AI制造业增长在各东盟国家之间的具体分解。新加坡29.1%的产出飙升是否可持续还是由订单前置驱动的暂时激增。美国出口管制对东盟新订单管线的影响——具体而言订单是否被取消、推迟或转移。AI驱动的制造业繁荣有多少正在产生国内就业和工资增长，而非主要归属于外资企业。东盟制造业扩张是否正在从AI半导体供应链扩展到其他技术领域，如电动车组件和可再生能源设备。东盟半导体供应链面临的产能约束，以及关键制造区是否出现电力供应、水资源或技术劳动力的瓶颈。
