Asian stocks suffer sharpest selloff in months as AI bubble fears spread

Event: 6 November 2026. Multiple market sources.

Verified reporting

Asian equity markets saw their largest single-day losses in months on 6 November. South Korea's KOSPI fell 8.6% to 4,923, triggering a 20-minute trading halt. Japan's Nikkei 225 dropped about 6%. Taiwan's TAIEX fell more than 8%. The selloff followed a 5.52% decline on the Nasdaq Composite and a more than 12% drop in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index overnight.

The Institute of International Finance reported emerging-market net portfolio outflows of US$33.8 billion in October — the largest monthly outflow since March. Equity outflows totalled US$31.7 billion, with emerging Asia accounting for US$28.7 billion. Taiwan alone saw US$31.8 billion in equity sales, the largest monthly figure in IIF records dating to 2000.

In Southeast Asia, the Straits Times Index in Singapore fell sharply at market open, weighed down by the three major banks. Indonesia's Jakarta Composite Index also declined. The Philippine peso, already under pressure, weakened further to near P64 per US dollar. Regional currencies faced broad selling pressure as risk appetite evaporated.

Our analysis

The selloff was triggered by a combination of AI valuation doubts, profit-taking after strong gains, and warnings from major bank CEOs about potential 10–20% equity declines. For ASEAN businesses, the immediate question is whether this capital flight extends to regional markets and currencies, or remains concentrated in the tech-heavy North Asian exchanges. The Straits Times Index's decline underscores how interconnected regional banking and trade-dependent economies are to global tech sentiment. Southeast Asian central banks may face a dual challenge: supporting currencies while maintaining growth-friendly monetary conditions.

Unknowns & what comes next

Whether this is a correction or the start of a broader downturn depends on upcoming tech earnings, US Federal Reserve signals, and whether AI investment commitments hold. Watch Southeast Asian currency and bond-flow data in coming weeks.

AI泡沫忧虑蔓延，亚洲股市遭遇数月来最大跌幅

事件：2026年11月6日。来源：多家市场报道。

已核实事实

11月6日亚洲股市遭遇数月来最大单日跌幅。韩国KOSPI指数暴跌8.6%至4,923点，触发20分钟临时停牌。日经225指数跌约6%，台湾加权指数跌逾8%。此前纳斯达克综合指数跌5.52%，费城半导体指数跌逾12%。

DBS posts S$2.81 billion Q3 profit as trading income cools, declares nine-month record

Released 5 Nov 2026; Q3 and nine-month 2026. DBS Group Holdings, Singapore.

Verified reporting

DBS Group, Singapore's largest bank by assets, reported third-quarter net profit of S$2.81 billion, down 5% year on year. Total income fell 1%. The decline was attributed to lower market trading income amid unfavourable equity market conditions. However, nine-month net profit reached a record S$8.82 billion, up 2% year on year, on total income of S$17.9 billion — also a new high. Fee income and treasury customer sales grew by double-digit percentages to new highs in the nine-month period, led by wealth management. DBS's share price declined at market open on 6 November, weighing on the broader Straits Times Index.

Our analysis

DBS's mixed quarter — weaker trading but record nine-month earnings — mirrors the tension across Asian banking: strong wealth and lending franchises are generating sustained income, but market-sensitive revenue streams are vulnerable to the kind of volatility seen in the AI-driven selloff. With all three Singapore banks now reporting, the sector picture is clear: wealth management is the growth engine, but NIM compression and volatile trading are headwinds. For ASEAN corporates, the banks' continued lending momentum suggests credit conditions remain supportive, even as equity markets wobble.

Unknowns & what comes next

Whether wealth management growth can offset trading headwinds in Q4 depends on market recovery. Watch DBS's full-year guidance update and dividend policy.

Verified reporting

UOB Q3 profit misses forecasts as NIM compresses; wealth income hits record

Released 4 Nov 2026; Q3 2026. United Overseas Bank, Singapore.

Verified reporting

UOB reported Q3 net profit of S$1.25 billion, missing the S$1.38 billion analyst consensus and falling 15% from the previous quarter. Net interest income was S$2.28 billion, down 1% quarter on quarter and up 1% year on year. Net interest margin declined to 1.68%, down 6 basis points from 1.74% in Q2. Net fee income hit a record S$733 million, up 10% quarter on quarter and 19% year on year, driven by wealth management and credit cards. Customer loans grew 3% quarter on quarter and 6% year on year to S$372 billion, with trade loans surging 31% year on year. The non-performing loan ratio improved to 1.5% from 1.6%. Nine-month net profit was S$4.17 billion, up 27% year on year. Credit costs were 24 basis points, within the 25–30 bps full-year guidance.

Our analysis

UOB's quarter reveals the NIM compression hitting Singapore banks as funding costs catch up with lending rates. The 31% trade loan surge, however, signals strong cross-border trade financing demand — particularly relevant given ASEAN's robust intra-regional trade growth. The record fee income shows the wealth management pivot is working. Greater China NPL ratio at 5.0% (up from 4.8%) bears watching as a potential source of provisions. The bank's mid-single-digit 2027 loan growth guidance suggests cautious optimism about the lending environment ahead.

Unknowns & what comes next

Whether NIM stabilises in Q4 depends on deposit repricing dynamics. Greater China property exposure (NPL at 3.8% for real estate and hospitality) remains a risk to watch. No dividend was declared in Q3.

Singapore Airlines unveils S$2 billion cabin overhaul, its largest ever

Announced 6 Nov 2026. Singapore Airlines.

Verified reporting

Singapore Airlines unveiled new seat products across all four cabin classes — its largest-ever investment at S$2 billion. The seats will go into retrofitted Airbus A350 aircraft and new Boeing 777-9s. First Class gets 1-2-1 suites with 80-inch beds and retractable centre dividers for double beds. Business Class features 1-2-1 seats with 79-inch flat beds and privacy doors. Premium Economy and Economy also receive new designs. Entry into service is expected H2 2027, with A350 retrofit completion targeted by end 2030. The investment underscores the airline's strategy of competing on premium product quality in Asia's growing long-haul market.

Our analysis

The investment signals SIA's bet on premium travel demand in Asia. However, the timeline carries execution risk: the Boeing 777-9 is certified but not yet in service, and the A350 seat certification is still pending. Competitors have been upgrading aggressively; whether SIA's new product arrives fast enough to maintain its premium positioning is the key question. The S$2 billion spend also comes at a time when the airline industry faces fuel-price uncertainty and potential headwinds from the global equity selloff dampening corporate travel budgets.

Unknowns & what comes next

Boeing 777-9 delivery timeline remains uncertain. Watch for fare and yield impacts once the new cabins enter service.
