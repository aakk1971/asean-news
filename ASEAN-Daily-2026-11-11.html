ASEAN Inclusive Growth Summit Opens in Manila with Mastercard, Google and OpenAI

11 November 2026. Mastercard Center for Inclusive Growth / Manila Bulletin / Philippine Star.

Verified reporting

The 2026 ASEAN Inclusive Growth Summit opened in Manila on 11 November, convening business leaders, government officials and civil society representatives under the theme "Resilient and Rising" ahead of the 49th ASEAN Summit scheduled for 15–17 November. The summit was organised by the Mastercard Center for Inclusive Growth in partnership with the ASEAN Business Advisory Council Philippines.

Featured speakers and attendees included Teresita Sy-Coson, Vice Chairperson of SM Investments Corporation; Jaime Augusto Zobel de Ayala, Chairman of Ayala Corporation; Nitin Gajria, Regional Managing Director of Google; Sandhya Devanathan, Regional Managing Director of OpenAI; Jane Sun, CEO of Trip.com Group; Kuok Hui Kwong, Chairman and CEO of Shangri-La Group; Jon Huntsman, Vice Chairman of Mastercard; and Joey Concepcion, Chairman of ASEAN-BAC Philippines. Actress and UNHCR Special Envoy Angelina Jolie and Filipino Broadway star Lea Salonga also addressed the gathering.

Agenda topics included digital transformation and AI in Southeast Asia, cybersecurity, financial health for micro, small and medium enterprises, sustainable tourism, the creator economy, and regional economic integration. Officials highlighted that ASEAN's 700 million people and 70 million MSMEs represent one of the world's largest untapped digital markets. The summit serves as a prelude to the main ASEAN Summit where the Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA) is expected to be signed — described as the world's first binding regional digital economy pact.

Our analysis

The corporate lineup at the Inclusive Growth Summit — spanning Mastercard, Google, OpenAI, Shangri-La, Trip.com and ASEAN's two largest conglomerates (SM and Ayala) — signals that ASEAN's positioning in the global technology governance conversation has moved beyond aspiration to active engagement. The presence of OpenAI's regional head alongside Google's is particularly notable: it suggests ASEAN is becoming a contested market for AI platform deployment, not merely a passive recipient of Silicon Valley products. The summit's timing, four days before the expected DEFA signing, is designed to build private-sector momentum behind the agreement's implementation. The challenge is translating summit declarations into on-the-ground impact for the 70 million MSMEs that account for over 90 per cent of ASEAN businesses — many of which still lack basic digital payment infrastructure, let alone AI capabilities. The Philippines' own MSME sector, while large, faces persistent barriers in access to finance and digital literacy that summit speeches alone cannot resolve.

What we don't know yet

No specific commercial agreements or investment commitments were announced at the summit's opening. It is unclear whether the corporate participants will make binding pledges related to DEFA implementation. The economic impact of the summit and the broader ASEAN week on Manila's economy — including the cost of flight cancellations and business disruptions — has not been estimated.

未知事项

峰会开幕时未宣布具体的商业协议或投资承诺。企业参与者是否会就DEFA实施作出具约束力的承诺尚不清楚。峰会及更广泛的东盟周对马尼拉经济的影响——包括航班取消和商业中断的成本——尚未估算。

IHSG Slips to 5,115 as Markets Brace for MSCI November Rebalancing Announcement

11 November 2026. CNBC Indonesia / Okezone / Bisnis Indonesia.

Verified reporting

The Jakarta Composite Index (IHSG) closed at 5,115 on 11 November, down 10.70 points or 0.21 per cent, as investors adopted a cautious stance ahead of MSCI's semi-annual index rebalancing announcement scheduled for Thursday, 12 November (Indonesian time). The index opened higher at 5,139.09 and touched an intraday high of 5,144.79 before selling pressure pushed it to a low of 5,086.02. Total market capitalisation stood at Rp8,785 trillion.

Of 945 stocks traded, 446 declined, 218 advanced, and 281 were unchanged. Basic materials led sectoral declines with a 1.86 per cent drop, followed by industrials at 0.93 per cent and consumer non-primer at 0.74 per cent. Only transportation (+0.91 per cent), energy (+0.50 per cent), and consumer primer (+0.25 per cent) closed in positive territory.

The previous session (10 November) had seen the IHSG rally 1.02 per cent to close at 5,125.73, with foreign investors recording net purchases of Rp171.89 billion. However, several major stocks continued to see foreign selling, led by Barito Pacific (BRPT, Rp51.05 billion net sell), Telkom Indonesia (TLKM, Rp47.50 billion), and Barito Renewables Energy (BREN, Rp41.19 billion). Analysts noted that the MSCI rebalancing could result in additional Indonesian stocks being removed from the MSCI Emerging Markets Index, following the index provider's ongoing concerns about market transparency and governance.

Our analysis

The IHSG's inability to hold gains above 5,125 despite a broad rally the previous day underscores the deep uncertainty weighing on Indonesia's equity market ahead of the MSCI announcement. The rebalancing event scheduled for 12 November is distinct from the broader reclassification consultation — this is a routine semi-annual index review that will determine which Indonesian stocks remain in or are added to MSCI's investable indices. However, the two processes are intertwined: any further removals of Indonesian constituents would reinforce the narrative that Indonesia's capital market is shrinking within global indices rather than growing. The pattern of foreign selling in Barito-linked stocks (BRPT, BREN) and blue chips (TLKM, BBRI) suggests institutional investors are pre-positioning for potential exclusions. With cumulative foreign net selling exceeding Rp98 trillion year-to-date and the IHSG down approximately 45 per cent from its January peak, Indonesia's market is operating under a level of sustained institutional pressure rarely seen in ASEAN emerging markets.

What we don't know yet

The specific additions and deletions in MSCI's November rebalancing will not be known until the announcement on 12 November. Whether the rebalancing results will trigger additional passive fund outflows beyond those already priced in is uncertain. The interaction between the routine rebalancing and the broader reclassification consultation timeline remains unclear — specifically, whether a harsh rebalancing outcome could accelerate the reclassification decision.

前一交易日（11月10日）IHSG曾大涨1.02%收于5,125.73，外资录得净买入1,718.9亿印尼盾。然而，多只主要股票持续遭遇外资抛售，领先的有Barito Pacific（BRPT，净卖510.5亿印尼盾）、印尼电信（TLKM，475.0亿）和Barito Renewables Energy（BREN，411.9亿）。分析师指出，MSCI再平衡可能导致更多印尼股票从MSCI新兴市场指数中被剔除，此前该指数提供商对市场透明度和治理持续表示关切。

原创分析

IHSG在前一交易日大幅反弹后仍无法守住5,125上方的涨幅，凸显了MSCI公告前笼罩印尼股市的深层不确定性。定于11月12日的再平衡事件不同于更广泛的重新分类磋商——这是例行的半年度指数审查，将决定哪些印尼股票留在或被纳入MSCI可投资指数。然而两个过程紧密交织：任何进一步剔除印尼成分股的行动都将强化印尼资本市场在全球指数中正在缩小而非扩大的叙事。外资持续抛售Barito系股票（BRPT、BREN）和蓝筹股（TLKM、BBRI）的模式表明机构投资者正在为潜在的剔除提前布局。年初至今外资累计净卖出超过98万亿印尼盾，IHSG较1月峰值下跌约45%，印尼市场正承受东盟新兴市场中罕见的持续机构抛压。

未知事项

MSCI11月再平衡的具体纳入和剔除结果要到11月12日公告后才能知晓。再平衡结果是否会引发超出市场已消化预期的额外被动基金流出尚不确定。例行再平衡与更广泛的重新分类磋商时间表之间的互动关系仍不清楚——具体来说，严厉的再平衡结果是否可能加速重新分类决定。

World Bank Calls for Decisive Reforms as Malaysia's Economy Stays Resilient

11 November 2026. World Bank / Malay Mail / The Star.

Verified reporting

The World Bank launched its October 2026 Malaysia Economic Monitor on 11 November, titled "From Quantity to Quality: Financing Malaysia's Productive SMEs," warning that the country must deepen structural reforms to sustain its trajectory toward high-income status. Zafer Mustafaoglu, the World Bank's division director for the Philippines, Malaysia and Brunei, said Malaysia's economy remained positive but faces significant external headwinds.

"As a highly open economy, Malaysia remains exposed, especially through trade and financial channels," Mustafaoglu said, citing geopolitical conflicts, trade tensions, and weaker global demand as key risks. He called on policymakers to rebuild fiscal space to strengthen resilience against future shocks and to keep monetary and macroprudential policy appropriately calibrated.

The report's central finding is that Malaysia's SME sector — critical to the country's high-income ambitions — must shift its financing from basic working capital toward higher-risk expansion capital. This includes funding for global market scaling, automation, digitalisation, AI adoption, and green transitions. "Deepening structural reforms is not optional, but is essential for sustainable growth and better jobs," Mustafaoglu stated. Separately, HSBC has upgraded Malaysia's 2026 GDP growth forecast to 5.5 per cent, while the OECD's latest economic survey highlighted the need for continued fiscal consolidation alongside investment-friendly reform.

Our analysis

The World Bank's framing of Malaysia's reform imperative as "not optional" carries particular weight given the country's current macro-economic performance. Malaysia is delivering 5.3 per cent GDP growth with 1.8 per cent inflation — numbers that could easily breed complacency. The report's focus on SME financing quality rather than quantity is a subtle but important shift: Malaysia's SMEs can generally access credit, but the available financing is overwhelmingly short-term working capital rather than the patient, risk-tolerant capital needed for technology adoption and export scaling. This matters because Malaysia's stated goal of reaching high-income status by 2028 depends on productivity gains that only come from innovation and automation — areas where SMEs, which employ over 60 per cent of the workforce, are lagging. The timing also coincides with the ASEAN summit's DEFA discussions, which will impose new digital economy compliance requirements that Malaysian SMEs will need capital to meet. The Bank's call to rebuild fiscal space is a diplomatic way of saying the RON95 fuel subsidy rationalisation cannot be deferred indefinitely.

What we don't know yet

The report's specific numerical forecasts for Malaysia's GDP growth have not been disclosed in the initial coverage. Whether the World Bank's SME financing recommendations will influence Malaysia's 2027 budget allocations is unclear. The impact of potential fuel subsidy restructuring on SME operating costs — and whether compensating support measures are planned — has not been addressed.

世界银行呼吁果断改革，马来西亚经济保持韧性

2026年11月11日。世界银行 / 马来邮报 / 星报。

经核实的报道

世界银行于11月11日发布2026年10月《马来西亚经济监测》报告，题为"从量到质：为马来西亚的生产性中小企业融资"，警告该国必须深化结构性改革以维持迈向高收入国家的轨迹。世界银行菲律宾、马来西亚和文莱部门主管扎费尔·穆斯塔法奥卢表示，马来西亚经济保持积极态势，但面临显著的外部逆风。

"作为高度开放的经济体，马来西亚仍然面临风险敞口，特别是通过贸易和金融渠道，"穆斯塔法奥卢表示，他提及地缘政治冲突、贸易紧张和全球需求减弱是主要风险。他呼吁决策者重建财政空间以增强抵御未来冲击的韧性，并保持货币和宏观审慎政策的适当校准。

报告的核心发现是，马来西亚中小企业部门——对该国高收入抱负至关重要——必须将融资从基本流动资金转向高风险扩张资本。这包括全球市场拓展、自动化、数字化、AI采用和绿色转型的资金需求。"深化结构性改革不是可选项，而是实现可持续增长和更好就业的必要条件，"穆斯塔法奥卢表示。另外，汇丰银行已将马来西亚2026年GDP增长预测上调至5.5%，而经合组织最新经济调查则强调需要在继续财政整合的同时推进有利于投资的改革。

原创分析

世界银行将马来西亚的改革紧迫性定性为"不是可选项"，在该国当前宏观经济表现的背景下尤具分量。马来西亚正在实现5.3%的GDP增长和1.8%的通胀——这些数字很容易滋生自满情绪。报告聚焦于中小企业融资质量而非数量，是一个微妙但重要的转变：马来西亚中小企业通常可以获得信贷，但可用融资绝大多数是短期流动资金，而非技术采用和出口拓展所需的耐心、高风险容忍度的资本。这一点很重要，因为马来西亚在2028年前达到高收入国家地位的目标依赖于创新和自动化带来的生产率提升——而雇佣了60%以上劳动力的中小企业在这些领域正在落后。发布时间恰逢东盟峰会DEFA讨论，这将对马来西亚中小企业提出新的数字经济合规要求，需要资本来应对。世界银行呼吁重建财政空间是一种外交辞令，意思是RON95燃油补贴合理化不能无限期推迟。

未知事项

报告对马来西亚GDP增长的具体数字预测尚未在初步报道中披露。世界银行的中小企业融资建议是否会影响马来西亚2027年预算分配尚不清楚。潜在的燃油补贴重组对中小企业运营成本的影响——以及是否规划了补偿性支持措施——也未得到解答。

Vietnam's GDP Surges 9.95% in Q3 2026 — Fastest Pace Since 2022

October 2026. General Statistics Office of Vietnam / VietnamNet / Trading Economics.

Verified reporting

Vietnam's economy grew an estimated 9.95 per cent year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026, accelerating from 8.81 per cent in Q2 and 8.15 per cent in Q1, according to the General Statistics Office (GSO). This marks the fastest quarterly growth rate since 2022 and brings the nine-month average to 9.01 per cent — placing Vietnam firmly on track to be the fastest-growing major economy in ASEAN.

Industry and construction led the expansion with 12.50 per cent growth, contributing 51.57 per cent of total gross value added growth. Services expanded 9.54 per cent (43.29 per cent contribution), while agriculture, forestry and fisheries grew 4.21 per cent (5.14 per cent contribution). On the demand side, exports of goods and services surged 23.27 per cent, gross capital formation rose 21.39 per cent, and final consumption grew 8.96 per cent.

GSO Director Nguyen Thi Huong credited strong government direction and said ministries and localities are working to achieve double-digit growth for the full year 2026. Twelve of thirty-four provinces and cities recorded growth of at least 10 per cent in the first nine months, led by Quang Ninh (12.54 per cent), Ha Tinh (12.36 per cent), and Hai Phong (12.08 per cent). UOB has separately raised its full-year 2026 forecast for Vietnam to 8.5 per cent.

Our analysis

Vietnam's 9.95 per cent Q3 growth rate places it in rarefied territory — not just within ASEAN, where it is growing at roughly four times Thailand's rate, but globally, where only India among large economies is posting comparable expansion. The acceleration from 8.15 per cent in Q1 to 9.95 per cent in Q3 is driven by the manufacturing export cycle reaching full capacity: the 23.27 per cent surge in goods and services exports reflects factories that were ramping up component imports earlier in the year now shipping finished products. The geographic concentration of growth in northern industrial provinces — Quang Ninh, Hai Phong, Bac Ninh — maps directly onto the electronics and precision manufacturing corridor that has attracted Samsung, LG, and their supplier networks. The government's double-digit growth target for 2026 is ambitious but not unrealistic: achieving it would require Q4 growth above 12 per cent, which is possible if export momentum holds through the holiday ordering season. The risk is that the same concentration that produces spectacular headline growth also leaves southern and agricultural provinces behind — the gap between Quang Ninh at 12.54 per cent and Son La at 5.59 per cent illustrates a development divide that rapid national growth can mask but not resolve.

What we don't know yet

Whether Q4 growth can accelerate sufficiently to achieve the government's double-digit annual target has not been formally assessed. The impact of the Section 301 duties imposed in July on export volumes in the second half has not been separately quantified. The sustainability of the 21.39 per cent investment growth rate — and whether it reflects one-off mega-projects or a broad-based investment cycle — requires further data.

越南第三季度GDP飙升9.95%——2022年以来最快增速

2026年10月。越南统计总局 / 越南网 / Trading Economics。

经核实的报道

据统计总局数据，越南经济2026年第三季度同比增长预计达9.95%，从第二季度的8.81%和第一季度的8.15%进一步加速。这是2022年以来最快的季度增长率，使前九个月平均增长达到9.01%——牢牢确立越南作为东盟增速最快的主要经济体的地位。

工业和建筑业以12.50%的增速领跑，贡献了总增加值增长的51.57%。服务业扩张9.54%（贡献43.29%），农林渔业增长4.21%（贡献5.14%）。需求侧方面，货物和服务出口飙升23.27%，资本形成总额增长21.39%，最终消费增长8.96%。

原创分析

Our analysis

The 74 per cent surge in registered FDI tells a compelling story about Vietnam's positioning in global supply chain reconfiguration. The most important figure, however, is the US$24.08 billion in disbursed FDI — up 13.1 per cent — because it measures what investors are actually spending on the ground. The gap between registered (US$54.85 billion) and disbursed (US$24.08 billion) FDI reflects the reality that many registered projects take years to deploy capital fully, and some never do. Vietnam's challenge is converting the historically high registration pipeline into actual factories, jobs, and technology transfer — and doing so in higher-value segments rather than remaining an assembly platform.

What we don't know yet

The sectoral and geographic breakdown of disbursed FDI has not been fully released for the ten-month period. Whether the Luxembourg-sourced investments represent genuine new productive capacity or financial restructuring of existing investments is unclear. The impact of Vietnam's global minimum tax implementation on future FDI registration trends — particularly from jurisdictions that previously benefited from Vietnam's tax incentives — has not been assessed.

ASEAN Set to Sign Landmark Digital Economy Framework Agreement at Manila Summit

November 2026. ASEAN Secretariat / Rajah & Tann Asia / ASEAN Briefing.

Verified reporting

The ten ASEAN member states are expected to sign the Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA) during the 49th ASEAN Summit in Manila on 15–17 November 2026, marking the conclusion of negotiations that were formally completed in October 2026. DEFA is described as the world's first binding regional digital economy agreement, covering cross-border data flows, digital payments, e-commerce facilitation, cybersecurity cooperation, and AI governance for a market of approximately 670 million people.

The agreement was a signature priority of the Philippines' 2026 ASEAN chairship. Rajah & Tann Asia noted that negotiations were concluded ahead of the November target, while ANTARA News Agency reported that ASEAN economic ministers had targeted the signing for the November summit. The Philippine News Agency described DEFA as expected to "boost ease of doing business" across the region by harmonising digital trade rules that currently vary significantly among member states.

DEFA's scope extends beyond traditional trade agreements to include provisions on digital identity, consumer protection in e-commerce, competition policy for digital platforms, and a framework for emerging technologies including artificial intelligence. The agreement will require ratification by individual member states before its provisions take full legal effect, a process that typically takes twelve to twenty-four months across ASEAN.

Our analysis

DEFA's expected signing would be a genuine milestone for ASEAN — no other regional bloc has achieved a binding digital economy framework of this scope. The European Union's Digital Markets Act and Digital Services Act regulate digital platforms but are single-jurisdiction instruments; DEFA attempts to harmonise rules across ten sovereign nations with vastly different levels of digital development. This ambition is also its greatest challenge: Singapore's digital economy (with near-universal smartphone penetration and a sophisticated fintech sector) operates at a fundamentally different level from Myanmar's or Cambodia's. The agreement's provisions on cross-border data flows will be particularly contentious in implementation, as several ASEAN members — notably Vietnam and Indonesia — maintain strict data localisation requirements that conflict with the free-flow principles DEFA espouses. The ratification timeline of twelve to twenty-four months means the earliest substantive impact would be felt in 2028, by which time the AI governance provisions may already require updating given the pace of technology change. The real test will be whether DEFA creates a genuinely integrated digital single market or becomes another aspirational framework — like the ASEAN Economic Community blueprint — that sets targets without mechanisms to enforce them.

What we don't know yet

The exact signing date within the 15–17 November summit period has not been publicly confirmed. Whether all ten member states will sign simultaneously or whether some will reserve positions on specific provisions is unclear. The enforcement mechanisms for DEFA compliance — and whether they include dispute resolution or sanctions for non-compliance — have not been publicly detailed. The agreement's interaction with existing bilateral digital economy agreements (such as the Singapore-Australia Digital Economy Agreement) has not been clarified.

Notable removals include DCI Indonesia (DCII, market cap approximately Rp417 trillion), controlled by Otto Toto Sugiri; Bayan Resources (BYAN, approximately Rp305 trillion), controlled by Low Tuck Kwong; Barito Renewables Energy (BREN, approximately Rp240 trillion), affiliated with Prajogo Pangestu; Dian Swastatika Sentosa (DSSA); and several Sinar Mas-linked issuers including SMAR, GEMS, and DUTI.

Our analysis

The removal of 82 HSC stocks is the most aggressive single-day compositional reform in the IHSG's history and represents Indonesia's most tangible response to MSCI's governance concerns.
