Malaysia's Trade Breaches RM400 Billion for First Time as October Exports Surge 61.1% Year-on-Year

25–26 November 2026. Department of Statistics Malaysia / Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry / Bernama / The Edge.

Verified reporting

Malaysia's total merchandise trade reached RM436.61 billion in October 2026, the first time monthly trade has exceeded RM400 billion, representing a 58 per cent increase year-on-year, according to data released by the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) on 25 November. Exports surged 61.1 per cent to RM239.20 billion, while imports rose 54.4 per cent to RM197.41 billion, producing a trade surplus of RM41.80 billion.

The October export performance was driven overwhelmingly by the electrical and electronic (E&E) sector. E&E exports rose RM58.51 billion in absolute terms compared to October 2025, reflecting sustained global demand for semiconductors, AI-related chips, and data centre equipment. This single category accounted for the majority of the total export increase, underscoring Malaysia's deepening integration into the global AI supply chain.

Year-to-date trade for January to October 2026 reached RM3.348 trillion, up 33.2 per cent from the same period in 2025. The cumulative trade surplus stood at RM274.81 billion, more than double the RM134.5 billion surplus recorded in the corresponding period of 2025. The sustained surplus expansion reflects the structural shift in Malaysia's export mix toward higher-value electronics and semiconductor products.

By destination, China remained Malaysia's largest trading partner, followed by Singapore, the United States, Japan, and Thailand. ASEAN collectively accounted for approximately 27 per cent of total trade. The Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) attributed the record performance to Malaysia's strategic positioning in the semiconductor value chain and the ongoing wave of data centre investment approvals across the country.

Our analysis

Malaysia's October trade figures are extraordinary by any historical standard — RM436.61 billion in a single month represents a step change in the country's trade profile that would have been difficult to imagine even two years ago. The 61.1 per cent surge in exports is not a statistical anomaly driven by base effects; the October 2025 base was itself a reasonably strong month, which makes the year-on-year jump genuinely remarkable. The E&E sector's RM58.51 billion increase in absolute terms is the story within the story. Malaysia has long been a significant player in semiconductor back-end operations — testing, packaging, and assembly — but the AI boom has elevated the country's role in the supply chain from important to critical. The global shortage of advanced packaging capacity, combined with Malaysia's cost advantages over Singapore and Taiwan for certain process nodes, has created a structural demand tailwind that is showing no signs of easing. The year-to-date trade surplus of RM274.81 billion — more than doubling the same period last year — has significant macroeconomic implications. A surplus of this magnitude provides a natural cushion for the ringgit, helps fund the current account, and reduces Malaysia's vulnerability to capital flow reversals. It also gives Bank Negara Malaysia additional policy flexibility, as the external position supports currency stability even if rate differentials with the US remain wide. The comparison with the previous day's story on Malaysia's Q3 GDP growth of 5.5 per cent is instructive: the trade data confirms that the GDP figures, far from being inflated, may actually understate the momentum in the external sector. Manufacturing growth of 7.7 per cent in Q3 was impressive, but October's export surge suggests Q4 manufacturing output could accelerate further. The risk, as with Singapore, is concentration. When E&E exports account for the majority of the trade surplus expansion, the economy becomes a leveraged bet on the continuation of the AI investment cycle. A correction in global semiconductor demand — whether from overbuilding of data centre capacity, inventory correction, or a reassessment of AI returns — would hit Malaysia's trade position directly and swiftly. The doubling of the trade surplus also creates potential friction with trading partners, particularly the United States, where large bilateral surpluses with Asian exporters have historically attracted political scrutiny.

What we don't know yet

Whether October's RM436.61 billion trade figure represents a new plateau or a one-off peak driven by seasonal front-loading of orders ahead of year-end. The breakdown of E&E exports by sub-segment — specifically how much is semiconductor components versus finished data centre equipment versus consumer electronics. Whether the 117 approved data centre projects in Malaysia are generating additional import demand for construction materials and equipment that inflates the import figure. How much of the export surge represents genuine final demand versus inventory building by downstream customers anticipating supply disruptions. Whether the United States will increase scrutiny of Malaysia's growing trade surplus as part of broader trade policy toward ASEAN. The sustainability of the RM41.80 billion monthly surplus and its implications for ringgit appreciation pressure, which could reduce export competitiveness over time.

马来西亚贸易额首次突破4000亿令吉，10月出口同比飙升61.1%

2026年11月25–26日。马来西亚统计局 / 投资、贸易及工业部 / 马新社 / The Edge。

经核实的报道

根据马来西亚统计局（DOSM）11月25日发布的数据，马来西亚2026年10月商品贸易总额达4,366.1亿令吉，月度贸易额首次突破4,000亿令吉大关，同比增长58%。出口飙升61.1%至2,392亿令吉，进口增长54.4%至1,974.1亿令吉，贸易顺差418亿令吉。

10月出口表现主要由电子电气（E&E）行业驱动。E&E出口较2025年10月绝对值增加585.1亿令吉，反映全球对半导体、AI相关芯片和数据中心设备的持续需求。仅此单一类别就占出口总增幅的大部分，凸显马来西亚在全球AI供应链中日益深化的整合。

2026年1月至10月累计贸易额达3.348万亿令吉，同比增长33.2%。累计贸易顺差为2,748.1亿令吉，是2025年同期1,345亿令吉的两倍多。持续的顺差扩大反映了马来西亚出口结构向更高价值电子和半导体产品的结构性转变。

按目的地划分，中国仍是马来西亚最大贸易伙伴，其次是新加坡、美国、日本和泰国。东盟整体约占总贸易额的27%。投资、贸易及工业部（MITI）将创纪录表现归因于马来西亚在半导体价值链中的战略定位以及全国范围内持续的数据中心投资审批浪潮。

原创分析

以任何历史标准衡量，马来西亚10月贸易数据都非同凡响——单月4,366.1亿令吉代表着该国贸易格局的阶梯式跃升，即使两年前也难以想象。出口61.1%的飙升并非基数效应造成的统计异常；2025年10月的基数本身就相当强劲，这使得同比跃升真正引人注目。E&E行业绝对值585.1亿令吉的增长是故事中的故事。马来西亚长期以来在半导体后端运营（测试、封装和组装）中扮演重要角色，但AI热潮已将该国在供应链中的角色从重要提升至关键。全球先进封装产能的短缺，加上马来西亚在某些工艺节点上相对新加坡和台湾的成本优势，创造了一个没有减弱迹象的结构性需求顺风。年初至今2,748.1亿令吉的贸易顺差——比去年同期翻一倍多——具有重大宏观经济影响。如此规模的顺差为令吉提供了天然缓冲，有助于经常账户融资，并降低马来西亚对资本流动逆转的脆弱性。这也为国家银行提供了额外的政策灵活性，因为外部头寸支撑汇率稳定，即使与美国的利差仍然较大。与前一天马来西亚第三季度GDP增长5.5%的报道相对照颇有启示：贸易数据确认GDP数据非但没有夸大，实际上可能低估了外部部门的动能。第三季度制造业7.7%的增长已令人印象深刻，但10月出口飙升表明第四季度制造业产出可能进一步加速。与新加坡一样，风险在于集中度。当E&E出口占贸易顺差扩大的大部分时，经济就成为AI投资周期持续的杠杆赌注。全球半导体需求的修正——无论是数据中心产能过剩、库存调整还是对AI回报的重新评估——都将直接而迅速地冲击马来西亚的贸易地位。贸易顺差翻倍也可能与贸易伙伴产生摩擦，特别是美国，大额双边顺差历来吸引政治审视。

未知事项

10月4,366.1亿令吉的贸易额代表新的台阶还是年底前季节性订单提前的一次性高峰。E&E出口的细分——具体有多少是半导体组件、成品数据中心设备还是消费电子产品。马来西亚117个已批准的数据中心项目是否正在产生额外的建筑材料和设备进口需求从而推高进口数据。出口飙升中有多少代表真正的最终需求而非下游客户预期供应中断的库存积累。美国是否会加大对马来西亚不断增长的贸易顺差的审视，作为对东盟更广泛贸易政策的一部分。418亿令吉月度顺差的可持续性及其对令吉升值压力的影响，这可能随时间推移削弱出口竞争力。

Source: DOSM / MITI / Bernama / The Edge

Vietnam FDI Commitments Hit 17-Year High of US$54.85 Billion as Singapore Leads Capital Inflows

25–26 November 2026. Ministry of Planning and Investment / Foreign Investment Agency / VnExpress / Vietnam Investment Review.

Verified reporting

Foreign direct investment commitments in Vietnam reached US$54.85 billion for the first eleven months of 2026, the highest level in seventeen years and a 74 per cent increase year-on-year, according to data from the Ministry of Planning and Investment's Foreign Investment Agency (FIA) released on 25 November. The figure encompasses newly registered capital, additional capital from existing projects, and capital contributions through share purchases.

Disbursed FDI — the amount actually deployed into the economy — reached US$24.08 billion over the same period, up 13.1 per cent year-on-year. The gap between committed and disbursed FDI widened significantly, with the disbursement ratio falling to approximately 44 per cent compared to roughly 65 per cent in the same period of 2025, reflecting the front-loaded nature of several mega-projects that will disburse over multiple years.

Singapore was the largest source of FDI commitments at US$9.52 billion, followed by South Korea at US$5.81 billion and Luxembourg at US$4.99 billion. Luxembourg's unusually high ranking reflects large-scale channelling of European investment through the Grand Duchy's holding company structures. Manufacturing and processing dominated the sectoral breakdown, accounting for 83 per cent of disbursed FDI and reflecting Vietnam's continued role as a preferred destination for supply chain diversification away from China.

Major projects driving the headline figure include expansions by Samsung, LG, and other electronics manufacturers in the northern provinces, as well as new investments in renewable energy, data centres, and industrial park infrastructure. The northern provinces of Bac Ninh, Hai Phong, and Quang Ninh collectively attracted a disproportionate share of commitments, reinforcing the concentration of FDI in Vietnam's established industrial corridors.

Our analysis

Vietnam's US$54.85 billion in FDI commitments is a headline-grabbing figure that confirms the country's status as one of the world's most attractive destinations for manufacturing investment — but the more meaningful number is the US$24.08 billion in actual disbursements, which represents the capital that has entered the economy and is generating productive activity. The widening gap between commitments and disbursements — the ratio falling from roughly 65 per cent to 44 per cent — is a pattern that Vietnam has experienced before during investment surges, and it typically takes three to five years for committed capital to fully deploy. This matters because it means the GDP impact of today's headline commitments will be spread over the next several years, providing a multi-year growth tailwind but also creating execution risk if global conditions deteriorate before projects reach completion. Singapore's position as the largest FDI source at US$9.52 billion deserves careful interpretation. A significant portion of Singapore-origin FDI represents capital from third countries — including China, Japan, and Western multinationals — that is routed through Singapore-domiciled holding companies and regional headquarters. Singapore's role as ASEAN's financial hub means that its FDI figures into Vietnam overstate the bilateral economic relationship and understate the true diversity of Vietnam's investment sources. Luxembourg's US$4.99 billion similarly reflects European holding company structures rather than Luxembourg-based manufacturing operations. The 83 per cent concentration of disbursed FDI in manufacturing and processing is both a strength and a vulnerability. It confirms that Vietnam is successfully capturing China-plus-one supply chain diversification — the structural megatrend that has been driving FDI into Vietnam since the US-China trade war began in 2018 — but it also means the economy remains dependent on a single type of investment that is sensitive to global trade policy, tariff regimes, and manufacturing cost competitiveness. The connection to Vietnam's 10 per cent GDP growth target for 2027 is direct: the National Assembly's investment plan assumes US$30 to 32 billion in FDI disbursements next year, which would require a 25 to 33 per cent acceleration from the current eleven-month run rate. This is achievable if the mega-projects in the pipeline begin disbursing at scale, but it leaves little margin for a slowdown in new commitments or project delays.

What we don't know yet

Whether the 74 per cent surge in commitments represents a sustainable shift in global investment patterns or reflects one-off mega-projects that inflate the headline. The composition of Luxembourg-origin FDI — which European corporates are channelling investment through Luxembourg structures and what sectors they are targeting. Whether Vietnam's infrastructure — particularly power supply, logistics networks, and skilled labour availability — can absorb the investment pipeline without bottlenecks that delay disbursements. The impact of potential US tariff changes on Vietnam's attractiveness as a manufacturing destination, given that Vietnam's trade surplus with the United States has drawn political attention. Whether the disbursement ratio will recover toward historical norms or whether the structural shift to larger, longer-horizon projects will permanently lower the ratio. How the FDI pipeline interacts with Vietnam's domestic investment targets — specifically whether foreign and domestic investment are complementary or whether FDI crowds out local enterprises in key sectors.

越南外商直接投资承诺达548.5亿美元创17年新高，新加坡领跑资本流入

2026年11月25–26日。计划投资部 / 外国投资局 / VnExpress / 越南投资评论。

经核实的报道

根据计划投资部外国投资局（FIA）11月25日发布的数据，越南2026年前11个月外商直接投资承诺达548.5亿美元，为17年来最高水平，同比增长74%。该数据涵盖新注册资本、现有项目增资以及通过股权收购的出资。

实际到位FDI——真正投入经济的金额——同期达240.8亿美元，同比增长13.1%。承诺与到位FDI之间的差距显著扩大，到位比率降至约44%，而2025年同期约为65%，反映了若干大型项目将在多年内到位的前置特征。

新加坡是最大的FDI承诺来源国，达95.2亿美元，其次是韩国58.1亿美元和卢森堡49.9亿美元。卢森堡异常高的排名反映了欧洲投资通过大公国控股公司结构的大规模输送。制造和加工业在行业分布中占主导地位，占到位FDI的83%，反映越南作为从中国分散供应链首选目的地的持续角色。

推动总数的主要项目包括三星、LG和其他电子制造商在北部省份的扩张，以及可再生能源、数据中心和工业园区基础设施的新投资。北部省份北宁、海防和广宁共同吸引了不成比例的承诺份额，强化了FDI集中在越南成熟工业走廊的格局。

原创分析

越南548.5亿美元的FDI承诺是一个引人注目的数字，确认了该国作为全球最具吸引力制造业投资目的地之一的地位——但更有意义的数字是240.8亿美元的实际到位金额，这代表已进入经济并产生生产活动的资本。承诺与到位之间差距的扩大——比率从约65%降至44%——是越南在投资激增期间经历过的模式，承诺资本通常需要三到五年才能完全部署。这很重要，因为这意味着今天承诺总额的GDP影响将分散在未来几年，提供多年增长顺风但也产生执行风险——如果全球条件在项目完成前恶化。新加坡作为最大FDI来源国（95.2亿美元）值得审慎解读。新加坡来源的FDI中有相当部分代表来自第三国的资本——包括中国、日本和西方跨国公司——通过新加坡注册的控股公司和区域总部转移。新加坡作为东盟金融中心的角色意味着其对越南的FDI数据高估了双边经济关系，低估了越南投资来源的真正多样性。卢森堡的49.9亿美元同样反映的是欧洲控股公司结构而非卢森堡制造业务。到位FDI中83%集中在制造加工业既是优势也是脆弱性。它确认越南正在成功捕获中国加一供应链分散化——自2018年中美贸易战以来推动FDI流入越南的结构性大趋势——但也意味着经济仍依赖于对全球贸易政策、关税制度和制造成本竞争力敏感的单一投资类型。与越南2027年10%GDP增长目标的联系是直接的：国会投资计划假设明年FDI到位300亿至320亿美元，这需要在当前11个月运行率基础上加速25%至33%。如果在管线中的大型项目开始大规模到位，这是可以实现的，但几乎没有留下新承诺放缓或项目延迟的余地。

未知事项

承诺74%的飙升代表全球投资格局的可持续转变还是反映了膨胀标题数据的一次性大型项目。卢森堡来源FDI的构成——哪些欧洲企业通过卢森堡结构输送投资以及它们瞄准哪些行业。越南的基础设施——特别是电力供应、物流网络和熟练劳动力供应——能否在不产生瓶颈延迟到位的情况下吸收投资管线。美国潜在关税变化对越南作为制造业目的地吸引力的影响，鉴于越南对美贸易顺差已引起政治关注。到位比率是否会恢复至历史常态，还是向更大、更长期项目的结构性转变将永久降低该比率。FDI管线如何与越南国内投资目标互动——具体而言外国投资和国内投资是互补的还是FDI在关键行业挤出了本地企业。

PHILIPPINES · MONETARY POLICY / 菲律宾 · 货币政策

BSP Governor Remolona Calls for Fiscal Stimulus as Inflation Hits 7.8% and Q3 Growth Slows to 2.1%

24–26 November 2026. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas / Philippine Statistics Authority / Bloomberg / BusinessWorld / Philippine Star.

Verified reporting

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Governor Eli Remolona Jr. publicly called for fiscal policy support on 24 November, stating that monetary policy alone cannot address the Philippines' simultaneous challenges of high inflation and slowing growth. Speaking at a business forum in Manila, Remolona said the government should consider targeted fiscal stimulus to support sectors most affected by the economic slowdown while the central bank continues its inflation-fighting mandate.

The call comes as the Philippines navigates worsening stagflation. October 2026 headline CPI reached 7.8 per cent year-on-year, up from 7.2 per cent in September, with core inflation — excluding volatile food and energy — accelerating to 5.4 per cent from 4.8 per cent. Rice inflation remained elevated at 20.7 per cent year-on-year. Several analysts, including Barclays, have forecast that November inflation could breach 8 per cent.

Third-quarter GDP growth decelerated to 2.1 per cent year-on-year, the weakest quarterly result since the pandemic. The BSP has raised its benchmark rate by a cumulative 125 basis points in 2026, bringing the overnight reverse repurchase rate to 5.50 per cent, but the real policy rate remains negative at approximately minus 2.3 per cent when adjusted for headline inflation.

Governor Remolona noted that the BSP faces a "classic policy dilemma" where further rate hikes risk deepening the growth slowdown, while holding or cutting rates risks allowing inflation expectations to become unanchored. He emphasised that fiscal measures — particularly targeted subsidies for rice and energy, infrastructure spending acceleration, and tax relief for lower-income households — could address supply-side inflation pressures that monetary policy is structurally unable to resolve. The Philippine Stock Exchange Index has fallen 28 per cent year-to-date, with net foreign selling of PHP 123 billion and forward valuations at approximately 9.5 times earnings — the lowest in over a decade.

Our analysis

Governor Remolona's public call for fiscal stimulus is a remarkable break from the typical central bank playbook, where governors are careful to avoid prescribing fiscal policy to their governments. That he felt compelled to do so publicly — rather than through private channels — signals the depth of the BSP's concern about the macroeconomic trajectory and suggests that private entreaties may not have produced the desired response. The underlying logic is sound: the Philippines' inflation problem is primarily supply-driven, with rice (20.7 per cent inflation), fuel, and electricity accounting for the bulk of headline price pressures. Rate hikes address demand-side inflation by making borrowing more expensive and cooling economic activity, but they cannot increase rice production, repair typhoon-damaged supply chains, or bring down imported energy costs. When applied to supply-side inflation, rate hikes are a blunt instrument that suppresses growth without resolving the price pressures — which is precisely the stagflation trap the Philippines now finds itself in. The fiscal measures Remolona proposed — targeted rice subsidies, energy support, infrastructure acceleration, and tax relief — are all supply-side interventions that could, in theory, ease inflationary pressures without requiring further monetary tightening. Rice subsidies or tariff reductions would directly lower the cost of the staple food; energy support would reduce utility bills; infrastructure spending could alleviate logistics bottlenecks that contribute to food price inflation; and tax relief would increase disposable income without stoking demand-pull inflation. The challenge is fiscal space. The Philippine fiscal deficit stood at 6.1 per cent of GDP through nine months of 2026, and the government's debt-to-GDP ratio has risen since the pandemic. Implementing meaningful fiscal stimulus on top of an already elevated deficit would require either reallocation from other spending priorities or acceptance of a wider deficit, which could trigger credit rating agency concerns. The Philippines' BBB+ investment-grade rating provides some buffer, but sustained stagflation combined with fiscal expansion could push the outlook to negative. The comparison with ASEAN peers is now stark: Malaysia is growing at 5.5 per cent with 1.9 per cent inflation; Singapore at 6 per cent with sub-3 per cent inflation; even Thailand, with its structural challenges, has inflation under 2.5 per cent. The Philippines is the clear outlier, and Remolona's public intervention reflects an institutional recognition that the current policy trajectory is insufficient.

What we don't know yet

Whether the fiscal authorities will respond to Remolona's call with concrete measures, and what the timeline for implementation would be. The specific rice import liberalisation or tariff reduction proposals under consideration, and whether the domestic rice farming lobby will block supply-side reforms. Whether the BSP will proceed with a 25 or 50-basis-point hike at the 17 December meeting, and how the fiscal policy debate affects the Monetary Board's deliberations. The November inflation figure — if it breaches 8 per cent as Barclays forecasts, whether this accelerates the fiscal policy response or triggers additional monetary tightening. Danantara Deploys Rp162 Trillion Across Energy, Food and Hajj as Indonesia's Sovereign Fund Takes Shape

22–26 November 2026. Daya Anagata Nusantara (Danantara) / Ministry of State-Owned Enterprises / Bisnis Indonesia / Jakarta Globe / Reuters.

Verified reporting

Indonesia's sovereign wealth fund Daya Anagata Nusantara (Danantara) has deployed approximately Rp162 trillion (US$8.85 billion) across three strategic sectors since its formal establishment in early 2026, according to disclosures compiled from official filings and media reports through 25 November. The fund, which manages assets transferred from state-owned enterprises, is positioning itself as the vehicle for Indonesia's long-term strategic investments under President Prabowo Subianto's economic programme.

In the energy sector, Danantara has committed approximately Rp10 trillion to waste-to-energy (WtE) infrastructure, funding the construction of waste processing facilities across major Indonesian cities as part of the government's push to address urban waste management and generate baseload electricity. The WtE projects are structured as public-private partnerships with Danantara providing the anchor equity investment.

In the food sector, Danantara acquired a 25 per cent stake in JBS S.A., the Brazilian meat processing giant, as part of a strategic push to secure protein supply chains for Indonesia's 280 million population. The JBS investment represents Danantara's most prominent international transaction and signals the fund's willingness to make large-scale overseas acquisitions in strategically important sectors. The transaction value has not been officially disclosed but is estimated at several billion US dollars based on JBS's market capitalisation.

In the hajj sector, Danantara has invested in the acquisition of 1,461 hotel rooms in Makkah, Saudi Arabia, to provide accommodation for Indonesian pilgrims performing the annual hajj. Indonesia sends the largest national contingent to the hajj each year — approximately 220,000 to 240,000 pilgrims — and the hotel acquisition aims to reduce accommodation costs and improve service quality for future pilgrim cohorts. The fund has set a timeline of 2028 to 2029 to establish itself as a world-class sovereign wealth fund benchmarked against peers such as Singapore's GIC and Temasek.

Our analysis

Danantara's deployment of Rp162 trillion represents the most consequential institutional development in Indonesian state capitalism since the consolidation of the state-owned enterprise sector under a single ministry. The fund's investment choices across energy, food, and hajj reveal a strategic logic that is distinctly Indonesian — shaped by the country's specific development challenges rather than following the template of other Asian sovereign wealth funds. The JBS stake is the most globally significant transaction. By acquiring 25 per cent of the world's largest meat processor, Danantara is pursuing food security through vertical integration of the supply chain — a strategy that echoes China's approach through COFCO and other state-backed entities but applied to protein rather than grains. For Indonesia, where rising middle-class incomes are driving protein demand growth of approximately 5 to 7 per cent annually, securing a position in one of the world's dominant meat processing networks is a hedge against future supply disruptions and price volatility. The WtE investment addresses one of Indonesia's most pressing infrastructure gaps: the country generates approximately 68 million tonnes of solid waste annually, of which less than half is properly managed. Converting waste to energy serves dual purposes — reducing the environmental burden of unmanaged waste while generating electricity for a grid that needs approximately 5,000 to 7,000 MW of additional capacity annually to meet demand growth. The Rp10 trillion committed is likely a first tranche, with total WtE investment requirements estimated at several times that amount. The hajj hotel acquisition is the most culturally specific investment in any sovereign wealth fund's portfolio globally. No other SWF invests in pilgrim accommodation as a strategic asset class. For Indonesia, however, the logic is straightforward: the hajj is both a religious obligation and a significant fiscal expenditure, with the government subsidising pilgrim costs through the Hajj Fund (BPKH). Owning accommodation in Makkah directly reduces the per-pilgrim cost and gives Indonesia greater control over the quality and logistics of the pilgrimage experience. The 2028-2029 timeline to achieve world-class SWF status is ambitious but not unrealistic if the fund can demonstrate professional governance, transparent reporting, and investment returns comparable to its benchmarks. The key risk is political interference — the history of Indonesian state enterprises is littered with examples of commercial decisions being overridden by political considerations, and Danantara will need to establish credible institutional independence to attract co-investment and maintain international credibility.

What we don't know yet

The total asset base under Danantara's management and the composition of assets transferred from state-owned enterprises. The acquisition price and financing structure for the JBS stake, and whether Danantara used external leverage or deployed entirely from its asset base. The return expectations and performance benchmarks the fund has set, and how these compare with GIC and Temasek as stated benchmarks. Whether the WtE projects will be commercially viable at scale or will require ongoing government subsidies, and the technology partners involved. The governance structure — specifically the degree of operational independence from the Ministry of State-Owned Enterprises and the presidential office. Whether international institutional investors — pension funds, endowments, other sovereign wealth funds — will co-invest alongside Danantara, which would be the strongest external validation of its governance and investment process.

Singapore Records All-Time High of 238 Million-Dollar HDB Resale Flats in October as Market Defies Cooling Measures

24–26 November 2026. Housing and Development Board / data.gov.sg / Straits Times / PropertyGuru / Business Times.

Verified reporting

A record 238 Housing and Development Board (HDB) resale flats changed hands at S$1 million or above in October 2026, surpassing the previous monthly record of 209 set in September 2026, according to HDB resale data published on data.gov.sg. The October figure represents a 13.9 per cent increase from September and extends an unbroken streak of monthly records for million-dollar HDB transactions.

The highest transacted price in October was S$1,818,000 for a unit at Skyparc @ Dawson, a Design, Build and Sell Scheme (DBSS) development in Queenstown. DBSS flats — which were built by private developers under a discontinued government programme — have consistently commanded the highest resale prices due to their premium finishes and central locations. Other notable transactions included units in Pinnacle@Duxton, City View@Boon Keng, and mature estate flats in Toa Payoh and Bishan.

Total HDB resale volume reached 2,178 flats in October, up 61.7 per cent year-on-year, reflecting both strong demand and an increase in the supply of flats reaching their Minimum Occupation Period (MOP). The overall HDB resale price index continued its upward trend, though the pace of appreciation has moderated compared to the sharp increases seen in 2023 and 2024.

The surge in million-dollar transactions has occurred despite a series of cooling measures implemented by the government, including higher Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD) rates introduced in 2023, tighter loan-to-value limits, and a 15-month wait-out period for private property downgraders. Industry analysts attribute the persistent price increases to limited supply of large flats in mature estates, strong household income growth averaging 5 to 7 per cent annually, and the continued appeal of HDB flats as an affordable alternative to private property in a market where condo prices now frequently exceed S$2,000 per square foot.

Our analysis

The 238 million-dollar HDB transactions in October mark a psychological threshold for Singapore's public housing market — a market that was designed, fundamentally, to provide affordable housing for the population and in which a seven-figure price tag would have been unimaginable a decade ago. The steady escalation — from occasional million-dollar transactions to 238 in a single month — reflects structural changes in Singapore's housing market that go beyond cyclical demand. Three forces are converging. First, income growth: Singapore's GDP expansion of 6 per cent in 2026, driven by the AI boom, has lifted household incomes significantly, expanding the pool of buyers who can afford seven-figure flats. When combined with dual-income professional households, the affordability mathematics of million-dollar HDB flats are less extreme than the headline suggests — a couple earning S$15,000 per month combined, which is increasingly common in Singapore's professional class, can service a S$1 million mortgage within HDB's existing financial framework. Second, the supply constraint: large flats (5-room and executive) in mature estates with remaining leases of 70 years or more are genuinely scarce, and no policy intervention short of compulsory acquisition can increase their supply in the near term. These flats are in established neighbourhoods with excellent transport connectivity, schools, and amenities — location advantages that command a premium in any housing market globally. Third, the private-public arbitrage: with private condominiums now frequently transacting above S$2,000 per square foot, a 1,200-square-foot HDB flat in a central location at S$1.5 million represents approximately S$1,250 per square foot — a 35 to 40 per cent discount to private property for comparable floor space in similar locations. For buyers priced out of the private market, million-dollar HDB flats are the rational alternative. The policy implications are significant. Singapore's social compact has historically been built on the premise that HDB housing is accessible to the broad middle class. When a quarter of monthly resale transactions exceed S$1 million, that compact is under strain — not because lower-income households cannot access subsidised Build-to-Order flats, but because the resale market is developing its own dynamic that increasingly resembles the private property market in pricing behaviour. The government's cooling measures have moderated the pace of appreciation but have not reversed it, suggesting that the structural drivers are more powerful than the policy tools currently deployed.

What we don't know yet

Whether the government will implement additional cooling measures specifically targeting the HDB resale market, such as resale levy adjustments or tighter financing limits for million-dollar transactions. The distribution of million-dollar transactions by flat type and estate — specifically whether the phenomenon is spreading beyond DBSS and central-area flats to ordinary BTO resale units. How much of the demand is driven by private property downgraders converting equity into HDB purchases, and whether the 15-month wait-out period has had any measurable effect on this channel. The income profile of million-dollar HDB buyers — whether these are predominantly dual-income professional households or include speculative purchasers. Whether the trend will plateau as prices approach a ceiling determined by HDB mortgage limits, or whether the ceiling itself will be raised in response to market reality. The long-term implications for the HDB flat as a retirement asset — whether million-dollar flats will retain value over the remaining lease, particularly as leases shorten below 60 years.

原创分析

10月238套百万组屋交易标志着新加坡公共住房市场的心理门槛——这个市场从根本上是为人口提供可负担住房而设计的，十年前七位数价格是不可想象的。从偶发的百万交易到单月238套的稳步升级，反映了新加坡住房市场超越周期性需求的结构性变化。三股力量正在汇聚。第一，收入增长：新加坡2026年在AI热潮驱动下6%的GDP增长显著提升了家庭收入，扩大了能够负担七位数组屋的买家群体。结合双职工专业家庭，百万组屋的可负担性计算没有标题所暗示的那么极端——月收入合计1.5万新元的夫妇在HDB现有金融框架内可以偿还100万新元的抵押贷款，这在新加坡专业阶层中越来越普遍。第二，供应约束：成熟区剩余租约70年或以上的大型组屋（五房式和行政式）确实稀缺，除了强制征收外没有政策干预可以在短期内增加其供应。这些组屋位于交通便利、学校和设施齐全的成熟社区——在全球任何住房市场都会获得溢价的位置优势。第三，私宅与公屋的套利：私人公寓现在经常以每平方英尺2,000新元以上成交，中心位置1,200平方英尺的HDB组屋以150万新元计算约为每平方英尺1,250新元——在类似位置相比私宅折让35%至40%。对于被私宅市场排除在外的买家，百万组屋是理性的替代选择。政策影响重大。新加坡的社会契约历来建立在HDB住房对广大中产阶级可及的前提上。当每月四分之一的转售交易超过100万新元时，这一契约面临压力——不是因为低收入家庭无法获得补贴的预购组屋，而是因为转售市场正在发展出其自身的动态，在定价行为上越来越类似私宅市场。政府的降温措施已放缓升值速度但未逆转，表明结构性驱动因素比目前部署的政策工具更为强大。

未知事项

政府是否会实施专门针对HDB转售市场的额外降温措施，例如调整转售税或收紧百万交易的融资限制。百万交易按组屋类型和区域的分布——具体而言该现象是否正从DBSS和中心区域组屋扩展到普通预购转售单位。有多少需求来自私宅降级者将资产转换为HDB购买，以及15个月等待期是否对此渠道产生了可衡量的效果。百万组屋买家的收入特征——是以双职工专业家庭为主还是包括投机性购买者。趋势是否会在价格接近由HDB按揭贷款限额决定的天花板时趋于平稳，还是天花板本身会根据市场现实被提高。百万组屋作为退休资产的长期影响——特别是当租约缩短至60年以下时，百万组屋是否会保值。

Bank of Thailand Projects 2027 Inflation Dipping Negative as K-Shaped Recovery Deepens and Oil Fund Deficit Hits 108 Billion Baht

24–26 November 2026. Bank of Thailand / National Economic and Social Development Council / Oil Fuel Fund Office / Bangkok Post / Reuters.

Verified reporting

The Bank of Thailand's (BoT) latest Monetary Policy Report projects headline inflation falling to minus 1.3 per cent in certain months of 2027, with the annual average hovering near zero, raising the prospect of outright deflation for the first time since 2009. The projection is driven by a combination of declining global oil prices, weak domestic demand, the anticipated unwinding of diesel subsidy caps, and the high base effect from energy price adjustments in mid-2026.

GDP growth is projected at 2.4 per cent for 2026 and 2.1 per cent for 2027, among the lowest in ASEAN. The BoT held its benchmark one-day repurchase rate at 1.00 per cent at its November meeting — the fifth consecutive hold — with a 5-to-2 vote, two members favouring a cut to 0.75 per cent. Governor Vitai Ratanakorn reiterated that the rate is "consistent with sustainably returning inflation to target" and that "structural problems cannot be solved by monetary policy alone."

The K-shaped recovery that the BoT has described for several quarters showed further evidence of deepening. The technology export sector and large corporates with AI supply chain exposure are performing strongly — the Board of Investment reported Bt1.71 trillion (US$50.3 billion) in applications for January to September 2026, up 28 per cent, with approximately 70 per cent AI-related. However, small and medium enterprises (SMEs), domestic-oriented services, and agriculture remain weak. Household debt stands at 91.4 per cent of GDP, among the highest in Asia, constraining consumer spending.

The Oil Fuel Fund deficit has reached 108 billion baht (US$3.2 billion), accumulated from subsidising domestic fuel prices below international market levels. The Fund's governing board is under pressure to unwind the subsidies by allowing retail diesel and gasoline prices to rise, which would narrow the deficit but add to consumer cost pressures in an already weak domestic economy. Tourism arrivals for January to October 2026 totalled 25.46 million, down 4.59 per cent year-on-year, with the revised full-year target lowered to 31.5 million from 2025's actual 32.97 million.

Our analysis

The prospect of negative inflation in 2027 would make Thailand the only major ASEAN economy experiencing deflation — a distinction that highlights the depth of the structural challenges facing the Thai economy. Deflation is not simply low inflation; it creates a distinct set of economic pathologies. When prices are expected to fall, consumers and businesses delay purchases and investment in anticipation of lower future prices, which further suppresses demand and reinforces the deflationary cycle. For an economy with household debt at 91.4 per cent of GDP, deflation is particularly pernicious because it increases the real burden of debt — incomes stagnate or fall while debt obligations remain fixed, squeezing disposable income and increasing default risk. The K-shaped recovery is Thailand's defining economic characteristic in 2026-2027. The divergence between the AI-linked export sector — booming, attracting billions in investment — and the domestic economy — struggling with weak demand, high household debt, declining tourism, and near-zero inflation — represents a form of structural dualisation that monetary policy genuinely cannot resolve. The 5-to-2 vote to hold rates at 1.00 per cent, with two members favouring a cut, reflects this tension: cutting rates might stimulate domestic demand and weaken the baht (supporting exporters and tourism), but with rates already at 1.00 per cent, the marginal impact of a 25-basis-point cut on real economic activity would be minimal while potentially creating financial stability risks. The Oil Fuel Fund deficit of 108 billion baht is a fiscal time bomb. The government has been subsidising fuel prices to shield consumers from international price volatility, but the accumulated deficit must eventually be unwound either through higher retail fuel prices or a fiscal transfer from the government budget. If fuel subsidies are removed at a time when the domestic economy is already weak and inflation is approaching zero, the resulting price increase could simultaneously push headline inflation positive while further suppressing consumer spending — an uncomfortable policy trade-off. The tourism decline compounds the problem. Thailand's tourism sector employs approximately 20 per cent of the workforce either directly or indirectly, and when tourism contracts, it removes income from precisely the population segments that drive domestic consumption. The 20 per cent potential decline in Chinese arrivals in November-December, flagged by the Association of Thai Travel Agents, would exacerbate this pressure. The comparison with the Philippines is instructive: Thailand has the opposite problem — not too much inflation but too little — but both countries share the common challenge of an economy that monetary policy alone cannot fix. The Philippines needs fiscal measures to address supply-side inflation; Thailand needs structural reforms to address demand-side weakness. Neither has an easy path forward.

What we don't know yet

Whether the BoT will cut rates at its February 2027 meeting if the inflation data confirms deflationary trends, and whether a dissenting minority will grow. The Oil Fuel Fund unwinding timeline — specifically whether the government will allow fuel prices to rise in Q1 2027, and the estimated impact on headline inflation and consumer spending. Whether the government will implement fiscal stimulus measures to offset weak domestic demand, and what fiscal space is available given the existing Oil Fuel Fund deficit and other fiscal obligations. The depth of the K-shaped divergence — specifically whether AI-related investment is creating any measurable employment or income spillover into the broader economy, or whether the gains are captured entirely by large corporates and foreign investors. Whether the tourism decline is cyclical — driven by global economic uncertainty and the China slowdown — or structural, reflecting competition from Vietnam, Japan, and other destinations that have improved their tourism offerings. The household debt trajectory and whether the BoT's macro-prudential measures are sufficient to prevent a debt-driven consumption contraction.

泰国央行预测2027年通胀转负，K型复苏加深且油价基金赤字达1080亿泰铢

2026年11月24–26日。泰国银行 / 国家经济社会发展委员会 / 油价基金办公室 / 曼谷邮报 / 路透社。

经核实的报道

泰国银行（BoT）最新货币政策报告预测2027年某些月份整体通胀将降至负1.3%，全年平均接近零，自2009年以来首次出现彻底通缩的前景。该预测由全球油价下跌、国内需求疲弱、柴油补贴上限预期取消以及2026年中能源价格调整的高基数效应共同驱动。

GDP增长预测为2026年2.4%和2027年2.1%，在东盟中属最低之列。BoT在11月会议上以5比2的投票将基准一日回购利率维持在1.00%——连续第五次维持不变——两名成员支持降至0.75%。行长维泰·拉达纳功重申利率"与通胀可持续回归目标一致"且"结构性问题不能仅靠货币政策解决"。

BoT数个季度以来描述的K型复苏显示出进一步加深的迹象。科技出口部门和拥有AI供应链敞口的大型企业表现强劲——投资委员会报告2026年1月至9月投资申请1.71万亿泰铢（503亿美元），增长28%，其中约70%与AI相关。然而中小企业（SME）、面向国内的服务业和农业仍然疲弱。家庭债务占GDP的91.4%，为亚洲最高之列，制约消费支出。

油价基金赤字已达1,080亿泰铢（32亿美元），由补贴国内燃油价格低于国际市场水平累积而来。基金管理委员会面临压力，需通过允许零售柴油和汽油价格上涨来取消补贴，这将缩小赤字但在本已疲弱的国内经济中增加消费者成本压力。2026年1月至10月旅游入境人数为2,546万，同比下降4.59%，修订后的全年目标从2025年实际3,297万降至3,150万。

原创分析

2027年通胀转负的前景将使泰国成为唯一经历通缩的主要东盟经济体——这一特征凸显了泰国经济面临的结构性挑战之深。通缩不仅仅是低通胀；它会创造一系列独特的经济病症。当预期价格将下跌时，消费者和企业会延迟购买和投资以等待未来更低的价格，这进一步抑制需求并强化通缩循环。对于家庭债务占GDP 91.4%的经济体，通缩尤为有害，因为它增加了债务的实际负担——收入停滞或下降而债务义务固定不变，挤压可支配收入并增加违约风险。K型复苏是泰国2026-2027年的决定性经济特征。与AI相关的出口部门——蓬勃发展、吸引数十亿投资——与国内经济——需求疲弱、家庭债务高企、旅游业下滑、通胀接近零——之间的分化代表着货币政策确实无法解决的结构性二元化。5比2的投票维持利率在1.00%，两名成员支持降息，反映了这种张力：降息可能刺激国内需求并削弱泰铢（支持出口商和旅游业），但在利率已经1.00%的情况下，25个基点降息对实际经济活动的边际影响极小，同时可能产生金融稳定风险。油价基金1,080亿泰铢的赤字是一颗财政定时炸弹。政府一直在补贴燃油价格以保护消费者免受国际价格波动影响，但累积的赤字最终必须通过提高零售燃油价格或政府预算的财政转移来化解。如果在国内经济已经疲弱、通胀接近零时取消燃油补贴，由此产生的价格上涨可能在推高整体通胀的同时进一步抑制消费支出——一个令人不安的政策权衡。旅游业下滑加剧了问题。泰国旅游业直接或间接雇用约20%的劳动力，当旅游收缩时，它正好从驱动国内消费的人群中移除收入。泰国旅行社协会标记的11月-12月中国游客可能减少20%将加剧这一压力。与菲律宾的比较具有启示性：泰国有着相反的问题——不是通胀过高而是过低——但两国共同面临仅靠货币政策无法解决经济问题的挑战。菲律宾需要财政措施解决供给侧通胀；泰国需要结构性改革解决需求侧疲弱。两者都没有容易的前进道路。

未知事项

如果通胀数据确认通缩趋势，BoT是否会在2027年2月会议上降息，以及反对少数是否会增加。油价基金取消补贴的时间表——具体而言政府是否会在2027年第一季度允许燃油价格上涨，以及对整体通胀和消费支出的估计影响。政府是否会实施财政刺激措施以抵消疲弱的国内需求，以及考虑到现有油价基金赤字和其他财政义务的可用财政空间。K型分化的深度——具体而言AI相关投资是否正在创造任何可衡量的就业或收入溢出至更广泛经济，还是收益完全被大型企业和外国投资者捕获。旅游业下滑是周期性的——由全球经济不确定性和中国放缓驱动——还是结构性的，反映了来自越南、日本和其他已改善旅游产品的目的地的竞争。家庭债务轨迹以及BoT的宏观审慎措施是否足以防止债务驱动的消费收缩。
