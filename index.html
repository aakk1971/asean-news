Malaysia's Economy Grows 5.5% in Q3 2026, Beating Estimates as Tech Exports Surge 66.9%

18 November 2026. BNM / The Star / Business Today / NST.

Verified reporting

Malaysia's economy expanded 5.5 per cent year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026, according to revised data released by Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) on 18 November, surpassing the Department of Statistics' advance estimate of 5.3 per cent but moderating from the 6.0 per cent recorded in Q2. On a quarter-on-quarter seasonally adjusted basis, GDP grew 1.5 per cent, down from 2.5 per cent in the previous quarter. Growth for the first nine months of 2026 averaged 5.6 per cent.

Electrical and electronic goods exports jumped 66.9 per cent year-on-year, while total exports grew 5.7 per cent. Investment and exports were the key contributors to expansion. Private consumption moderated from Q2, which BNM attributed primarily to base effects from higher government cash transfers in the third quarter of 2025 rather than any fundamental weakening of household demand.

BNM Governor Datuk Sri Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour said Malaysia's economy is poised to enter 2027 "from a position of strength," supported by sound fundamentals. The government in October raised its full-year 2026 GDP forecast to close to the upper end of the 4.8 to 5.3 per cent range, putting Malaysia on track to exceed 5 per cent growth for a third consecutive year. The 2027 growth forecast stands at 4.2 to 5.2 per cent. BNM projects headline inflation at 1.5 to 2.5 per cent for 2026 and 1.8 to 2.8 per cent for 2027, noting that higher input costs may pass through with a delay, though targeted fuel subsidies and stable domestic demand should mitigate the impact. BNM held its benchmark overnight policy rate unchanged for the eighth consecutive meeting, with economists widely expecting a rate hike in the first quarter of 2027.

Our analysis

Malaysia's revised Q3 GDP of 5.5 per cent — beating the advance estimate and maintaining above-5 per cent growth for a ninth consecutive quarter — confirms the economy's resilience even as the pace inevitably moderates from Q2's 6.0 per cent. The 66.9 per cent surge in electrical and electronic exports is the standout figure, demonstrating that Malaysia is capturing an outsized share of the global AI and semiconductor supply chain buildout alongside Singapore. This dovetails with yesterday's MIDA data showing RM123.5 billion in data centre approvals and the United States surging to become Malaysia's top foreign investor — the investment pipeline is now translating directly into trade flows. The moderation in private consumption is not alarming: BNM's attribution to base effects from 2025 cash transfers is credible, and the labour market remains tight enough to sustain household spending. The more interesting signal is BNM's eighth consecutive rate hold despite growth consistently above 5 per cent and economists pricing in a hike by Q1 2027. HSBC's forecast of a BNM rate increase in early 2027 reflects the view that the central bank will need to normalise monetary policy as inflation edges higher toward the 1.8–2.8 per cent range projected for next year. The risk is that Malaysia's AI-driven export boom creates overheating pressures that force BNM to tighten more aggressively than markets currently expect, particularly if the ringgit faces depreciation pressure from the strong US dollar environment.

What we don't know yet

The full sector breakdown by services, manufacturing, mining, agriculture, and construction for Q3. Whether the 66.9 per cent E&E export surge is broadening across product categories or concentrated in a few AI-related segments. How BNM will balance the tension between supporting growth and preventing overheating when it next reviews rates in January 2027.

马来西亚第三季度经济增长5.5%超预期，科技出口飙升66.9%

2026年11月18日。马来西亚国家银行 / 星报 / 今日商业 / 新海峡时报。

经核实的报道

根据马来西亚国家银行（BNM）11月18日发布的修订数据，马来西亚第三季度经济同比增长5.5%，超过统计局5.3%的初步估计，但低于第二季度的6.0%。经季节性调整后环比增长1.5%，低于上一季度的2.5%。2026年前九个月平均增长5.6%。

电子电气产品出口同比飙升66.9%，总出口增长5.7%。投资和出口是经济扩张的主要贡献因素。私人消费较第二季度有所放缓，BNM将此主要归因于2025年第三季度政府现金转移较高带来的基数效应，而非家庭需求的根本性减弱。

BNM总裁拿督斯里阿卜杜勒·拉希德·加福尔表示，马来西亚经济在稳健的基本面支撑下，有望以"强势地位"进入2027年。政府10月将2026年全年GDP预测上调至4.8%至5.3%区间的上限附近，使马来西亚有望连续第三年实现超过5%的增长。2027年增长预测为4.2%至5.2%。BNM预计2026年整体通胀率为1.5%至2.5%，2027年为1.8%至2.8%，指出较高的投入成本可能延迟传导，但有针对性的燃油补贴和稳定的国内需求应能缓解影响。BNM连续第八次会议维持隔夜政策利率不变，经济学家普遍预期将于2027年第一季度加息。

原创分析

马来西亚第三季度GDP修订至5.5%——超过初步估计，并连续第九个季度保持5%以上增长——证实了经济的韧性，即便增速不可避免地从第二季度的6.0%回落。电子电气出口飙升66.9%是最突出的数据，表明马来西亚正与新加坡一起，在全球AI和半导体供应链建设中占据超额份额。这与昨日MIDA数据显示的1,235亿令吉数据中心审批以及美国跃升为马来西亚最大外资来源国相呼应——投资管道现在正直接转化为贸易流量。私人消费的放缓并不令人担忧：BNM将其归因于2025年现金转移的基数效应是可信的，劳动力市场仍然足够紧张以维持家庭支出。更有趣的信号是，尽管增长持续超过5%且经济学家预计2027年第一季度加息，BNM仍连续第八次维持利率不变。汇丰预测BNM将在2027年初加息，反映出央行需要在通胀向明年1.8%–2.8%的预测区间上行时实现货币政策正常化的观点。风险在于，AI驱动的出口繁荣可能产生过热压力，迫使BNM比市场目前预期更积极地收紧，尤其是在强美元环境下令吉面临贬值压力时。

未知事项

第三季度按服务业、制造业、矿业、农业和建筑业的完整行业细分数据。66.9%的电子电气出口飙升是在各产品类别中广泛分布还是集中在少数AI相关领域。BNM在2027年1月下次审议利率时将如何平衡支持增长与防止过热之间的张力。

Bank Indonesia Surprises Markets with Rate Hike to 6.00% as Rupiah Hits Record Low of 18,285 per Dollar

18 November 2026. Bank Indonesia / Indonesia Investments / Investing.com / Xinhua.

Verified reporting

Bank Indonesia unexpectedly raised its benchmark seven-day reverse repo rate by 25 basis points to 6.00 per cent on 18 November, bringing the deposit facility rate to 5.25 per cent and the lending facility rate to 6.75 per cent. It was the fourth rate hike since May 2026, taking cumulative tightening to 125 basis points from the 4.75 per cent level at the start of the hiking cycle. The rate now stands at its highest since January 2025.

The decision surprised the majority of economists: in a survey of 11 analysts, 54.55 per cent had expected a hold at 5.75 per cent, while 36.36 per cent correctly forecast a 25 basis point increase and 9.09 per cent anticipated a 50 basis point hike. Governor Destry Damayanti said the move was aimed at stabilising the rupiah amid heightened global uncertainty and keeping inflation within the 2.5 per cent plus-or-minus 1 percentage point target for 2026 and 2027.

The rupiah had fallen to a record low of 18,285 per US dollar on the day, extending its 2026 loss to approximately 8.7 per cent — the worst performance among emerging Asian currencies. The currency rose 0.2 per cent after the decision. The Jakarta Composite Index initially rose 0.4 per cent on the announcement but closed the session at 5,247.88, down 56.88 points or 1.07 per cent. Annual inflation stood at 3.23 per cent in October, within the target band but near its upper limit, with core inflation at 2.56 per cent. Third-quarter GDP growth reached 5.47 per cent year-on-year, up from 5.29 per cent in Q2. Commercial bank lending grew 13.21 per cent year-on-year in October. The rate hike comes amid continued political uncertainty following the resignation of Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka and the ongoing MSCI reclassification consultation that could see Indonesia downgraded from Emerging Market to Standalone Market status.

Our analysis

Bank Indonesia's surprise hike to 6.00 per cent — against majority expectations of a hold — reveals the depth of policymakers' concern about the rupiah's accelerating depreciation. The currency's 8.7 per cent loss in 2026, the worst among emerging Asian peers, reflects a perfect storm: the MSCI reclassification threat that has driven an estimated 45 per cent decline in the Jakarta Composite from its January peak, persistent capital outflows from the bond market, and a strengthening US dollar that has pressured all emerging market currencies. The 125 basis points of cumulative tightening since May — from 4.75 to 6.00 per cent — is aggressive by any standard, and yet the rupiah barely responded, gaining just 0.2 per cent. This underscores the limits of monetary policy when the fundamental drivers of currency weakness are governance-related (MSCI) and geopolitical (US dollar strength) rather than inflation-related. Indeed, with inflation at 3.23 per cent — well within target — and GDP growing at a healthy 5.47 per cent, the economy does not intrinsically need tighter monetary policy. BI is hiking to defend the currency, not to cool the economy, and that creates a dangerous tension: higher rates risk choking the lending growth (currently 13.21 per cent) that is supporting the real economy's expansion. Capital Economics' forecast of two more hikes before the cycle ends suggests the market expects BI to push rates toward 6.50 per cent, which would be the most restrictive monetary stance Indonesia has experienced in over a decade. The fundamental resolution lies not in rate hikes but in addressing the MSCI governance concerns before the January 2027 consultation deadline.

What we don't know yet

How the rupiah will perform in coming weeks given that the initial post-decision bounce was minimal. Whether Bank Indonesia will hike again in December or pause to assess the impact. The scale of foreign bond outflows that have contributed to the rupiah's weakness. Whether Indonesia's regulatory authorities will announce specific governance reforms before the MSCI consultation closes on 15 January 2027.

印尼央行意外加息至6.00%，印尼盾创历史新低至18,285兑1美元

2026年11月18日。印尼央行 / Indonesia Investments / Investing.com / 新华社。

经核实的报道

印尼央行11月18日意外将基准七天逆回购利率上调25个基点至6.00%，存款便利利率升至5.25%，贷款便利利率升至6.75%。这是自2026年5月以来的第四次加息，累计从加息周期初始的4.75%收紧125个基点。目前利率处于2025年1月以来的最高水平。

该决定出乎大多数经济学家意料：在对11位分析师的调查中，54.55%预期维持5.75%不变，36.36%正确预测加息25个基点，9.09%预期加息50个基点。行长德斯特里·达马扬蒂表示，此举旨在面对加剧的全球不确定性稳定印尼盾，并将通胀保持在2026年和2027年2.5%上下1个百分点的目标范围内。

当日印尼盾跌至18,285兑1美元的历史新低，2026年累计贬值约8.7%——为新兴亚洲货币中表现最差。决定公布后印尼盾回升0.2%。雅加达综合指数最初上涨0.4%，但收盘报5,247.88点，下跌56.88点或1.07%。10月年通胀率为3.23%，处于目标范围内但接近上限，核心通胀为2.56%。第三季度GDP同比增长5.47%，高于第二季度的5.29%。10月商业银行贷款同比增长13.21%。此次加息正值副总统吉布兰辞职带来的政治不确定性持续，以及MSCI可能将印尼从新兴市场降级为独立市场的重新分类磋商仍在进行之际。

原创分析

印尼央行意外加息至6.00%——违背多数预期的维持不变——揭示了决策者对印尼盾加速贬值的深度担忧。印尼盾2026年8.7%的跌幅为新兴亚洲最差，反映了完美风暴：MSCI重新分类威胁推动雅加达综合指数从1月高点预计下跌45%，债券市场持续资本外流，以及强势美元对所有新兴市场货币的压力。自5月以来累计125个基点的收紧——从4.75%到6.00%——以任何标准衡量都是激进的，然而印尼盾几乎没有反应，仅上涨0.2%。这凸显了当货币疲软的根本驱动因素是治理相关（MSCI）和地缘政治（美元走强）而非通胀相关时，货币政策的局限性。事实上，在通胀3.23%——远在目标范围内——且GDP健康增长5.47%的情况下，经济本身并不需要更紧缩的货币政策。印尼央行加息是为了捍卫汇率，而非为了冷却经济，这造成了危险的张力：更高的利率有可能扼杀正在支撑实体经济扩张的贷款增长（目前13.21%）。凯投宏观预测在周期结束前还将再加息两次，表明市场预期印尼央行将把利率推向6.50%，这将是印尼十多年来最紧缩的货币政策立场。根本解决方案不在于加息，而在于在2027年1月磋商截止日期前解决MSCI的治理关切。

未知事项

鉴于决定公布后的初始反弹微乎其微，印尼盾未来几周将如何表现。印尼央行是否会在12月再次加息还是暂停以评估影响。导致印尼盾疲软的外资债券流出规模。印尼监管机构是否会在MSCI磋商于2027年1月15日截止前宣布具体的治理改革措施。

US and Thailand Substantially Conclude Reciprocal Trade Framework as Bangkok Pledges to Eliminate Tariffs on American Goods

17–18 November 2026. White House / USTR / AP / Malay Mail.

Verified reporting

The United States and Thailand announced on the sidelines of the ASEAN Summit in Manila that they had "substantially concluded negotiations for an Agreement on Reciprocal Trade," according to a joint statement signed by President Trump and Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul. Under the proposed framework, Thailand would eliminate tariffs on approximately 99 per cent of American exports and remove substantial non-tariff barriers affecting automobiles, medical devices, pharmaceuticals, ethanol, and poultry.

Thailand agreed in principle to accept vehicles meeting US federal standards and to recognise certain FDA certificates and marketing authorisations for medical devices and pharmaceuticals. Thailand would also issue import permits for American ethanol and address barriers affecting US meat, poultry, and other agricultural products. The framework includes approximately US$2.6 billion in annual purchases of American agricultural products — specifically feed corn, soybean meal, and dried distillers grains with solubles. It also references approximately US$5.4 billion in annual US energy purchases and 80 American aircraft valued at US$18.8 billion as forthcoming commercial arrangements.

Bilateral goods trade in 2025 totalled approximately US$110.8 billion, with US exports to Thailand at US$19.5 billion and US imports from Thailand at US$91.3 billion, producing a US goods trade deficit with Thailand of US$71.9 billion. The agreement has not yet been signed or implemented — both governments must complete internal procedures before it takes effect. The framework builds on an initial framework reached in October 2025. Trump indicated the deal could be completed in the coming weeks, while Anutin acknowledged that further work remained before formal signing.

Our analysis

The US-Thailand trade framework is the most significant bilateral trade development in ASEAN this year, though its current status as a "substantially concluded" framework rather than a signed agreement means the precise terms remain subject to change. The headline commitment — Thailand eliminating tariffs on 99 per cent of US goods — represents a dramatic concession from Bangkok, driven by the need to address the US$71.9 billion trade deficit that made Thailand a prominent target for US reciprocal tariff policy. The agricultural purchases of US$2.6 billion annually are concrete and immediate: feed corn, soybean meal, and dried distillers grains are commodities that Thailand's livestock industry already imports, so redirecting purchases toward US suppliers is feasible without disrupting domestic supply chains. The aircraft and energy references — US$18.8 billion and US$5.4 billion respectively — are described as forthcoming commercial arrangements rather than government commitments, suggesting they involve Thai airlines and energy companies that may or may not have finalised these deals. For Thailand's domestic politics, the deal is a double-edged sword: it demonstrates Anutin's ability to engage constructively with the United States and potentially avoid punitive tariffs, but the tariff elimination commitment will face resistance from Thai manufacturers who have benefited from protectionist barriers, particularly in the automotive and pharmaceutical sectors. The critical question is timing — if internal procedures drag into 2027, the framework risks being overtaken by shifts in US trade policy priorities.

What we don't know yet

The precise timeline for internal ratification procedures in both countries. Whether the aircraft and energy deals are firm commitments from Thai companies or aspirational targets. The applicable US tariff rate on Thai goods under the current framework — the 2025 framework referenced 19 per cent, but current applicable rates may differ. How Thai automotive manufacturers and pharmaceutical companies will respond to the tariff elimination commitment.

美泰基本达成对等贸易框架协议，曼谷承诺取消对美国商品关税

2026年11月17–18日。白宫 / 美国贸易代表办公室 / 美联社 / 马来邮报。

经核实的报道

美国和泰国在马尼拉东盟峰会期间宣布，双方已"基本完成对等贸易协议谈判"，根据特朗普总统与泰国总理阿努廷签署的联合声明。在拟议框架下，泰国将取消对约99%美国出口产品的关税，并消除影响汽车、医疗器械、药品、乙醇和禽肉的主要非关税壁垒。

泰国原则上同意接受符合美国联邦标准的车辆，并承认美国FDA对医疗器械和药品的某些证书和上市许可。泰国还将为美国乙醇发放进口许可证，并解决影响美国肉类、禽肉和其他农产品的壁垒。该框架包括每年约26亿美元的美国农产品采购——具体为饲料玉米、豆粕和干酒糟及可溶物。框架还提及约54亿美元的美国年度能源采购和价值188亿美元的80架美国飞机，作为即将达成的商业安排。

2025年双边商品贸易总额约为1,108亿美元，其中美国对泰出口195亿美元，美国自泰进口913亿美元，美国对泰商品贸易逆差719亿美元。协议尚未签署或实施——双方政府须完成内部程序方可生效。该框架建立在2025年10月达成的初步框架基础之上。特朗普表示协议可能在未来几周内完成，而阿努廷承认在正式签署前仍有工作要做。

原创分析

美泰贸易框架是今年东盟最重要的双边贸易进展，尽管其目前作为"基本达成"框架而非已签署协议的状态意味着确切条款仍可能变化。标题承诺——泰国取消99%美国商品关税——代表曼谷的重大让步，这是出于解决使泰国成为美国对等关税政策突出目标的719亿美元贸易逆差的需要。每年26亿美元的农产品采购是具体且即时的：饲料玉米、豆粕和干酒糟是泰国畜牧业已经进口的大宗商品，因此将采购转向美国供应商是可行的，不会扰乱国内供应链。飞机和能源提及——分别为188亿和54亿美元——被描述为即将达成的商业安排而非政府承诺，表明涉及的是可能但尚未最终确定这些交易的泰国航空公司和能源企业。对泰国国内政治而言，该协议是一把双刃剑：它展示了阿努廷与美国建设性接触并可能避免惩罚性关税的能力，但关税取消承诺将面临受益于保护壁垒的泰国制造商的抵制，特别是在汽车和制药领域。关键问题是时间——如果内部程序拖延到2027年，该框架有被美国贸易政策优先事项变化所超越的风险。

未知事项

两国内部批准程序的确切时间表。飞机和能源交易是泰国企业的确定承诺还是愿景目标。当前框架下美国对泰国商品的适用关税税率——2025年框架提及19%，但目前适用税率可能不同。泰国汽车制造商和制药企业将如何回应关税取消承诺。

Vietnam's Trade Turnover Surpasses US$1 Trillion for First Time as Exports and Imports Surge in 2026

November 2026. Vietnam Briefing / VietnamNet / Xinhua / BERNAMA.

Verified reporting

Vietnam's total merchandise trade surpassed US$1 trillion for the first time in the country's history during the first ten months of 2026, reaching US$1,010.55 billion — up 32.3 per cent year-on-year, according to data from the General Statistics Office. Exports totalled US$499.24 billion, rising 27.5 per cent, while imports reached US$511.31 billion, surging 37.4 per cent. The cumulative trade balance showed a US$12.07 billion deficit as import growth outpaced exports.

In October alone, exports reached US$64.67 billion and imports US$57.36 billion, producing a monthly trade surplus of US$7.31 billion — a sharp improvement from September's US$1.30 billion surplus. Foreign-invested enterprises accounted for US$405.31 billion or 81.2 per cent of total exports, up 33 per cent, while domestic enterprises exported US$93.93 billion or 18.8 per cent, up 8.2 per cent. A total of 37 product categories exceeded US$1 billion each, together accounting for 94.9 per cent of exports, with eight categories exceeding US$10 billion and comprising 73.4 per cent of exports.

By trading partner, Vietnam maintained a surplus of US$140.10 billion with the United States and US$40.84 billion with the European Union, while running deficits of US$134.94 billion with China, US$48.63 billion with South Korea, and US$16.93 billion with ASEAN. From 24 July 2026, covered Vietnamese-origin products face an additional 12.5 per cent Section 301 duty when entering the United States, subject to exemptions. Registered foreign direct investment in the first nine months reached US$50.36 billion, up 76.4 per cent, while disbursed FDI hit US$21.07 billion, up 12.1 per cent — the highest for the same period in five years.

Our analysis

Vietnam crossing the US$1 trillion trade threshold is a symbolic milestone that reflects a genuine structural transformation. A country that exported less than US$100 billion as recently as 2012 now trades more than a trillion dollars annually — a tenfold increase in 14 years driven by the relentless expansion of foreign-invested manufacturing, particularly in electronics, textiles, and machinery. The 32.3 per cent year-on-year growth rate is exceptional even by Vietnam's standards and suggests the trade boom is accelerating rather than plateauing. However, two structural features deserve scrutiny. First, foreign-invested enterprises dominate exports at 81.2 per cent, meaning the trade achievement is substantially the accomplishment of Samsung, Apple suppliers, Intel, and other multinational corporations using Vietnam as a manufacturing platform — domestic enterprises contribute only 18.8 per cent and are growing at a much slower 8.2 per cent. Second, the US$140.10 billion surplus with the United States — by far the largest bilateral surplus of any ASEAN economy — makes Vietnam the most prominent target for continued US trade rebalancing. The 12.5 per cent Section 301 duty imposed in July is likely only the beginning; a surplus of this magnitude attracts political attention regardless of which party holds the White House. The cumulative deficit of US$12.07 billion despite massive export growth indicates that Vietnam's import bill is growing even faster, driven by the machinery, components, and raw materials that feed the export machine. The registered FDI surge of 76.4 per cent to US$50.36 billion confirms that manufacturers are still choosing Vietnam despite the tariff headwinds, though the gap between registered and disbursed FDI remains a persistent challenge.

What we don't know yet

The specific product categories driving the eight groups that each exceeded US$10 billion in exports — the data does not name them. Whether the Section 301 tariff has measurably diverted any planned US-bound export capacity away from Vietnam. How much of the import surge represents investment in new manufacturing capacity versus simply higher input costs for existing production.

Singapore Assumes ASEAN Chairmanship for 2027 with 'Deeper Integration, Wider Partnerships' Agenda

17–18 November 2026. AsiaOne / Manila Bulletin / Bloomberg / Yahoo Singapore.

Verified reporting

Singapore formally assumed the chairmanship of ASEAN for 2027 on 17 November as Prime Minister Lawrence Wong received the ceremonial gavel from Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. at the closing ceremony of the 49th ASEAN Summit in Manila. Singapore's chairmanship theme is "Deeper Integration, Wider Partnerships," and Wong indicated the bloc must take further steps so that businesses can set up and operate more smoothly across borders, and so people have more chances to work, invest, and prosper.

Wong flagged artificial intelligence and clean energy as emerging opportunities that ASEAN should harness collectively, building on the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA) signed under the Philippine chairmanship. He cited external pressures including major-power rivalry, a fragmented global economic order, and conflicts beyond the region that could affect ASEAN's economies, energy supplies, and livelihoods, saying ASEAN "will continue to face external pressures that will test our cohesion and unity."

The 2027 chairmanship will mark several significant milestones: ASEAN's 60th anniversary, the 50th anniversaries of ASEAN's formal relations with the United States, Canada, and the European Union, and the 30th anniversary of ASEAN Plus Three cooperation with China, Japan, and South Korea. Under the Philippines' 2026 chairmanship, notable achievements included the signing of DEFA — the world's first legally binding regional digital economy agreement — the early entry into force of the upgraded ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement, the early entry into force of the ASEAN–China Free Trade Agreement 3.0, and progress on Timor-Leste's integration into ASEAN, with Timor-Leste expected to assume the chairmanship in 2029. Marcos said in his closing remarks that "as ASEAN enters its 60th year, it is proper that Singapore should now take its helm."

Our analysis

Singapore's assumption of the ASEAN chair could not come at a more consequential moment. The 2027 chairmanship coincides with ASEAN's 60th anniversary and a cluster of milestone anniversaries with dialogue partners, giving Singapore natural platforms for high-profile diplomatic engagement. Wong's emphasis on AI and clean energy cooperation signals an intent to move beyond the traditional ASEAN agenda of tariff reduction and dispute management toward the emerging technologies that are reshaping the region's economies. Singapore's credibility on this front is unassailable: its AI-driven export boom, record trade surpluses, and co-championing of DEFA position it as the bloc's most digitally advanced economy and a natural leader on digital governance. The "Deeper Integration" theme is substantive rather than ceremonial — Wong explicitly called for making it easier for businesses to operate across ASEAN borders, which points toward practical steps on mutual recognition of standards, cross-border digital payments, and labour mobility. The "Wider Partnerships" component acknowledges the geopolitical reality: with the 50th anniversaries of ties with the US, Canada, and EU all falling in 2027, Singapore will host a year-long series of engagements with Western partners at a time when US-China rivalry is intensifying. The transition from the Philippines — an economy in stagflation — to Singapore — an economy at the peak of an AI-driven boom — could not be starker, and Singapore's diplomatic weight will be tested on the intractable files it inherits: the Myanmar crisis, South China Sea tensions, and the question of how ASEAN navigates between great powers without fracturing.

What we don't know yet

Singapore's specific workplan, meeting calendar, and priority deliverables for 2027 beyond the broad themes. How Singapore will handle the DEFA implementation framework — whether it will push for binding timelines or allow flexibility. Whether the multiple anniversary summits will produce substantive agreements or remain ceremonial. How Singapore will address the Myanmar crisis, which has resisted ASEAN's consensus-based approach.

Philippine Peso Breaches 64 Per Dollar as Markets Brace for More BSP Rate Hikes Amid Stagflation

October–November 2026. Business World / PhilStar / Tribune / Manila Times.

Verified reporting

The Philippine peso sank past 64 per US dollar to successive record lows in late October and November 2026, with the currency hitting as low as 64.17 per dollar on 27 October — its weakest level in history. The peso has set more than 30 record lows since the beginning of 2026, making it one of the worst-performing currencies in Asia. The depreciation has been driven by a combination of persistent capital outflows, elevated US interest rates, and deteriorating domestic economic fundamentals.

The Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI), the country's oldest bank, urged the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) to continue tightening monetary policy, arguing that the central bank must prioritise fighting inflation while other government agencies address the growth shortfall. Term deposit rates ended higher as markets priced in additional BSP and US Federal Reserve rate hikes. The BSP raised its benchmark rate by 50 basis points to 5.50 per cent in October after a 25 basis point increase to 5.00 per cent in August. BSP Governor Eli Remolona has flagged that inflation could breach 8 per cent, up from 7.8 per cent in October.

The Philippine Stock Exchange Index (PSEi) has fallen to its lowest level since May 2020, reflecting a broad loss of investor confidence. GDP growth slowed to 2.1 per cent in Q3 — a 16-year low excluding the pandemic period — while unemployment rose to 5.4 per cent in September from 3.8 per cent a year earlier, leaving 2.86 million Filipinos without work. Barclays has warned that inflation could reach 8 per cent in November and projects another jumbo rate hike. Both S&P Global and Citigroup have flagged stagflation, with Citigroup's Asia economics chief Johanna Chua describing the economy's condition as "slowflation." Public infrastructure spending plunged 34.6 per cent from January to August as investigations into flood-control projects froze government capital outlays.

Our analysis

The Philippine peso's breach of 64 per dollar — setting record after record in a relentless decline — is the currency market's verdict on what is now the most severe economic deterioration in ASEAN. The peso's trajectory reflects every dimension of the Philippines' challenge: foreign investors are leaving (PSEi at 2020 lows), the central bank is tightening into a recession-like environment (rates at 5.50 per cent and rising), inflation is running nearly four times the target midpoint (7.8 per cent against a 2–4 per cent target), and the government's fiscal firepower has been paralysed by the collapse in infrastructure spending. The vicious cycle is now firmly established: peso weakness drives imported inflation higher, which forces BSP to hike rates, which further depresses growth and investment, which drives more capital outflows and further peso weakness. BPI's public call for continued tightening illustrates the impossible trade-off: the banking sector recognises that fighting inflation requires higher rates, even though those same rates are strangling an economy already growing at just 2.1 per cent. The comparison with regional peers is devastating. While Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand ride an AI-driven export boom, and Vietnam crosses the trillion-dollar trade milestone, the Philippines' domestic-demand-dependent economy has no access to the semiconductor supply chain windfall. The peso's decline, the stock market's collapse, and the growth slowdown are three manifestations of the same underlying problem: the Philippines has not developed the manufacturing and export base that would allow it to participate in the technology-driven growth cycle reshaping the rest of ASEAN. Until that structural gap is addressed — a project measured in years, not quarters — the peso and the PSEi will remain under pressure regardless of where BSP sets its benchmark rate.

What we don't know yet

Whether the BSP will deliver a standard 25 basis point hike or another jumbo 50 basis point increase at its 17 December meeting. Whether November inflation will breach 8 per cent as Barclays and BSP Governor Remolona have warned. How far the peso will fall before foreign exchange intervention or rate hikes stabilise it. Whether the government will announce specific measures to restart the frozen infrastructure spending pipeline before year-end.
