USTR Proposes 18–25% Tariffs on Malaysia Under Section 301 as Government Fights to Cap Duties at 19%

25–28 November 2026. US Trade Representative / Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry / The Malaysian Reserve / VNA / Vietnam+.

Verified reporting

The US Trade Representative has proposed tariffs of 18 to 25 per cent on Malaysian goods following the completion of Section 301 investigations covering 14 economies, according to a USTR announcement on 25 November. The investigations examined concerns about industrial expansion policies, preferential financing, labour protection regulations, and measures related to excess manufacturing capacity. The proposed rates are indicative, with final tariff levels expected to be announced in the first quarter of 2027.

Malaysia's Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) responded on 26 November, stating that the government will continue negotiating and consulting with the United States to ensure total tariffs on Malaysian goods do not exceed 19 per cent. MITI maintained that Malaysia's manufacturing sectors have no excess capacity that distorts or disrupts global markets, and that the country's exports complement US domestic production and support global supply chains. MITI also indicated it will review goods imported from third countries to ensure Malaysia does not contribute to global excess capacity.

Currently, only the 10 per cent tariff imposed on Malaysia on 24 July 2026 under a separate Section 301 measure is in effect. The United States and Malaysia signed the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (ART) on 26 October 2025, but MITI confirmed in February 2026 that the agreement has not yet been ratified due to new developments in the US. Written submissions on the proposed tariffs are due by 5 January 2027, with public hearings beginning 25 January 2027.

Economist Dr Geoffrey Williams, founder of Williams Business Consultancy, warned that Malaysia could face a harder trading position despite the proposed 18 per cent rate being slightly below the previously negotiated 19 per cent ceiling. He noted that the ART remains unratified, tariffs are largely unchanged from the interim level, and duties could ultimately reach 25 per cent. Williams observed that bilateral trade had reached record levels under the earlier arrangement, but Malaysia's trade surplus with the US widened — a dynamic that tends to attract scrutiny. He flagged semiconductors and the electrical and electronics industry, previously in a favourable position, as now vulnerable to higher duties.

Our analysis

The USTR's Section 301 proposals represent a significant escalation in trade pressure on Malaysia at a moment when the country's economic model is performing exceptionally well — Q3 GDP growth of 5.5 per cent, record trade figures, and booming E&E exports. The irony is that Malaysia's success in attracting AI-related manufacturing investment is simultaneously making it a larger trade target. The US$436.61 billion in monthly trade recorded in October, driven overwhelmingly by electronics exports, translates into a widening trade surplus with the United States that Section 301 investigations are designed to address. The 18 to 25 per cent range is a negotiating bracket, not a final determination, and the timeline — written submissions due in January, hearings in late January, final determination in Q1 2027 — provides a window for diplomatic resolution. Malaysia's position that its manufacturing operates on market principles and complements US production is strategically sound but faces headwinds. The US argument about excess capacity is not directed solely at Malaysia but at the broader phenomenon of Chinese manufacturing capacity being redirected through ASEAN countries — a concern that connects directly to the chip export controls story. The unratified ART is a significant complication. The agreement was intended to provide a framework for managing bilateral trade tensions, but its non-ratification leaves Malaysia without the institutional mechanism that was supposed to prevent precisely this escalation. Dr Williams's comparison with the UK is instructive: countries that responded to early US trade measures with proactive liberalisation — lowering their own tariffs and expanding investment access — fared better than those that adopted defensive positions. Malaysia's semiconductor and E&E sector, which accounts for the majority of exports to the US, is particularly exposed because it sits at the intersection of two US policy concerns: trade surplus reduction and AI chip supply chain control. The challenge for MITI is negotiating on both fronts simultaneously — tariff levels and chip export licensing — with a US administration that has demonstrated willingness to use economic leverage aggressively.

What we don't know yet

Whether the final tariff rate will land closer to 18 or 25 per cent, and what criteria will determine the level. The specific product categories that will face the highest duties, and whether semiconductors and E&E will be treated differently from other exports. Whether the ART can be ratified before the tariff determination is finalised, and what concessions Malaysia would need to offer to secure ratification. How the Section 301 tariff proposals interact with the separate AI chip export control regime — whether compliance on one front eases pressure on the other. The impact on Malaysia's FDI pipeline if potential investors perceive increased trade risk in serving the US market from Malaysian facilities. Whether other ASEAN countries named in the 14-economy investigation face similar or different tariff proposals, and whether a coordinated ASEAN response is feasible. The timeline for resolution — whether the Q1 2027 determination represents a firm deadline or a starting point for extended negotiations.

美国贸易代表依据301条款提议对马来西亚加征18–25%关税，政府力争将税率上限控制在19%

2026年11月25–28日。美国贸易代表办公室 / 投资贸易及工业部 / The Malaysian Reserve / 越通社 / Vietnam+。

经核实的报道

据美国贸易代表办公室11月25日公告，美国贸易代表在完成对14个经济体的301条款调查后，提议对马来西亚商品征收18%至25%的关税。调查审查了关于产业扩张政策、优惠融资、劳动保护法规和与产能过剩相关措施的担忧。提议税率为指示性的，最终关税水平预计于2027年第一季度公布。

马来西亚投资贸易及工业部（MITI）于11月26日回应，表示政府将继续与美国谈判磋商，确保对马来西亚商品的总关税不超过19%。MITI坚持认为马来西亚的制造业部门不存在扭曲或干扰全球市场的过剩产能，该国出口与美国国内生产互补并支持全球供应链。MITI还表示将审查从第三国进口的商品，以确保马来西亚不会加剧全球产能过剩。

目前，仅有2026年7月24日依据另一项301条款措施对马来西亚征收的10%关税生效。美马两国于2025年10月26日签署了对等贸易协定（ART），但MITI在2026年2月确认该协定因美国出现新情况尚未获批准。关于拟议关税的书面意见提交截止日为2027年1月5日，公开听证会于2027年1月25日开始。

经济学家、威廉姆斯商业咨询创始人杰弗里·威廉姆斯博士警告，尽管拟议的18%税率略低于此前谈判的19%上限，马来西亚可能面临更艰难的贸易地位。他指出ART仍未获批准，关税基本维持在临时水平不变，且税率最终可能达到25%。威廉姆斯观察到，在早期安排下双边贸易已达创纪录水平，但马来西亚对美贸易顺差扩大——这种动态往往会引发审视。他指出半导体和电子电气行业此前处于有利地位，现在面临更高关税的风险。

原创分析

美国贸易代表的301条款提议代表着在马来西亚经济模式表现卓越之际——第三季度GDP增长5.5%、贸易创纪录、电子电气出口繁荣——对贸易压力的显著升级。讽刺的是，马来西亚在吸引AI相关制造业投资方面的成功同时使其成为更大的贸易目标。10月录得的4,366.1亿令吉月度贸易额主要由电子出口驱动，转化为对美贸易顺差扩大，而这正是301条款调查旨在解决的问题。18%至25%的范围是谈判区间而非最终裁定，时间表——1月提交书面意见、1月下旬听证、2027年第一季度最终裁定——为外交解决提供了窗口。马来西亚关于其制造业按市场原则运营并与美国生产互补的立场在战略上是合理的，但面临逆风。美国关于产能过剩的论点并非仅针对马来西亚，而是针对中国制造产能通过东盟国家转移的更广泛现象——这一担忧与芯片出口管制的报道直接相关。未获批准的ART是重大复杂因素。该协定旨在为管理双边贸易紧张提供框架，但其未获批准使马来西亚缺乏本应防止这种升级的制度机制。威廉姆斯博士与英国的比较具有启示性：以主动自由化——降低自身关税和扩大投资准入——回应早期美国贸易措施的国家表现优于采取防御立场的国家。马来西亚的半导体和电子电气行业占对美出口的大部分，尤其暴露，因为它处于美国两项政策关切的交汇处：削减贸易顺差和AI芯片供应链管控。MITI面临的挑战是同时在两条战线——关税水平和芯片出口许可——与已展示出激进使用经济杠杆意愿的美国政府谈判。

未知事项

最终关税税率将更接近18%还是25%，以及什么标准将决定水平。将面临最高关税的具体产品类别，以及半导体和电子电气是否将获得与其他出口不同的待遇。ART能否在关税裁定最终确定前获得批准，以及马来西亚需要提供哪些让步以确保批准。301条款关税提议如何与单独的AI芯片出口管制制度互动——一条战线上的合规是否会减轻另一条战线的压力。如果潜在投资者认为从马来西亚设施服务美国市场的贸易风险增加，对马来西亚FDI管线的影响。在14经济体调查中被点名的其他东盟国家是否面临类似或不同的关税提议，以及协调的东盟应对是否可行。解决时间表——2027年第一季度的裁定是否代表确定的截止日期还是长期谈判的起点。

Bank Indonesia Raises Rate to 6% and Warns Global Uncertainty Could Challenge 2027 Growth Target as Rupiah Weakens 9.87% Year-to-Date

18–27 November 2026. Bank Indonesia / ANTARA News / RRI.

Verified reporting

Bank Indonesia raised the BI Rate by 25 basis points in November to 6 per cent, bringing cumulative rate hikes in 2026 to 125 basis points, as the central bank seeks to defend the rupiah amid intensifying global uncertainty. BI Governor Destry Damayanti announced the decision following the November Board of Governors Meeting on 18 November, stating that Indonesia's economic growth remains solid but needs further strengthening amid persistently weak global conditions.

As of 27 November, the rupiah had weakened approximately 9.87 per cent against the US dollar year-to-date based on BI's JISDOR rate, reflecting sustained capital outflow pressure and a strengthening US dollar. The rate hikes — which have taken the BI Rate from 4.75 per cent at the start of 2026 to 6 per cent — represent the most aggressive tightening cycle in the region outside the Philippines.

Speaking at the 2026 Bank Indonesia Annual Meeting on 27 November, Governor Destry warned that global economic uncertainty is likely to persist into 2027 even as global growth is expected to accelerate to 3.4 per cent. She identified five factors driving persistent uncertainty: unresolved geopolitical conflicts with no clear timeline; growing protectionist policies that could accelerate economic fragmentation and fuel inflation; rising public debt reducing governments' fiscal space; persistently high global interest rates strengthening the US dollar; and artificial intelligence developments that could add inflation pressure, drive up energy prices, and increase financial market volatility.

Indonesia's Q3 2026 GDP grew 5.47 per cent year-on-year, up from 5.29 per cent in Q2, driven mainly by domestic demand. The manufacturing PMI rose from 52.40 in September to 53.98 in October. BI projects Indonesia's 2026 economic growth at 4.9 to 5.7 per cent and has set the 2027 rupiah assumption at Rp17,800 to Rp18,200 per US dollar, with the rate at 6 per cent. The Indonesian government targets 6 per cent economic growth in 2027.

Our analysis

Bank Indonesia's November rate hike to 6 per cent and Governor Destry's unusually detailed warning about 2027 risks represent a significant shift in the central bank's communication posture. By listing five specific structural factors — geopolitical conflicts, protectionism, debt burdens, high global rates, and AI-driven inflation — Destry is effectively telling markets and the government that the external environment is fundamentally hostile to Indonesia's ambitious 6 per cent growth target. The 125 basis points of cumulative tightening in 2026 — from 4.75 per cent to 6 per cent — places Indonesia in an uncomfortable position. The rate hikes are primarily designed to defend the rupiah, which has weakened nearly 10 per cent year-to-date, rather than to control domestic inflation, which remains relatively contained. This creates a tension between currency stability and growth support. At 6 per cent, the BI Rate is now the highest since the 2018 tightening cycle, and the implied real interest rate is moderately positive — suggesting the monetary stance is restrictive relative to economic conditions. The rupiah's 9.87 per cent depreciation reflects Indonesia's vulnerability to global risk-off flows. As a large current account deficit economy dependent on commodity exports and portfolio capital inflows, Indonesia is structurally exposed to the strong-dollar, high-rates global environment that Destry described. The rate hikes have slowed but not reversed the depreciation, suggesting that interest rate differentials alone are insufficient to stabilise the currency when global risk appetite deteriorates. The 2027 growth target of 6 per cent looks increasingly challenging. While Q3 GDP growth of 5.47 per cent shows solid momentum, the manufacturing PMI of 53.98 is encouraging, and domestic demand remains supportive, achieving 6 per cent would require a step-up from current levels at a time when monetary policy is tightening and global headwinds are intensifying. Governor Destry's reference to AI-driven inflation as a risk factor is notable — it represents one of the first times a major central bank governor in ASEAN has explicitly flagged AI energy demand as a potential inflationary force.

What we don't know yet

Whether BI will pause rate hikes at 6 per cent or continue tightening into 2027 if the rupiah remains under pressure. The specific channels through which AI developments could add to Indonesian inflation — whether through energy demand, commodity prices, or imported equipment costs. Whether the government's 6 per cent growth target will be formally revised downward in light of BI's cautious outlook. The impact of 125 basis points of tightening on credit growth, business investment, and household consumption in Indonesia. Whether the rupiah assumption of Rp17,800 to Rp18,200 for 2027 is achievable or whether further depreciation is likely. How the Danantara sovereign wealth fund's investment plans interact with BI's monetary tightening — specifically whether Danantara's capital deployment could offset some of the contractionary effects. The scale of portfolio capital outflows from Indonesian assets in 2026 and the extent to which BI's rate hikes have been effective in attracting or retaining foreign investment.

印尼央行加息至6%并警告全球不确定性或挑战2027年增长目标，卢比年初至今贬值9.87%

2026年11月18–27日。印尼央行 / 安塔拉通讯社 / RRI。

经核实的报道

印尼央行11月将BI利率上调25个基点至6%，2026年累计加息125个基点，因央行在全球不确定性加剧之际寻求保卫卢比。BI行长戴丝特丽·达玛扬蒂在11月18日理事会会议后宣布这一决定，表示印尼经济增长保持稳固，但在全球状况持续疲弱之际需要进一步加强。

截至11月27日，根据BI的JISDOR汇率，卢比年初至今对美元贬值约9.87%，反映持续的资本流出压力和美元走强。加息——已将BI利率从2026年初的4.75%提高到6%——代表着除菲律宾外该地区最激进的紧缩周期。

戴丝特丽行长在11月27日举行的2026年印尼央行年会上警告，全球经济不确定性可能持续到2027年，即便全球增长预计将加速至3.4%。她列出了推动持续不确定性的五个因素：没有明确时间表的未解决地缘政治冲突；日益增长的保护主义政策可能加速经济碎片化并助长通胀；不断上升的公共债务减少政府财政空间；持续高企的全球利率加强美元；以及人工智能发展可能增加通胀压力、推高能源价格和加剧金融市场波动。

印尼2026年第三季度GDP同比增长5.47%，高于第二季度的5.29%，主要由国内需求驱动。制造业PMI从9月的52.40升至10月的53.98。BI预计印尼2026年经济增长4.9%至5.7%，并将2027年卢比假设设定为每美元17,800至18,200卢比，利率6%。印尼政府的2027年经济增长目标为6%。

原创分析

印尼央行11月加息至6%及戴丝特丽行长对2027年风险异常详细的警告，代表着央行沟通姿态的重大转变。通过列出五个具体结构性因素——地缘政治冲突、保护主义、债务负担、全球高利率和AI驱动的通胀——戴丝特丽实际上在告诉市场和政府，外部环境从根本上不利于印尼雄心勃勃的6%增长目标。2026年累计125个基点的紧缩——从4.75%到6%——使印尼处于不舒适的位置。加息主要旨在保卫年初至今贬值近10%的卢比，而非控制国内通胀，后者仍相对温和。这在货币稳定和增长支持之间制造了紧张关系。在6%的水平上，BI利率现在是2018年紧缩周期以来的最高水平，隐含的实际利率为适度正值——表明相对于经济状况货币立场是限制性的。卢比9.87%的贬值反映了印尼对全球避险资金流动的脆弱性。作为依赖大宗商品出口和组合资本流入的大型经常账户赤字经济体，印尼在戴丝特丽所描述的强美元、高利率全球环境中存在结构性风险。加息减缓但未逆转贬值，表明当全球风险偏好恶化时，仅利率差异不足以稳定货币。2027年6%的增长目标看起来越来越具有挑战性。虽然第三季度5.47%的GDP增长显示稳健势头，制造业PMI 53.98令人鼓舞，国内需求仍具支撑性，但在货币政策收紧和全球逆风加剧之际实现6%需要从当前水平提升。戴丝特丽行长将AI驱动的通胀作为风险因素提及，值得注意——这是东盟主要央行行长首次明确将AI能源需求标记为潜在通胀力量之一。

未知事项

BI是否将在6%暂停加息，还是在卢比继续承压时于2027年继续收紧。AI发展可能通过哪些具体渠道增加印尼通胀——是通过能源需求、大宗商品价格还是进口设备成本。鉴于BI的谨慎前景，政府6%的增长目标是否会被正式下调。125个基点的紧缩对印尼信贷增长、企业投资和家庭消费的影响。2027年卢比17,800至18,200的假设是否可实现，还是进一步贬值更可能。达南塔拉主权财富基金的投资计划如何与BI的货币紧缩互动——具体而言，达南塔拉的资本部署能否抵消部分紧缩效应。2026年从印尼资产流出的组合资本规模，以及BI加息在吸引或留住外资方面的效果。

Vietnam International Financial Centre Recognises First 10 Holding Companies with US$2.7 Billion Capital Mobilisation Target

28 October – 28 November 2026. VIFC-HCMC Executive Authority / The Asian Banker / Vietnam News / VnExpress / Vietnam+.

Verified reporting

The Executive Authority of the Vietnam International Financial Centre in Ho Chi Minh City (VIFC-HCMC) has recognised its first 10 holding companies as Members of the centre, with a combined registered charter capital of US$33.63 million and total capital expected to be mobilised and invested through 2027 of approximately US$2.7 billion, according to The Asian Banker. The recognition certificates were presented at the Autumn Economic Forum 2026 in the presence of Prime Minister Le Minh Hung and other senior officials.

The 10 founding Members span infrastructure, energy, fintech, logistics, and asset management. The largest individual capital mobilisation commitments include Saigon Marina IFC at approximately US$816 million for technology and physical infrastructure; AG&P Holding Vietnam (Singapore-based) at approximately US$425 million for the LNG-to-power-and-data-centre value chain with plans to scale to US$750 million to US$1 billion by 2030; EQuest Capital at US$325 million through a cooperation agreement with Standard Chartered Bank for education infrastructure; Son Kim Capital IFC at US$275 million as a self-managed investment company for VIFC infrastructure; and REE Energy Finance at US$200 million for renewable energy projects.

Keppel Asset Management (Vietnam) is studying co-investment platforms with global partners targeting capital mobilisation of up to US$1 billion, primarily from third-party capital. Other Members include VinaCapital International Partners with US$200 million for the 99-storey Iconic VIFC tower and a gas-fired power project, Gemadept Holdings with US$100 million for a maritime data system, IFC Vietnam Commodity Exchange with US$57.7 million to establish a commodity exchange, and Rovela Pay with an expected platform transaction value of approximately US$60 million for tourist payment services.

The recognition round covers holding companies only. Banks, securities companies, and investment funds will be considered in subsequent rounds once licensing regulations for conditional business lines are issued. Vietnam is pushing to fast-track the licensing of member banks, with the VIFC-HCMC expected to finalise and promulgate regulations on admission of investment funds in November 2026. The legal framework is built on National Assembly Resolution No. 222/2025/QH15 and Decree No. 323/2025/ND-CP.

Our analysis

The recognition of VIFC-HCMC's first 10 Members represents the moment when Vietnam's international financial centre transitions from legislation and infrastructure planning to operational reality. The numbers tell a story of ambition calibrated to Vietnam's position: US$33.63 million in registered charter capital against US$2.7 billion in projected capital mobilisation is a leverage ratio that relies heavily on the centre's ability to attract co-investment and serve as a capital-raising platform rather than a repository of paid-in capital. This is consistent with the VIFC's stated model — it aims to function as a conduit connecting domestic enterprises with global capital markets, not as a Dubai International Financial Centre-style regulatory enclave with its own legal system. The mix of founding Members reveals the centre's strategic priorities. Infrastructure and energy dominate the initial cohort — AG&P's LNG-to-power-and-data-centre chain, REE Energy's renewable projects, and Son Kim Capital's infrastructure focus reflect Vietnam's urgent infrastructure needs as the economy grows at nearly 10 per cent annually. The absence of major international banks in this initial recognition round is the most notable gap, and the authorities have acknowledged this by pushing to fast-track bank licensing regulations. Verified reporting

The SCB Economic Intelligence Center (SCB EIC) has warned that Thailand's economy faces a K-shaped recovery in which gains from AI-related exports and foreign investment are concentrated in specific industries and larger businesses, while domestic demand, SMEs, and lower-income households continue to lag, according to research published in November. SCB EIC projects Thailand's GDP growth at 2.2 per cent in 2026 and 2.1 per cent in 2027, below the country's growth potential.

Third-quarter GDP grew 2.9 per cent year-on-year, beating the previous quarter but missing analyst forecasts, according to the National Economic and Social Development Council (NESDC) data released on 16 November. The result placed Thailand fifth among six major ASEAN economies in Q3 growth — ahead only of the Philippines' 2.1 per cent, and far behind Vietnam's 9.95 per cent, Singapore's 6.1 per cent, Malaysia's 5.5 per cent, and Indonesia's 5.47 per cent.

The growth drivers are increasingly external and narrow. AI-related electronics exports, foreign direct investment in data centres and digital infrastructure, and a new investment cycle are supporting headline growth. However, SCB EIC finds these industries rely heavily on imported capital goods, raw materials, and intermediate products, so less value is added domestically. Links with Thai suppliers and workers remain relatively limited, meaning stronger exports and investment have not yet translated into broad-based domestic income, employment, and purchasing power improvement.

SMEs and domestic-demand businesses face weak income, falling profit margins, liquidity problems, tighter credit access, and intensifying competition, limiting their ability to invest in new technology. The Bank of Thailand policy rate stands at 1 per cent, with SCB EIC expecting it to remain there through the end of 2027. Financial conditions remain relatively tight, especially for households and SMEs with higher credit risk and weaker debt-servicing capacity. Lower-income households face slow income growth, high debt, and few financial buffers.

Our analysis

Thailand's K-shaped recovery is the clearest example in ASEAN of a structural challenge that affects the entire region to varying degrees: the AI investment cycle is generating impressive headline numbers while leaving large segments of the domestic economy behind. The 2.9 per cent Q3 GDP growth — ranking fifth in ASEAN, ahead only of the stagflation-trapped Philippines — is a stark contrast to the booming electronics export and FDI data that dominates business headlines. SCB EIC's finding that AI-related industries rely heavily on imported inputs and have limited linkages with Thai suppliers is a critical diagnosis. It means Thailand is capturing a share of the global AI supply chain but extracting relatively little domestic value from it. The multiplier effect that should theoretically flow from export growth and FDI to wages, consumption, and SME business activity is significantly attenuated. This is not unique to Thailand — similar dynamics exist in Malaysia and to some extent Vietnam — but Thailand's domestic economy is weaker than its peers', making the K-shape more pronounced. The comparison with ASEAN peers is instructive and unflattering. Vietnam is growing at nearly 10 per cent, Singapore at 6 per cent, Malaysia at 5.5 per cent, and Indonesia at 5.5 per cent. Thailand's 2.2 per cent forecast for 2026 is barely above the Philippines' stagflation-depressed level and roughly one-third of the ASEAN average. The policy rate at 1 per cent — the lowest in ASEAN — reflects the Bank of Thailand's acknowledgment that the economy is too fragile for normalisation, yet even this accommodative stance has not translated into credit flow for SMEs, which face tighter risk assessments from commercial banks. SCB EIC's warning that the K-shaped pattern could become a constraint on future growth is the central insight. If export and investment gains do not generate broader employment, income, and spending, the domestic economy becomes structurally dependent on external demand — making Thailand vulnerable to the very global shocks that Governor Destry described in Indonesia. The recommended solutions — using foreign investment to create domestic value through higher-value activities, strengthening links between foreign investors and Thai SMEs, and supporting struggling businesses and households — are strategically correct but require policy execution that has historically been challenging in Thailand's political environment.

What we don't know yet

新加坡副总理兼贸易部长颜金勇在11月27日新报业媒体亚洲未来峰会的主旨演讲中呼吁东盟以更深层次的一体化和更广泛的伙伴关系来对抗经济民族主义。颜金勇认为，人工智能、地缘政治冲击以及华盛顿与北京之间的地缘经济竞争使东盟一体化尤为紧迫。

颜金勇呼吁建立清晰可预测的规则，尊重法律和承诺，并对不当行为采取坚决行动以赢得伙伴信任。他提到了一项海关互认安排，将为受信任的贸易商在参与东盟国家中提供更快的通关和优先待遇。一项区域数字经济框架协定于2026年11月早些时候签署，正在进行升级东盟与韩国和加拿大贸易协定的谈判。

升级版东盟货物贸易协定（ATIGA）定于2026年底推出，代表着十多年来东盟内部贸易规则最重要的更新。升级涉及原产地规则、非关税措施和贸易便利化——这些领域的实施差距历来削弱了东盟关税自由化承诺的有效性。

颜金勇的演讲正值美国在此前两周宣布新的关税和芯片出口限制，影响多个东盟经济体。新加坡将在2027年担任东盟轮值主席国，恰逢东盟成立60周年，这使这个城市国家能够在日益紧张的贸易环境中塑造集团的应对。新加坡最近还将2026年GDP增长预测上调至约6%，受AI相关出口驱动，并将2026年非石油国内出口预测翻倍至30%至32%增长——凸显该城市国家在维持开放贸易流方面的直接利益。

原创分析

颜金勇深化东盟一体化的呼吁在时机上具有战略性，在实质上具有重要意义。随着新加坡在2027年担任东盟主席国，这次演讲起到了先行政策声明的作用——表明贸易一体化将是新加坡在东盟60周年之际主席国议程的核心。背景是快速恶化的贸易环境。美国已宣布覆盖14个经济体包括多个东盟成员国的301条款关税提议，将AI芯片出口管制扩展到东南亚，并且没有显示出缓和保护主义轨迹的迹象。对于东盟——一个将经济模式建立在开放贸易、供应链整合和出口导向增长基础上的地区——这代表着对数十年快速增长的发展模式的生存性挑战。升级版ATIGA是颜金勇发言中最具体的可交付成果。2010年实施的原ATIGA消除了99%的东盟内部货物贸易关税，但其有效性被非关税壁垒、复杂的原产地规则和不一致的实施所削弱。升级解决了这些实施差距，如果有效推出，可以降低东盟内部贸易成本，并通过使东盟生产商更容易从集团内部采购投入来部分抵消外部关税上升的影响。海关互认安排同样实用——为受信任贸易商提供更快通关和优先待遇，降低使东盟内部贸易在给定关税结构下竞争力不如应有水平的摩擦成本。11月早些时候签署的数字经济框架协定将一体化议程扩展到数字服务、电子商务和数据流——东盟监管碎片化一直是数字经济增长潜力障碍的领域。新加坡对这一议程的兴趣并非纯粹意识形态的。AI相关出口推动城市国家经济增长至6%，非石油国内出口预计增长30%至32%，新加坡在维持开放贸易流、可预测监管框架和深度东盟供应链整合方面有直接商业利益。挑战在于东盟一体化历来进展缓慢，基于共识的决策和主权敏感性限制了实施步伐。美国关税和芯片管制带来的外部压力是否会加速一体化——通过使碎片化成本更加明显——还是减缓一体化——通过制造个别国家优先于区域倡议的双边压力——是2027年东盟经济治理的决定性问题。

未知事项

升级版ATIGA的具体细节，包括将解决哪些非关税壁垒、如何简化原产地规则以及实施承诺的执行机制。海关互认安排是否将从启动时覆盖所有东盟成员还是在自愿参与者之间分阶段实施。新加坡2027年东盟主席国在贸易一体化之外的具体议程项目。东盟是否将对美国301条款关税提议制定协调应对，还是各国将进行双边谈判。与韩国和加拿大的东盟贸易协定升级的时间表和范围。数字经济框架协定是否包含关于跨境数据流的约束性承诺，这在采取不同监管方法的东盟成员之间一直是有争议的问题。升级版ATIGA如何与东盟与中国、日本、韩国、印度和澳新的现有自由贸易协定互动——具体而言东盟一体化是否可能无意中为这些外部协定制造复杂性。

BSP Flags Weak Export Performance Behind Widening Balance of Payments Deficit as External Finances Face Pressure Through 2027

26–28 November 2026. Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas / BusinessMirror / Inquirer / PhilStar / Sun.Star.

Verified reporting

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has identified weak export performance as the primary structural driver behind the Philippines' widening balance of payments deficit, according to BusinessMirror on 27 November. The central bank flagged that external finances will remain under pressure through 2027, with both the balance of payments (BOP) and current account deficits expected to widen further as global trade headwinds intensify.

The Philippines returned to a dollar deficit after a period of surplus, according to the Inquirer, marking the end of a streak of positive BOP positions. The BSP had earlier warned of a wider BOP deficit in 2026 and subsequently raised its deficit forecasts, flagging structural risks to the current account including the country's persistent merchandise trade deficit, declining competitiveness in manufacturing exports, and dependence on remittances and services exports to offset goods trade shortfalls.

The external balance deterioration compounds the broader economic challenges facing the Philippines. With Q3 GDP growth at 2.1 per cent — the weakest since the pandemic recovery — October inflation at 7.8 per cent, the BSP benchmark rate at 5.50 per cent after 125 basis points of hikes, and the PSEi down 28 per cent year-to-date, the Philippines faces simultaneous pressure on growth, prices, monetary policy, and external accounts. Philippine firms are reportedly deferring expansion plans amid political uncertainty, according to BusinessWorld, adding a business confidence dimension to the economic headwinds.

The BSP has flagged that the country's external finances are structurally vulnerable because the Philippines has not developed the manufacturing export base that has driven growth in Vietnam, Malaysia, and Thailand. While ASEAN peers are capturing shares of the global AI semiconductor supply chain — generating export surpluses and attracting manufacturing FDI — the Philippines' export profile remains concentrated in services, remittances, and a narrow band of electronics assembly operations that are increasingly uncompetitive against Vietnam's lower costs and larger scale.

Our analysis

The BSP's diagnosis of weak exports as the structural driver of the Philippines' external balance deterioration is both accurate and deeply consequential. It represents an implicit acknowledgment by the central bank that the country's economic model — historically built on remittances, domestic consumption, and services — faces a fundamental challenge in an era when ASEAN's growth is being driven by manufacturing export competitiveness, particularly in the AI semiconductor supply chain. The Philippines' merchandise trade deficit is structural, not cyclical. The country imports far more goods than it exports, and the gap has been widening as global supply chain reconfiguration benefits manufacturing-oriented ASEAN peers. Vietnam's exports rose 24.5 per cent through September; Malaysia's monthly trade hit record levels; Singapore's factory output surged 29.1 per cent. The Philippines has not participated meaningfully in any of these trends. The result is visible in the BOP data: the Philippines is the only major ASEAN economy running a balance of payments deficit, while peers are accumulating surpluses from manufacturing exports and FDI inflows. The connection to the broader stagflation narrative is direct. A widening BOP deficit puts structural downward pressure on the peso, which in turn feeds import-driven inflation — particularly for food and energy, which the Philippines imports extensively. This creates a vicious cycle: peso weakness drives inflation, which forces BSP to maintain high interest rates, which suppresses domestic demand and growth, which further weakens business confidence and investment, which limits the development of the export manufacturing base that could address the trade deficit. Breaking this cycle requires structural economic reform — developing manufacturing exports, attracting FDI into production facilities rather than just services, and improving infrastructure to reduce logistics costs. These are multi-year transformations that cannot be achieved through monetary policy alone. The comparison with Vietnam is particularly painful: two decades ago, the Philippines and Vietnam had comparable GDP per capita and similar economic structures. Vietnam's decision to prioritise manufacturing export development, infrastructure investment, and FDI attraction has produced 9.95 per cent Q3 growth and a massive trade surplus. The Philippines' different path has produced 2.1 per cent growth and a widening deficit. The gap is now so wide that it represents a generational divergence in ASEAN development trajectories.

What we don't know yet

The BSP's specific forecast for the BOP and current account deficits in 2027, and the assumptions driving those projections. Whether the Philippines will develop a targeted strategy to attract manufacturing FDI in sectors where it has potential competitive advantages — such as aerospace components, medical devices, or EV battery assembly. The impact of political uncertainty on business expansion plans — specifically whether the deferral of investment cited by BusinessWorld is temporary or reflects a longer-term reassessment of the Philippines' business environment. Whether remittance inflows — historically the Philippines' largest source of external income — will accelerate sufficiently to partially offset the merchandise trade deficit. The BSP's assessment of the peso outlook for 2027 and whether the central bank has sufficient reserves to manage orderly depreciation without triggering a currency crisis. Whether the Philippines can develop an AI-related manufacturing niche despite its late entry, or whether the semiconductor supply chain positions are now effectively locked in among Vietnam, Malaysia, and Thailand. The fiscal implications of the external deficit — specifically whether the government can maintain infrastructure spending commitments if external financing costs continue to rise.

菲央行指出出口疲弱导致国际收支逆差扩大，外部融资压力或持续至2027年

2026年11月26–28日。菲律宾中央银行 / BusinessMirror / 询问者报 / PhilStar / Sun.Star。

经核实的报道

据BusinessMirror 11月27日报道，菲律宾中央银行（BSP）将出口表现疲弱认定为菲律宾国际收支逆差扩大的主要结构性驱动因素。央行指出外部融资将在2027年之前持续承压，随着全球贸易逆风加剧，国际收支（BOP）和经常账户赤字预计将进一步扩大。

据询问者报报道，菲律宾在一段顺差期后重返美元逆差，标志着正BOP头寸连续期的结束。BSP此前已警告2026年BOP逆差将更大，随后上调了逆差预测，标记了经常账户的结构性风险，包括该国持续的商品贸易逆差、制造业出口竞争力下降，以及对汇款和服务出口抵消货物贸易缺口的依赖。

外部余额恶化加剧了菲律宾面临的更广泛经济挑战。第三季度GDP增长2.1%——疫情复苏以来最弱——10月通胀7.8%、BSP基准利率在加息125个基点后为5.50%、PSEi年初至今下跌28%，菲律宾在增长、物价、货币政策和外部账户方面同时面临压力。据BusinessWorld报道，菲律宾企业在政治不确定性下推迟扩张计划，为经济逆风增添了商业信心维度。

BSP指出该国外部融资在结构上是脆弱的，因为菲律宾没有发展出推动越南、马来西亚和泰国增长的制造业出口基础。当东盟同行正在获取全球AI半导体供应链份额——产生出口顺差并吸引制造业FDI——时，菲律宾的出口结构仍集中在服务、汇款和狭窄的电子组装业务中，后者在与越南更低成本和更大规模的竞争中日益缺乏竞争力。

原创分析

BSP将出口疲弱诊断为菲律宾外部余额恶化的结构性驱动因素，既准确又深具后果。这代表央行隐含承认该国的经济模式——历史上建立在汇款、国内消费和服务基础上——在东盟增长由制造业出口竞争力特别是AI半导体供应链驱动的时代面临根本性挑战。菲律宾的商品贸易逆差是结构性的而非周期性的。该国进口商品远多于出口，随着全球供应链重组使制造业导向的东盟同行受益，差距一直在扩大。越南截至9月出口增长24.5%；马来西亚月度贸易创纪录；新加坡工厂产出飙升29.1%。菲律宾未有意义地参与任何这些趋势。结果在BOP数据中可见：菲律宾是唯一运行国际收支逆差的主要东盟经济体，而同行正从制造业出口和FDI流入中积累顺差。与更广泛滞胀叙事的联系是直接的。BOP逆差扩大对比索产生结构性下行压力，进而加剧进口驱动的通胀——特别是菲律宾大量进口的食品和能源。这创造了恶性循环：比索走弱推动通胀，迫使BSP维持高利率，压制国内需求和增长，进一步削弱商业信心和投资，限制了可以解决贸易逆差的出口制造业基础的发展。打破这一循环需要结构性经济改革——发展制造业出口、吸引FDI投入生产设施而非仅服务、改善基础设施以降低物流成本。这些是多年转型，仅靠货币政策无法实现。与越南的比较尤其痛苦：二十年前菲律宾和越南人均GDP相当、经济结构类似。越南优先发展制造业出口、基础设施投资和FDI吸引的决定产生了第三季度9.95%的增长和巨额贸易顺差。菲律宾不同的路径产生了2.1%的增长和不断扩大的逆差。差距现在已经如此之大，代表着东盟发展轨迹的代际分化。

未知事项

BSP对2027年BOP和经常账户赤字的具体预测及驱动假设。菲律宾是否将制定有针对性的战略，在具有潜在竞争优势的行业——如航空航天零部件、医疗设备或电动车电池组装——吸引制造业FDI。政治不确定性对企业扩张计划的影响——具体而言BusinessWorld引用的投资推迟是暂时性的还是反映对菲律宾商业环境更长期的重新评估。汇款流入——历史上菲律宾最大的外部收入来源——是否会充分加速以部分抵消商品贸易逆差。BSP对2027年比索前景的评估，以及央行是否有足够外汇储备在不引发货币危机的情况下管理有序贬值。菲律宾能否在进入较晚的情况下发展AI相关制造业利基，还是半导体供应链位置现在已被越南、马来西亚和泰国有效锁定。外部赤字的财政影响——具体而言如果外部融资成本继续上升，政府能否维持基础设施支出承诺。
