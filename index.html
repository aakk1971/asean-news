MAS Launches Future of Finance Institute to Govern AI in Banking

3 November 2026. Monetary Authority of Singapore.

Verified reporting

The Monetary Authority of Singapore on 3 November formally established the Future of Finance Institute (FFI), a dedicated body tasked with governing the deployment of artificial intelligence across the city-state's financial sector. The institute will operate under three core pillars — PathFin.ai for industry roadmapping, BuildFin.ai for technology development, and SafeFin.ai for risk governance — each designed to ensure that Singapore's financial institutions adopt AI responsibly while maintaining competitive advantage.

MAS said the FFI will initially focus on agentic AI governance, addressing the rapid proliferation of autonomous AI systems that can execute multi-step financial transactions without continuous human oversight. The institute plans to issue its first set of guidelines for agentic AI deployment in banking by mid-2027, covering areas such as algorithmic accountability, model explainability requirements, and consumer protection safeguards for AI-driven advisory services.

The FFI is also expanding into tokenisation, working with banks and fintech firms to develop standards for tokenised deposits, digital securities, and cross-border payment rails. Singapore has positioned itself as a global hub for digital asset innovation, and the FFI's tokenisation workstream is expected to complement MAS's existing Project Guardian and Project Orchid initiatives.

Our analysis

The FFI represents Singapore's attempt to stay ahead of both innovation and regulation simultaneously — a difficult balance that few jurisdictions have managed. By creating dedicated pillars for development and safety, MAS avoids the common trap of regulation lagging technology by years. The agentic AI focus is particularly forward-looking; most regulators worldwide have not yet addressed autonomous AI systems in finance. This positions Singapore to export its governance framework regionally, potentially becoming the de facto standard for ASEAN financial AI regulation.

What we don't know yet

FFI's budget and staffing levels have not been disclosed. It is unclear how binding the institute's guidelines will be versus existing MAS regulations, and whether smaller fintech firms will face the same compliance burden as major banks.

新加坡金管局成立未来金融研究所，规范银行业AI应用

2026年11月3日。新加坡金融管理局。

经核实的报道

新加坡金融管理局于11月3日正式成立未来金融研究所（FFI），专门负责管理该城市国家金融领域的人工智能部署。该研究所将在三大支柱下运行——PathFin.ai负责行业路线规划、BuildFin.ai负责技术开发、SafeFin.ai负责风险治理——旨在确保新加坡金融机构在保持竞争优势的同时负责任地采用AI。

金管局表示，FFI将首先聚焦代理型AI治理，应对无需持续人工监督即可执行多步骤金融交易的自主AI系统的快速扩散。该研究所计划在2027年中发布首套银行业代理型AI部署指南，涵盖算法问责、模型可解释性要求以及AI驱动咨询服务的消费者保护措施等领域。

FFI还在向代币化领域扩展，与银行和金融科技公司合作制定代币化存款、数字证券和跨境支付通道的标准。新加坡已将自身定位为全球数字资产创新中心，FFI的代币化工作流预计将补充金管局现有的Guardian项目和Orchid项目。

原创分析

FFI代表了新加坡同时走在创新和监管前列的尝试——这是少数司法管辖区能够成功实现的艰难平衡。通过为开发和安全创建专门支柱，金管局避免了监管落后于技术数年的常见陷阱。对代理型AI的关注尤其具有前瞻性；全球大多数监管机构尚未在金融领域解决自主AI系统问题。这使新加坡有望在区域内输出其治理框架，可能成为东盟金融AI监管的事实标准。

未知事项

FFI的预算和人员配置尚未公开。该研究所指南的约束力与现有金管局法规相比如何尚不明确，较小的金融科技公司是否面临与大型银行相同的合规负担也未可知。

DBS Posts S$2.81 Billion Q3 Profit as Nine-Month Earnings Hit Record

7 November 2026. DBS Group Holdings.

Verified reporting

DBS Group Holdings reported third-quarter net profit of S$2.81 billion, down 5 per cent year-on-year as lower trading income and a normalisation of treasury markets offset continued strength in wealth management and fee income. Despite the quarterly dip, the bank's nine-month profit reached a record S$8.82 billion, up 2 per cent from the same period in 2025, underscoring the resilience of Southeast Asia's largest lender.

Total income for the first nine months stood at S$17.9 billion, supported by new highs in fee income and wealth management revenues. DBS's wealth management assets under management grew to over S$400 billion, cementing its position as the region's largest private bank. The bank attributed the fee income growth to strong demand for structured products and insurance referrals across its Greater China and Southeast Asian client base.

CEO Tan Su Shan, in her first quarterly results since taking the helm from Piyush Gupta in March 2026, struck a cautious tone on the outlook, citing geopolitical uncertainties, potential tariff disruptions to regional trade flows, and the impact of higher-for-longer US interest rates on borrowing costs across ASEAN economies. She indicated the bank would maintain its conservative provisioning approach while continuing to invest in digital banking expansion across Indonesia, India, and Greater China.

Our analysis

DBS's results confirm that ASEAN's top-tier banks remain highly profitable even as the interest rate cycle turns less favourable. The shift toward fee-based income — particularly wealth management — provides a crucial buffer against net interest margin compression. Tan Su Shan's cautious tone signals that DBS is preparing for a potentially more volatile 2027, but the record nine-month figure gives significant room to absorb credit deterioration if regional economies slow.

What we don't know yet

DBS did not provide specific guidance for Q4 2026 or full-year 2027. The pace of wealth management inflows from Greater China, which has been a key growth driver, could be affected by capital flow controls or geopolitical tensions.

原创分析

星展银行的业绩证实，即使利率周期变得不太有利，东盟顶级银行仍然保持高盈利能力。向以手续费为基础的收入——特别是财富管理——的转型为净息差压缩提供了关键缓冲。陈淑珊的谨慎态度表明星展正在为可能更加动荡的2027年做准备，但创纪录的九个月业绩为在区域经济放缓时吸收信用恶化提供了充足空间。

未知事项

星展未提供2026年第四季度或2027年全年的具体指引。来自大中华区的财富管理资金流入速度——这一直是关键增长驱动力——可能受到资本流动管控或地缘政治紧张局势的影响。

STI Sets All-Time High as Bank Rally Lifts Singapore Equities

November 2026. Singapore Exchange.

Verified reporting

The Straits Times Index reached an all-time intraday high of 5,339.59 during the third quarter of 2026, propelled by a broad-based rally in Singapore's three major banks. DBS shares rose to S$68.72, up 2.7 per cent, while OCBC advanced 3.4 per cent to S$26.37 and UOB gained ground at S$41.69. The banking sector's dominance of the index, accounting for roughly 45 per cent of total weighting, amplified the impact of strong quarterly earnings across all three lenders.

Citi Research maintained 'Buy' ratings on both DBS and OCBC, citing their superior return on equity and well-capitalised balance sheets relative to regional peers. The broker noted that Singapore bank loan growth of 8.7 per cent was outpacing deposit growth of 6.8 per cent, suggesting sustained credit demand from corporates expanding across ASEAN markets. The Monetary Authority of Singapore's S$6.5 billion Equity and Debt Programme (EQDP) has also contributed to improved liquidity and institutional participation in the local market.

Beyond banks, the rally was supported by gains in REITs and industrial stocks, with investors rotating into Singapore-listed assets perceived as defensive amid global uncertainty. Average daily trading volume on SGX increased 15 per cent quarter-on-quarter, while net institutional inflows into Singapore equities turned positive for the first time in three quarters.

Our analysis

The STI's record high reflects not just domestic banking strength but also Singapore's role as a regional safe haven during periods of geopolitical uncertainty. The loan-deposit growth gap is a double-edged sword — it supports net interest margins in the short term but could create funding pressures if deposit competition intensifies. The EQDP programme has been instrumental in reversing the trend of delistings and thin trading volumes that plagued SGX in prior years.

What we don't know yet

Whether the STI can sustain levels above 5,300 if global risk appetite deteriorates remains untested. The concentration risk from banking stocks dominating the index means any sector-specific headwind would disproportionately affect the benchmark.

Singapore Raises 2026 GDP Forecast to 2–4% on AI and Electronics Demand

February 2026 (revised). Ministry of Trade and Industry.

Verified reporting

Singapore's Ministry of Trade and Industry raised its 2026 GDP growth forecast to 2–4 per cent, up from the initial 1–3 per cent range, citing stronger-than-expected demand for semiconductors, AI-related services, and digital infrastructure. The upgrade followed a robust 2025, in which the economy expanded 5.0 per cent for the full year, with Q4 2025 registering 6.9 per cent year-on-year growth driven by information and communications, finance, and manufacturing sectors.

Enterprise Singapore correspondingly raised its forecast for non-oil domestic exports (NODX) to 2–4 per cent growth, reflecting sustained orders from global technology firms expanding their AI training and inference capacities. Barclays raised its own Singapore GDP estimate to 3.5 per cent, the most bullish among major investment banks, noting that the city-state's position at the nexus of semiconductor supply chains and financial services gives it outsized exposure to both structural trends.

The ministry cautioned that downside risks remain, including the potential for US tariff escalation targeting ASEAN manufactured goods, a sharper-than-expected slowdown in China's property sector feeding through to regional demand, and supply chain disruptions from any intensification of geopolitical tensions in the Taiwan Strait. Nonetheless, officials expressed confidence that Singapore's diversified economy and strong fiscal position would provide sufficient buffers.

Our analysis

The forecast upgrade underscores how AI has become a tangible growth driver for Singapore, moving beyond narrative to measurable economic impact. The 6.9% Q4 2025 figure was exceptionally strong and unlikely to be replicated, but a 3–3.5% full-year outcome for 2026 would still represent robust performance for a mature economy. Singapore's challenge will be managing the inflationary pressure that comes with being a magnet for global tech talent and data centre investment, which is already driving commercial rents higher.

What we don't know yet

The precise contribution of AI-related activity to GDP is not separately reported by MTI, making it difficult to assess how dependent the forecast upgrade is on this single trend. The impact of potential US tariffs on Singapore's re-export trade has not been quantified.

Singapore Blockchain Week Spotlights Cross-Border Digital Economy with Johor

November 2026. Blockchain Week organisers.

Verified reporting

Singapore Blockchain Week 2026, held in the first week of November, drew more than 8,000 participants from 45 countries, with the centrepiece event highlighting cross-border digital economy collaboration between Singapore and Malaysia's Johor state. Panels explored how blockchain-based settlement systems could streamline the daily flow of goods, services, and workforce movements across the Causeway, which handles over 300,000 crossings per day.

Key announcements included a pilot programme for blockchain-verified trade documents between Singapore Customs and the Royal Malaysian Customs Department, aiming to reduce clearance times for commercial shipments by up to 40 per cent. Several Singapore-based fintech firms also unveiled digital payment solutions designed to serve the growing number of Singaporeans living in Johor while working remotely or commuting to Singapore, a trend that has accelerated since Johor's Forest City and Iskandar Malaysia developments expanded.

The event also featured sessions on central bank digital currencies (CBDCs), with representatives from MAS and Bank Negara Malaysia discussing interoperability frameworks for their respective digital currency projects. Industry participants noted that the Singapore-Johor corridor offers a natural testing ground for cross-border CBDC settlement due to the high volume of bilateral transactions and the geographic proximity that allows rapid iteration.

Our analysis

The Singapore-Johor digital corridor is arguably ASEAN's most commercially viable cross-border blockchain use case, given the sheer volume of daily economic activity across the Causeway. If the customs pilot succeeds, it could become a template for similar bilateral arrangements elsewhere in ASEAN, particularly the Indonesia-Singapore and Thailand-Malaysia corridors. The CBDC interoperability discussion between MAS and BNM is also significant — successful bilateral implementation would likely inform the broader ASEAN-wide digital payment architecture.

What we don't know yet

The customs pilot's timeline and participating companies have not been named. Whether the pilot will handle only low-risk commercial goods or extend to regulated items remains unspecified. The regulatory framework for cross-border digital payments between Singapore and Johor is still under development.

The report also recommended strengthening the fiscal framework by establishing a formal fiscal rule linking government spending to a sustainable estimate of long-term oil revenue rather than current production volumes, and enhancing the transparency and governance of Brunei's sovereign wealth fund, the Brunei Investment Agency, which is estimated to manage assets of approximately US$40–60 billion but does not publish detailed financial statements.

Our analysis

AMRO's recommendations are economically sound but politically explosive in the Bruneian context. The social compact between the monarchy and citizens has historically been built on the absence of taxation in exchange for generous public services, subsidies, and government employment. Introducing a GST, however modest, would fundamentally alter this compact and could face significant public resistance. The sovereign wealth fund recommendation is equally sensitive — greater transparency would subject royal investment decisions to public scrutiny for the first time. The fiscal urgency is real, but the pace of reform will ultimately be determined by the sultan's assessment of political risk rather than economic optimal timing.

What we don't know yet

Whether the Brunei government will implement any of AMRO's recommendations, or treat the report as advisory, has not been indicated. The BIA's actual asset value and investment performance are not publicly disclosed. The timeline for any potential introduction of a GST or other new taxes has not been discussed publicly.

AMRO建议文莱通过税费改革拓宽收入基础

2026年。东盟+3宏观经济研究办公室。

原创分析

AMRO的建议在经济上合理但在文莱背景下具有政治爆炸性。君主制与公民之间的社会契约历来建立在以不征税换取慷慨公共服务、补贴和政府雇佣的基础上。引入GST无论多么温和，都将从根本上改变这一契约，可能面临巨大的公众阻力。主权财富基金建议同样敏感——更大的透明度将首次使皇室投资决策接受公众审查。财政紧迫性是真实的，但改革步伐最终将由苏丹对政治风险的评估而非经济最优时机决定。

未知事项

Brunei Economic Outlook 2026: ADB Sees Moderate Growth Amid Energy Transition Pressures

2026. Asian Development Bank / CSPS Brunei.

Verified reporting

The Asian Development Bank forecasts Brunei's GDP to grow approximately 2.5 per cent in 2026, a moderate pace that reflects the offsetting dynamics of declining crude oil production volumes and growing non-oil economic activity. Oil production has fallen approximately 15 per cent from its peak a decade ago as mature fields in the South China Sea enter natural decline, and new exploration has yielded limited discoveries. The ADB notes that Brunei's growth has been 'lumpy,' alternating between years of contraction when oil output drops and years of expansion when downstream processing ramps up.

The Centre for Strategic and Policy Studies (CSPS), Brunei's government-affiliated think tank, has published a complementary outlook report emphasising the need for accelerated implementation of the Wawasan 2035 national vision. CSPS identifies five priority areas for the next phase of economic development: scaling up the halal economy ecosystem, expanding the Hengyi petrochemical complex to full Phase 2 operations, developing Brunei as a regional data hosting destination, growing the Islamic finance sector, and attracting foreign talent in technology and professional services.

The energy transition poses both threats and opportunities for Brunei. On the threat side, the global shift away from fossil fuels reduces the long-term value of Brunei's remaining oil and gas reserves and could strand assets if the transition accelerates faster than expected. On the opportunity side, Brunei's substantial natural gas reserves position it as a transitional fuel supplier for ASEAN economies shifting from coal to cleaner alternatives, and the sultanate's geographic location and political stability make it an attractive site for carbon capture and storage projects associated with regional LNG operations.

Our analysis

Brunei's economic outlook is a study in managed decline of the dominant sector combined with uncertain growth of replacement sectors. The 2.5% GDP growth forecast, while positive, masks the reality that without oil and gas, the economy would still be far from self-sustaining. The five priority areas identified by CSPS are sensible but each faces significant execution challenges — the halal economy requires scale that Brunei's small domestic market cannot provide alone, the data centre play competes with much larger rivals in Malaysia and Singapore, and Islamic finance requires regulatory sophistication that is still being developed. The transition fuel opportunity is perhaps the most immediately actionable.

What we don't know yet

Brunei's proven oil and gas reserves data has not been comprehensively updated in public documents since 2023. Whether the government will pursue carbon capture and storage projects, and the associated regulatory framework, has not been formally announced. The Wawasan 2035 vision's intermediate milestones and progress metrics have not been published, making it difficult to assess whether the country is on track for its long-term development goals.

2026年。亚洲开发银行/文莱战略与政策研究中心。

亚洲开发银行预测文莱2026年GDP增长约2.5%，这一温和速度反映了原油产量下降和非石油经济活动增长的相互抵消动态。石油产量较十年前峰值下降了约15%，因为南海成熟油田进入自然衰退，新勘探发现有限。亚开行指出文莱的增长一直是'不规则的'，在石油产量下降时萎缩和下游加工增加时扩张的年份之间交替。
