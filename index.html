ASEAN Inclusive Growth Summit Opens in Manila with Mastercard, Google and OpenAI

11 November 2026. Mastercard Center for Inclusive Growth / Manila Bulletin / Philippine Star.

Verified reporting

The 2026 ASEAN Inclusive Growth Summit opened in Manila on 11 November, convening business leaders, government officials and civil society representatives under the theme "Resilient and Rising" ahead of the 49th ASEAN Summit scheduled for 15–17 November. The summit was organised by the Mastercard Center for Inclusive Growth in partnership with the ASEAN Business Advisory Council Philippines.

Featured speakers and attendees included Teresita Sy-Coson, Vice Chairperson of SM Investments Corporation; Jaime Augusto Zobel de Ayala, Chairman of Ayala Corporation; Nitin Gajria, Regional Managing Director of Google; Sandhya Devanathan, Regional Managing Director of OpenAI; Jane Sun, CEO of Trip.com Group; Kuok Hui Kwong, Chairman and CEO of Shangri-La Group; Jon Huntsman, Vice Chairman of Mastercard; and Joey Concepcion, Chairman of ASEAN-BAC Philippines. Actress and UNHCR Special Envoy Angelina Jolie and Filipino Broadway star Lea Salonga also addressed the gathering.

Agenda topics included digital transformation and AI in Southeast Asia, cybersecurity, financial health for micro, small and medium enterprises, sustainable tourism, the creator economy, and regional economic integration. Officials highlighted that ASEAN's 700 million people and 70 million MSMEs represent one of the world's largest untapped digital markets.

Our analysis

The corporate lineup — spanning Mastercard, Google, OpenAI, Shangri-La, Trip.com and ASEAN's two largest conglomerates — signals that ASEAN's positioning in the global technology governance conversation has moved beyond aspiration to active engagement. The presence of OpenAI's regional head alongside Google's suggests ASEAN is becoming a contested market for AI platform deployment. The challenge is translating summit declarations into on-the-ground impact for the 70 million MSMEs that account for over 90 per cent of ASEAN businesses.

What we don't know yet

No specific commercial agreements or investment commitments were announced at the summit's opening. Whether the corporate participants will make binding pledges related to DEFA implementation is unclear.

IHSG Slips to 5,115 as Markets Brace for MSCI November Rebalancing Announcement

11 November 2026. CNBC Indonesia / Okezone / Bisnis Indonesia.

Verified reporting

The Jakarta Composite Index (IHSG) closed at 5,115 on 11 November, down 10.70 points or 0.21 per cent, as investors adopted a cautious stance ahead of MSCI's semi-annual index rebalancing announcement scheduled for 12 November. The index opened higher at 5,139.09 and touched an intraday high of 5,144.79 before selling pressure pushed it to a low of 5,086.02. Total market capitalisation stood at Rp8,785 trillion.

Of 945 stocks traded, 446 declined, 218 advanced, and 281 were unchanged. Basic materials led sectoral declines with a 1.86 per cent drop. The previous session had seen the IHSG rally 1.02 per cent with foreign net purchases of Rp171.89 billion. However, major stocks continued to see foreign selling, led by Barito Pacific, Telkom Indonesia, and Barito Renewables Energy.

Our analysis

The IHSG's inability to hold gains above 5,125 underscores deep uncertainty ahead of the MSCI announcement. The rebalancing is distinct from the broader reclassification consultation but the two are intertwined: further removals would reinforce the narrative that Indonesia's market is shrinking within global indices. With foreign net selling exceeding Rp98 trillion year-to-date and the IHSG down 45 per cent from its January peak, Indonesia faces sustained institutional pressure rarely seen in ASEAN.

What we don't know yet

The specific additions and deletions in MSCI's November rebalancing will not be known until 12 November. Whether the results will trigger additional passive fund outflows beyond those already priced in is uncertain.

World Bank Calls for Decisive Reforms as Malaysia's Economy Stays Resilient

11 November 2026. World Bank / Malay Mail / The Star.

Verified reporting

The World Bank launched its October 2026 Malaysia Economic Monitor on 11 November, titled "From Quantity to Quality: Financing Malaysia's Productive SMEs," warning that Malaysia must deepen structural reforms to sustain its trajectory toward high-income status. Zafer Mustafaoglu, the World Bank's division director, said Malaysia's economy remained positive but faces significant external headwinds.

The report's central finding is that Malaysia's SME sector must shift financing from basic working capital toward higher-risk expansion capital — including funding for automation, digitalisation, AI adoption, and green transitions. Separately, HSBC has upgraded Malaysia's 2026 GDP growth forecast to 5.5 per cent.

Our analysis

The World Bank's framing of reform as "not optional" carries weight given Malaysia's 5.3 per cent growth with 1.8 per cent inflation. The focus on SME financing quality is a subtle but important shift: available financing is overwhelmingly short-term working capital rather than the patient capital needed for technology adoption. Malaysia's high-income target by 2028 depends on productivity gains from innovation — areas where SMEs employing 60 per cent of the workforce are lagging.

What we don't know yet

The report's specific GDP forecasts have not been disclosed. Whether the SME financing recommendations will influence Malaysia's 2027 budget is unclear.

Vietnam's GDP Surges 9.95% in Q3 2026 — Fastest Pace Since 2022

October 2026. General Statistics Office of Vietnam / VietnamNet / Trading Economics.

Verified reporting

Vietnam's economy grew an estimated 9.95 per cent year-on-year in Q3 2026, accelerating from 8.81 per cent in Q2 and 8.15 per cent in Q1. This is the fastest quarterly growth since 2022, with the nine-month average at 9.01 per cent. Industry and construction led at 12.50 per cent growth. Exports surged 23.27 per cent while gross capital formation rose 21.39 per cent.

Twelve of thirty-four provinces recorded growth of at least 10 per cent, led by Quang Ninh (12.54%), Ha Tinh (12.36%), and Hai Phong (12.08%). UOB raised its full-year forecast to 8.5 per cent.

Our analysis

Vietnam's 9.95 per cent growth is in rarefied territory — roughly four times Thailand's rate. The acceleration is driven by the manufacturing export cycle reaching full capacity. The geographic concentration in northern industrial provinces maps onto the Samsung-LG corridor. The government's double-digit annual target would require Q4 growth above 12 per cent — ambitious but possible if export momentum holds.

What we don't know yet

Whether Q4 can accelerate sufficiently for double-digit annual growth has not been assessed. The impact of Section 301 duties on export volumes has not been separately quantified.

Vietnam FDI Inflows Hit Highest Level Since 2009 as 10-Month Total Reaches US$54.85 Billion

November 2026. Vietnam Investment Review / VOV / Asia News Network.

Verified reporting

FDI inflows reached US$54.85 billion in the first ten months, up 74 per cent year-on-year — the highest since 2009. Manufacturing dominated at US$14.61 billion (47.5%). Singapore led by source at US$9.52 billion (31%), followed by South Korea, Luxembourg, Hong Kong, China and Japan.

Disbursed FDI reached US$24.08 billion, up 13.1 per cent — the highest ten-month figure in five years. Manufacturing absorbed 82.9 per cent of disbursed capital.

Our analysis

The 74 per cent surge reflects Vietnam's positioning in supply chain reconfiguration. Singapore's dominance as conduit, South Korea's Samsung-LG ecosystem, and Luxembourg's private equity flows each tell different stories. The US$24.08 billion disbursed figure is the most important — it measures actual deployment. Vietnam's challenge is converting registrations into factories, jobs and technology transfer in higher-value segments.

What we don't know yet

The sectoral breakdown of disbursed FDI has not been fully released. Whether Luxembourg-sourced investments represent new productive capacity or financial restructuring is unclear.

ASEAN Set to Sign Landmark Digital Economy Framework Agreement at Manila Summit

November 2026. ASEAN Secretariat / Rajah & Tann Asia / ASEAN Briefing.

Verified reporting

The ten ASEAN member states are expected to sign DEFA during the 49th ASEAN Summit on 15–17 November — the world's first binding regional digital economy agreement covering data flows, digital payments, e-commerce, cybersecurity, and AI governance for 670 million people. Negotiations were completed in October 2026.

DEFA's scope extends to digital identity, consumer protection, competition policy for platforms, and emerging technologies. Ratification typically takes twelve to twenty-four months across ASEAN.

Our analysis

DEFA would be a genuine milestone — no other regional bloc has achieved a binding digital economy framework of this scope. The challenge is harmonising rules across ten nations with vastly different digital development levels. Data localisation requirements in Vietnam and Indonesia conflict with DEFA's free-flow principles. The ratification timeline means substantive impact arrives in 2028, by which time AI governance provisions may need updating.

What we don't know yet

The exact signing date and whether all ten states will sign simultaneously are unconfirmed. Enforcement mechanisms have not been publicly detailed.

IDX Removes 82 Stocks from IHSG in Sweeping Reform to Address MSCI Concerns

2 November 2026. Indonesia Business Post / DealStreet Asia / The Star (MY).

Verified reporting

IDX removed 82 high-shareholding-concentration stocks from the IHSG effective 2 November, reducing constituents from 963 to 881. The excluded stocks accounted for approximately 18 per cent of weighting and 30 per cent of total market capitalisation. Notable removals include DCI Indonesia (Rp417 trillion), Bayan Resources (Rp305 trillion), and Barito Renewables Energy (Rp240 trillion).

The removal was handled through reweighting rather than a level reset, with the IHSG's starting level unchanged at 5,057.

Our analysis

This is the most aggressive single-day compositional reform in IHSG history. By excising stocks with insufficient free float, IDX is attempting to demonstrate market integrity to MSCI. However, the excluded stocks' 30 per cent market cap share means the index now represents a smaller slice of Indonesia's equity universe. The timing — ten days before the MSCI rebalancing — suggests a last-minute reform case.

What we don't know yet

Whether MSCI will recognise the HSC removal as sufficient reform is unknown. Reinstatement criteria have not been publicly specified.

MSCI是否认可此举为充分改革尚不可知。恢复标准未公开明确。
