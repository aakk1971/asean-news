Vietnam Trade Turnover Tops US$1 Trillion for First Time as Exports Surge 27.5% in Ten Months

1–24 November 2026. General Statistics Office / Ministry of Industry and Trade / Vietnam Briefing / Xinhua / VietnamPlus.

Verified reporting

Vietnam's total merchandise trade turnover reached US$1,010.55 billion in the first ten months of 2026, surpassing US$1 trillion for the first time in the country's history and representing a 32.3 per cent year-on-year increase. Exports totalled US$499.24 billion, up 27.5 per cent, while imports reached US$511.31 billion, up 37.4 per cent, leaving a cumulative ten-month trade deficit of US$12.07 billion.

Foreign-invested enterprises dominated the export picture, accounting for US$405.31 billion or 81.2 per cent of total exports, up 33 per cent year-on-year. Domestic enterprises contributed US$93.93 billion, or 18.8 per cent, growing at a slower 8.2 per cent. Processed industrial goods represented 90.7 per cent of export value at US$452.74 billion. Thirty-seven product categories each exceeded US$1 billion in exports, together accounting for 94.9 per cent of the total, with eight categories each exceeding US$10 billion and together representing 73.4 per cent.

The bilateral trade balance reveals Vietnam's strategic position in global supply chains. Vietnam recorded a US$140.10 billion trade surplus with the United States — its largest by a wide margin — and a US$40.84 billion surplus with the European Union. These surpluses were offset by significant deficits with manufacturing input suppliers: US$134.94 billion with China, US$48.63 billion with South Korea, and US$16.93 billion with ASEAN partners, reflecting Vietnam's role as an intermediate processing hub that imports components from Northeast Asia and exports finished goods to Western markets.

October 2026 alone saw exports of US$64.67 billion and imports of US$57.36 billion, producing a monthly surplus of US$7.31 billion — a sharp improvement from September's US$1.30 billion surplus and the second consecutive monthly surplus. Production goods accounted for 94.1 per cent of imports, underscoring the manufacturing-driven nature of import demand.

Our analysis

Vietnam crossing the US$1 trillion trade threshold is a symbolic milestone with genuine economic substance. It places Vietnam — a country with a GDP of approximately US$460 billion — in the same trade-volume league as economies several times its size, and confirms its transformation from a low-cost agricultural exporter into a major node in global manufacturing supply chains. The trade-to-GDP ratio now exceeds 200 per cent, making Vietnam one of the most trade-dependent economies in the world alongside Singapore and Hong Kong. The composition of trade reveals both strengths and vulnerabilities. The 81.2 per cent share of exports from foreign-invested enterprises means that the trillion-dollar trade achievement is overwhelmingly driven by multinationals — Samsung, Foxconn, LG, Intel — using Vietnam as a production base. Domestic firms contribute less than a fifth of exports and are growing at a quarter of the rate of foreign firms, indicating that the technology transfer and supply chain integration that Vietnam's FDI strategy was designed to generate has been slow to materialise. The US$140.10 billion surplus with the United States is the most politically sensitive figure in the entire trade dataset. It is now larger than the US trade deficit with Japan and approaching the scale of the US deficit with the European Union. This surplus makes Vietnam a potential target for US trade enforcement actions, particularly scrutiny of Chinese-origin goods being transshipped through Vietnam to circumvent US tariffs. The US Section 301 duty of 12.5 per cent on Vietnamese-origin products that took effect in July 2026 is a direct consequence of this dynamic. The cumulative ten-month trade deficit of US$12.07 billion — driven by a 37.4 per cent surge in imports that outpaced the 27.5 per cent export growth — reflects the import intensity of Vietnam's manufacturing model. As Vietnam moves up the value chain into semiconductor packaging and testing, the import bill for capital equipment and advanced components will continue to grow before domestic capability catches up. This is a structural feature of the development model, not a warning sign, but it does mean that Vietnam's overall trade balance is likely to remain in deficit or near balance even as bilateral surpluses with Western markets widen further.

What we don't know yet

Whether the US$140.10 billion bilateral surplus with the United States will trigger additional trade enforcement actions beyond the existing Section 301 duty. How much of the trade routed through Vietnam originates from Chinese manufacturers using Vietnamese assembly to access lower US tariff rates, and whether US Customs and Border Protection will increase enforcement of origin rules. Whether Vietnam's domestic enterprises can close the growth gap with foreign-invested firms, or whether the 81-19 split becomes entrenched. The impact of the R&I investment-grade rating on Vietnam's ability to finance the infrastructure investment needed to sustain the trade expansion, and whether Moody's, S&P, or Fitch will follow. Whether the October trade surplus of US$7.31 billion marks the beginning of a rebalancing or is a one-off improvement driven by seasonal factors.

越南贸易额首次突破1万亿美元，前十月出口飙升27.5%

2026年11月1–24日。统计总局 / 工贸部 / Vietnam Briefing / 新华社 / VietnamPlus。

经核实的报道

越南2026年前十个月商品贸易总额达10,105.5亿美元，历史上首次突破1万亿美元大关，同比增长32.3%。出口总额4,992.4亿美元，增长27.5%；进口5,113.1亿美元，增长37.4%，前十月累计贸易逆差120.7亿美元。

外商投资企业在出口中占据主导地位，出口额达4,053.1亿美元，占出口总额的81.2%，同比增长33%。国内企业贡献939.3亿美元，占18.8%，增速较慢为8.2%。加工工业品占出口总值的90.7%，达4,527.4亿美元。37个产品类别出口额各超过10亿美元，合计占总额的94.9%，其中8个类别各超过100亿美元，合计占73.4%。

双边贸易差额揭示了越南在全球供应链中的战略地位。越南对美国录得1,401亿美元贸易顺差——遥遥领先的最大顺差——对欧盟顺差408.4亿美元。这些顺差被来自制造业投入品供应国的巨大逆差所抵消：对中国逆差1,349.4亿美元，对韩国逆差486.3亿美元，对东盟伙伴逆差169.3亿美元，反映了越南作为中间加工枢纽的角色——从东北亚进口零部件，向西方市场出口成品。

仅2026年10月，出口646.7亿美元，进口573.6亿美元，月度顺差73.1亿美元——较9月的13亿美元顺差大幅改善，为连续第二个月实现顺差。生产资料占进口的94.1%，凸显了进口需求的制造业驱动本质。

原创分析

越南跨越1万亿美元贸易门槛是一个具有真正经济实质的象征性里程碑。它使越南——一个GDP约4,600亿美元的国家——进入了与数倍于其规模的经济体同级别的贸易量联盟，并确认了其从低成本农产品出口国向全球制造业供应链重要节点的转型。贸易占GDP比率现已超过200%，使越南与新加坡和香港一样成为世界上最依赖贸易的经济体之一。贸易构成既揭示了优势也揭示了脆弱性。外商投资企业占出口的81.2%意味着万亿美元贸易成就绝大部分由跨国公司驱动——三星、富士康、LG、英特尔——将越南作为生产基地。国内企业贡献不到五分之一的出口，增速仅为外企的四分之一，表明越南FDI战略旨在产生的技术转让和供应链整合进展缓慢。对美国1,401亿美元的顺差是整个贸易数据集中政治上最敏感的数字。它现在已超过美国对日本的贸易逆差，接近美国对欧盟逆差的规模。这一顺差使越南成为美国贸易执法行动的潜在目标，尤其是对中国原产商品通过越南转运以规避美国关税的审查。2026年7月生效的对越南原产产品12.5%的第301条关税正是这一动态的直接后果。前十月累计贸易逆差120.7亿美元——由37.4%的进口飙升超过27.5%的出口增长所驱动——反映了越南制造业模式的进口密集性。随着越南向半导体封装测试等价值链上游移动，资本设备和先进零部件的进口账单将在国内能力赶上之前继续增长。这是发展模式的结构性特征而非警示信号，但这确实意味着即使与西方市场的双边顺差进一步扩大，越南的整体贸易差额可能仍将维持逆差或接近平衡。

未知事项

对美国1,401亿美元的双边顺差是否会触发现有第301条关税之外的额外贸易执法行动。通过越南转运的贸易中有多少来自利用越南组装以获得较低美国关税的中国制造商，以及美国海关和边境保护局是否会加强原产地规则执法。越南国内企业能否缩小与外商投资企业的增长差距，还是81比19的分化将固化。R&I投资级评级对越南为维持贸易扩张所需的基础设施投资融资能力的影响，以及穆迪、标普或惠誉是否会跟进。10月73.1亿美元的贸易顺差标志着再平衡的开始还是季节性因素驱动的一次性改善。

Singapore October NODX Surges 79.8% as Trade Surplus Balloons to SG$19.3 Billion on AI and Pharma Demand

16–24 November 2026. Enterprise Singapore / MT Newswires / AsiaOne / Bloomberg.

Verified reporting

Singapore's non-oil domestic exports (NODX) surged 79.8 per cent year-on-year in October 2026, accelerating from 68.3 per cent growth in September, according to data released by Enterprise Singapore. The merchandise trade surplus ballooned to SG$19.3 billion — more than double September's SG$9.03 billion and far exceeding the Trading Economics forecast of SG$7.90 billion.

Non-electronic exports rose 81.9 per cent, driven by extraordinary jumps in pharmaceuticals, which soared 253.1 per cent, and ships and boats structures, which Enterprise Singapore attributed partly to low base effects from the prior year. Electronic exports grew 73.9 per cent, with AI-related demand continuing to drive the category: disk media products surged 189.6 per cent to approximately SG$1.1 billion, personal computers rose 81 per cent to approximately SG$700 million, and integrated circuits increased 53.1 per cent.

Total merchandise trade expanded 50.7 per cent year-on-year in October, broadly stable from 50.8 per cent in September. Total exports grew 55.8 per cent while imports increased 44.9 per cent. Non-oil re-exports (NORX) rose 48.1 per cent, with electronics re-exports up 69.1 per cent and non-electronic re-exports up 15 per cent. For the first ten months of 2026, cumulative NODX growth stood at 33.6 per cent, up from an average of 27.6 per cent through the first nine months. The United States and China were Singapore's top NODX markets in October.

Our analysis

Singapore's October trade data is extraordinary by any standard — a 79.8 per cent NODX surge and a SG$19.3 billion trade surplus are numbers that would be remarkable for a full quarter, let alone a single month. The pharmaceutical contribution — a 253.1 per cent increase — is the primary driver of the non-electronic outperformance and reflects the lumpy, batch-driven nature of pharmaceutical manufacturing in Singapore. Large shipments of active pharmaceutical ingredients and finished drugs can produce dramatic single-month spikes that do not necessarily indicate a sustained acceleration. The ships and boats category carries a similar caveat: Enterprise Singapore itself noted the low base effect. What is more structurally significant is the electronics performance. The 73.9 per cent growth in electronic NODX — with disk media products up 189.6 per cent and personal computers up 81 per cent — reflects genuine demand related to the global AI infrastructure buildout. Singapore's semiconductor and data storage manufacturing benefits from the same tailwinds driving Malaysia's electronics sector, but at a higher point in the value chain. The cumulative NODX growth of 33.6 per cent for the first ten months — accelerating from 27.6 per cent through September — suggests that the momentum is building rather than fading. The SG$19.3 billion surplus is structurally significant because it reflects Singapore's position as a net value-added exporter: it imports raw materials and components, adds high-value processing in pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, and precision engineering, and exports finished products at substantially higher prices. The 44.9 per cent import growth supports this interpretation — imports are rising because manufacturing output is rising, not because of consumption-driven demand. The key risk is sustainability: pharmaceutical shipments are inherently volatile, and the AI-driven electronics demand could moderate if hyperscale data centre investment slows. But the breadth of the export expansion — across electronics, pharmaceuticals, and specialised machinery — suggests that Singapore's export engine is operating on multiple cylinders, reducing the risk of a single-sector slowdown producing a sharp correction.

What we don't know yet

Whether the pharmaceutical surge is a one-month spike driven by large batch shipments or indicates sustained demand from new production capacity coming online. The composition of the SG$19.3 billion surplus — how much reflects genuine value-added versus re-export intermediation that inflates gross trade figures. Whether the AI-related demand driving disk media and personal computer exports will sustain into 2027 or whether the current pace reflects front-loading of data centre equipment orders. The impact of US-China technology restrictions on Singapore's role as an intermediary in semiconductor supply chains. How the top NODX markets — the United States and China — are likely to perform in 2027, and whether a US recession or Chinese slowdown would reduce demand for Singapore's exports.

新加坡10月非石油国内出口飙升79.8%，贸易顺差在AI和制药需求推动下膨胀至193亿新元

2026年11月16–24日。新加坡企业发展局 / MT Newswires / AsiaOne / 彭博社。

经核实的报道

根据新加坡企业发展局发布的数据，新加坡2026年10月非石油国内出口（NODX）同比飙升79.8%，较9月的68.3%增长进一步加速。商品贸易顺差膨胀至193亿新元——是9月90.3亿新元的两倍多，远超Trading Economics预测的79亿新元。

非电子产品出口增长81.9%，受制药产品253.1%的惊人增长和船舶及船舶结构的推动——新加坡企业发展局将后者部分归因于上年的低基数效应。电子产品出口增长73.9%，AI相关需求持续驱动该类别：磁盘媒体产品飙升189.6%至约11亿新元，个人电脑增长81%至约7亿新元，集成电路增长53.1%。

10月商品贸易总额同比增长50.7%，与9月的50.8%基本持平。总出口增长55.8%，进口增长44.9%。非石油再出口（NORX）增长48.1%，其中电子产品再出口增长69.1%，非电子产品再出口增长15%。2026年前十个月，NODX累计增长33.6%，高于前九个月27.6%的平均水平。美国和中国是10月新加坡最大的NODX市场。

原创分析

新加坡10月贸易数据以任何标准衡量都非同寻常——79.8%的NODX飙升和193亿新元的贸易顺差，即使放在整个季度来看也令人瞩目，更何况是单月数据。制药贡献——253.1%的增长——是非电子产品优异表现的主要驱动力，反映了新加坡制药制造业的批次驱动特性。大批量活性药物成分和成品药物的出货可能产生戏剧性的单月峰值，不一定表示持续加速。船舶和船艇类别也有类似的注意事项：新加坡企业发展局本身就指出了低基数效应。更具结构性意义的是电子产品表现。电子NODX 73.9%的增长——磁盘媒体产品增长189.6%、个人电脑增长81%——反映了与全球AI基础设施建设相关的真实需求。新加坡的半导体和数据存储制造受益于驱动马来西亚电子行业的同一顺风，但处于价值链更高端。前十个月NODX累计增长33.6%——从9月的27.6%加速——表明势头正在增强而非消退。193亿新元的顺差具有结构性意义，因为它反映了新加坡作为净增值出口国的地位：进口原材料和零部件，在制药、半导体和精密工程领域进行高附加值加工，并以大幅溢价出口成品。44.9%的进口增长支持这一解读——进口增长因为制造业产出增长，而非消费驱动的需求。关键风险是可持续性：制药出货本质上波动性强，如果超大规模数据中心投资放缓，AI驱动的电子需求可能减弱。但出口扩张的广度——跨电子、制药和专用机械——表明新加坡的出口引擎正在多个气缸上运转，降低了单一行业放缓导致急剧回调的风险。

未知事项

制药飙升是由大批量出货驱动的单月峰值还是表明新产能投产带来的持续需求。193亿新元顺差的构成——有多少反映了真正的增值，有多少是膨胀总贸易数字的再出口中介。驱动磁盘媒体和个人电脑出口的AI相关需求能否延续至2027年，还是当前速度反映了数据中心设备订单的前置。美中技术限制对新加坡作为半导体供应链中介角色的影响。最大NODX市场——美国和中国——2027年的可能表现，以及美国衰退或中国放缓是否会减少对新加坡出口的需求。

INDONESIA · INVESTMENT / 印尼 · 投资

Prabowo Witnesses US$71.8 Billion in Strategic MoUs with China Covering Smart Cities, Manufacturing, and Infrastructure

18–24 November 2026. Cabinet Secretariat of Indonesia / ANTARA / Fortune / Observer ID.

Verified reporting

President Prabowo Subianto witnessed the exchange of memoranda of understanding and investment commitments worth approximately US$71.8 billion — equivalent to more than Rp1,300 trillion — with Chinese partners in Shenzhen on 18 November 2026, on the sidelines of the APEC summit. The agreements span four major categories: smart city development, manufacturing and technology, infrastructure financing, and coal-to-ethanol technology.

The largest component is a US$50 billion commitment for the development of ten initial "future smart city" sites across Indonesia. The agreement was signed between PT Kota Masa Depan Indonesia, operating under the Danantara Indonesia sovereign wealth vehicle, and a consortium led by Shaanxi province. Minister of Investment and Downstreaming Rosan Roeslani, who also serves as CEO of Danantara, described the smart city project as "a historical project to be implemented next year" that would create jobs and facilitate technology transfer.

Chinese electronics and home appliance manufacturer Hisense committed approximately US$10 billion for expanded operations in Indonesia, including a joint research and development laboratory, talent development programmes, and home appliance manufacturing, in collaboration with the Ministry of Higher Education, Science, and Technology and Danantara. A separate US$10 billion infrastructure financing partnership was affirmed between Danantara and a consortium led by China's State Development and Investment Corporation (SDIC). The fourth agreement, worth US$1.8 billion, covers coal-to-ethanol technology development between the Ministry of Higher Education, state-owned Pertamina, and Beijing Petrochemical Engineering Co., Ltd.

Separately, on 20 November, President Prabowo witnessed 35 additional Indonesia-China investment deals worth US$15 billion, bringing the total commitments from the China visit to approximately US$86.8 billion. The US-ASEAN Business Council, whose president led a delegation of 33 American companies to Jakarta in November, separately published a commentary calling on Indonesia to strengthen contractual and regulatory predictability to attract and retain foreign investment, citing concerns about changes to production-sharing contracts, shifting import licensing requirements, and overlapping data localisation rules.

Our analysis

The US$71.8 billion in MoUs with China demands careful disaggregation. The headline figure is dominated by the US$50 billion smart city commitment, which is an agreement between a Danantara subsidiary and a Chinese provincial consortium to develop ten urban sites — a category of investment announcement that historically has a very low conversion rate from signed MoU to actual capital deployment. Infrastructure and smart city MoUs of this scale routinely represent aspirational frameworks for projects that may take a decade or more to materialise, if they materialise at all. The more commercially concrete commitments — Hisense's US$10 billion for manufacturing and R&D, and the US$1.8 billion coal-to-ethanol project — are more likely to result in near-term investment, though even these will require years to fully deploy. The timing is strategically significant. Indonesia is pursuing Chinese investment at a moment when its capital markets are under severe stress — the Jakarta Composite Index down 41.59 per cent year-to-date, MSCI reclassification risk looming, and Rp61 trillion in net foreign selling. The US$71.8 billion MoU total provides a counter-narrative to the capital markets crisis: even as portfolio investors flee Indonesian equities, the government can point to massive bilateral commitments as evidence of long-term confidence. But MoUs and portfolio flows serve different functions — MoUs signal political alignment and future intent, while portfolio flows reflect current market conditions and risk pricing. The juxtaposition of US$71.8 billion in Chinese MoUs with the US-ASEAN Business Council's pointed commentary about contractual predictability highlights a fundamental tension in Indonesia's investment strategy. American companies are explicitly asking for assurances that signed agreements will be honoured and regulations applied consistently — concerns about production-sharing contract changes, stranded containers from import licensing shifts, and overlapping data rules. China-linked investment operates on different diplomatic and commercial logic, often accepting regulatory uncertainty as part of a broader strategic relationship. This creates a two-track investment environment where the nature and reliability of foreign capital varies significantly by source country.

What we don't know yet

What proportion of the US$71.8 billion in MoUs will convert to actual capital deployment within the next three to five years. The specific locations of the ten smart city sites and whether environmental impact assessments and land acquisition have been initiated. Whether the Hisense manufacturing commitment represents genuine new capacity or consolidation and expansion of existing operations in Indonesia. How the US$10 billion SDIC infrastructure financing will be structured — whether as equity, debt, or blended instruments — and what assets or revenue streams Indonesia is offering as collateral. Whether the coal-to-ethanol technology is commercially viable at the US$1.8 billion scale and what environmental impact it will have. How the United States will respond to the deepening Indonesia-China economic relationship, particularly in the context of ongoing US tariff negotiations with ASEAN members.

普拉博沃见证与中国签署718亿美元战略合作备忘录，涵盖智慧城市、制造和基础设施

2026年11月18–24日。印尼内阁秘书处 / 安塔拉通讯社 / Fortune / Observer ID。

经核实的报道

普拉博沃·苏比安托总统于2026年11月18日在APEC峰会期间在深圳见证了与中国合作伙伴交换价值约718亿美元（相当于超过1,300万亿印尼盾）的谅解备忘录和投资承诺。协议涵盖四大类别：智慧城市开发、制造与技术、基础设施融资和煤制乙醇技术。

NESDC Raises 2026 GDP Forecast to 2.5% but K-Shaped Recovery Widens Divide Between Tech Exports and Struggling SMEs

16–24 November 2026. NESDC / Bank of Thailand / The Nation / TMBThanachart / CIMB Thai Bank.

Verified reporting

Thailand's National Economic and Social Development Council (NESDC) raised its 2026 GDP growth forecast to 2.5 per cent on 16 November, up from an August estimate of 2.2 per cent, following Q3 GDP growth of 2.9 per cent year-on-year. The NESDC set its 2027 growth forecast at a range of 2.0 to 3.0 per cent. The Bank of Thailand separately forecasts 2.4 per cent growth for 2026 and 2.1 per cent for 2027, noting that 2.4 per cent is below the economy's potential.

However, the upgraded headline masks a widening K-shaped divide. Bangkok Bank chief economist Kobsak Pootrakool noted that part of the upgrade reflects a routine five-year revision to the Manufacturing Production Index weights that better captures smart electronics and AI-linked supply chains, estimating the reweighting adds approximately 0.5 percentage points — effectively turning a 2.0 per cent estimate into 2.5 per cent. Seasonally adjusted quarter-on-quarter output was flat in Q2 and grew only 0.3 per cent in Q3, according to CIMB Thai Bank's head of research Amonthep Chawla, who expects 0.3 to 0.4 per cent sequential growth in Q4.

The growing sectors are concentrated in electronics and AI-related investment, including data centres and infrastructure for artificial intelligence. Thailand's exports have benefited from the global semiconductor cycle, with AI-related product categories showing strong growth. Household debt has shown some improvement, falling to 85.2 per cent of GDP in Q2 2026 from 86.0 per cent in Q1, according to Bank of Thailand figures.

The struggling sectors present a sharply different picture. Lending to small and medium-sized businesses is contracting, with small firms facing tight cash flow and difficulty accessing credit. The lower end of the property market remains sluggish. Purchasing power is particularly weak in provinces outside the main tourist destinations, and lower-income households have little disposable income. Bank of Thailand spokesperson Chayawadee Chai-anant warned that tourism is exposed to a China slowdown — if Chinese households cut spending or overseas trips, Thai tourism would feel the impact immediately, with spillover effects through export and supply chain channels.

Our analysis

Thailand's K-shaped recovery is ASEAN's most instructive case study in how headline GDP growth can obscure the reality on the ground. The 2.5 per cent forecast — itself the weakest among major ASEAN economies except for the Philippines — is partly an artefact of statistical methodology rather than genuine economic acceleration. If Kobsak Pootrakool's estimate is correct that the MPI reweighting adds 0.5 percentage points, the underlying growth rate is closer to 2.0 per cent, which is what the Bank of Thailand's own forecast implies. The sequential growth data confirms this reading: flat in Q2 and 0.3 per cent in Q3 means the economy is barely expanding on a quarter-to-quarter basis. CIMB Thai Bank's Amonthep Chawla's assessment that Thailand's underlying potential may have fallen below 2 per cent unless productivity improves is the most concerning statement in the report. If true, it means Thailand is growing near its potential ceiling — not because the economy is overheating, but because the ceiling itself has lowered. This would explain the K-shape: the technology-linked sectors (electronics, AI infrastructure, data centres) are growing because they are connected to global demand that bypasses domestic constraints, while the domestically oriented sectors (SMEs, lower-end property, provincial services) are stagnating because domestic demand is structurally weak. The comparison with regional peers is unflattering. Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore, and Vietnam are all growing between 5 and 6 per cent. Thailand at 2.5 per cent is an outlier that cannot be explained by external conditions alone — if global demand were the constraint, all ASEAN exporters would be similarly affected. Thailand's specific challenges are structural: much of its tech production sits in the middle and lower parts of the supply chain with heavy reliance on imported inputs, so higher export sales produce smaller domestic income gains than in countries with more domestic value addition. The data centre investment cycle poses a timing risk — if it peaks while smaller firms are still weak, Thailand could lose a growth source beyond 2027 without having built domestic alternatives.

What we don't know yet

Whether the Bank of Thailand will cut rates in 2027 to support domestic demand, given Kobsak's expectation that inflation will ease toward 1 per cent over the next year. The specific contribution of the MPI reweighting to the GDP upgrade — whether NESDC will publish a reconciliation of old and new methodology. How much of the electronics and AI export growth is producing domestic income versus flowing through to foreign owners of Thai-based production facilities. Whether the tourism sector can maintain current visitor numbers if China's economic slowdown deepens and Chinese households reduce discretionary travel spending. The timeline for structural reforms to address the SME credit crunch and provincial purchasing power weakness — whether the government has specific policy interventions planned or is relying on the trickle-down from export sector growth.

Philippines Inflation Accelerates to 7.8% in October as BSP Faces Pressure for Further Rate Hikes Despite 2.1% GDP Growth

21–24 November 2026. Philippine Statistics Authority / Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas / Philippine Star / Barclays / BPI.

Verified reporting

The Philippines' headline inflation rate accelerated to 7.8 per cent in October 2026, up from 7.2 per cent in September, while core inflation — excluding volatile food and energy — rose to 5.4 per cent from 4.8 per cent. The acceleration underscores the increasingly embedded nature of price pressures across the economy, extending well beyond the food and energy categories that initially drove the inflation surge.

Bank of the Philippine Islands lead economist Emilio Neri Jr. argued that the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) must continue raising rates cautiously despite Q3 GDP growth of just 2.1 per cent — the weakest quarterly result since the pandemic. The BSP's benchmark rate stands at 5.50 per cent following a 50-basis-point increase in October and cumulative tightening of 125 basis points in 2026. However, the inflation-adjusted policy rate remains deeply negative at approximately minus 2.3 per cent — meaning that despite aggressive nominal hikes, real borrowing costs remain below inflation and monetary policy is effectively still accommodative.

Barclays forecasts that headline inflation could breach 8.0 per cent in November and sees the possibility of another 50-basis-point hike at the BSP's 17 December meeting. Neri cautioned that halting tightening too soon could allow inflation expectations to become entrenched, triggering further wage and price increases. He described the BSP as facing "a delicate tightrope walk" and said pausing normalisation prematurely carries "far greater long-term risks." The World Bank has separately urged the Philippines to pursue structural reforms in agricultural supply chains, stating that food price inflation is a supply-side problem requiring supply-side solutions.

The Philippine Stock Exchange Index has fallen 28 per cent year-to-date with net foreign selling of PHP 123 billion, trading at approximately 9.5 times forward earnings — the lowest valuation in over a decade. S&P and Deutsche Bank have both cut their Philippines growth forecasts, joining Moody's earlier downgrade to 2.3 per cent for 2026, amid what analysts are increasingly characterising as a stagflationary environment.

Our analysis

The core inflation acceleration from 4.8 to 5.4 per cent is the most alarming figure in this report because it confirms that price pressures have become generalised across the economy. When core inflation — which strips out the volatile items like food and fuel — is itself accelerating, it means that businesses are passing higher input costs through to consumers across a wide range of goods and services, and that inflation expectations are becoming unmoored. The negative real policy rate of minus 2.3 per cent explains why 125 basis points of hikes have failed to cool inflation: in real terms, money is still cheap. Borrowers are paying 5.50 per cent on a nominal basis but only need their investments to return more than inflation — currently 7.8 per cent — to come out ahead, which means the rate hikes have not yet made borrowing genuinely expensive. BPI economist Neri's assessment that "rate hikes don't grow rice, but pausing could harvest inflation" captures the central dilemma perfectly. The BSP is fighting a supply-driven inflation shock with a demand-side tool. Rate hikes cannot increase rice production, fix typhoon-damaged vegetable supply chains, or reduce transport costs — but failing to hike risks allowing inflation to become self-reinforcing through wage-price spirals. The World Bank's intervention explicitly stating that this is a supply-side problem requiring supply-side solutions is a pointed criticism of the policy mix. The 2.1 per cent Q3 GDP growth — combined with 7.8 per cent inflation — places the Philippines squarely in stagflation territory, the worst macroeconomic quadrant. No other ASEAN economy is in this position. The comparison is now extreme: Malaysia has 1.9 per cent inflation with 5.5 per cent growth; the Philippines has four times the inflation at less than half the growth. The 28 per cent decline in the PSEi and PHP 123 billion in foreign selling are the capital markets expression of this macro deterioration. The 9.5 times forward earnings is historically cheap, but cheapness alone does not attract capital — investors need a credible catalyst for improvement, and the Philippines currently offers none.

What we don't know yet

Whether the BSP will deliver a 25 or 50-basis-point hike at the 17 December meeting, and what forward guidance it will provide. Whether November inflation will breach 8.0 per cent as Barclays forecasts. The timeline for rice import liberalisation — whether the administration will reduce tariffs on imported rice to bring down food prices, and the political feasibility given domestic rice farmer opposition. Whether S&P's sovereign credit rating will follow the growth downgrade — the Philippines is currently rated BBB+ (investment grade), but sustained stagflation and fiscal deterioration could trigger a negative outlook. Whether the fiscal deficit, at 6.1 per cent of GDP through nine months, will narrow in Q4 or whether social spending will continue to expand.

原创分析

核心通胀从4.8%加速至5.4%是本报告中最令人担忧的数字，因为它确认价格压力已在整个经济中全面化。当核心通胀——剔除了食品和燃料等波动性项目——本身也在加速时，意味着企业正将更高的投入成本传导给各类商品和服务的消费者，通胀预期正在脱锚。负2.3%的实际政策利率解释了为何125个基点的加息未能冷却通胀：实际上，资金仍然便宜。借款人在名义基础上支付5.50%，但只需投资回报超过通胀——目前为7.8%——即可获利，这意味着加息尚未使借贷真正变得昂贵。BPI经济学家内里的评估"加息不能种出大米，但暂停可能收获通胀"完美概括了核心困境。BSP正在用需求侧工具对抗供给驱动的通胀冲击。加息无法增加大米产量、修复台风破坏的蔬菜供应链或降低运输成本——但不加息则有风险让通胀通过工资-价格螺旋变得自我强化。世界银行明确指出这是需要供给侧解决方案的供给侧问题，是对政策组合的尖锐批评。2.1%的第三季度GDP增长——加上7.8%的通胀——使菲律宾稳稳地处于滞胀领域，最糟糕的宏观经济象限。没有其他东盟经济体处于这一位置。对比现在极为悬殊：马来西亚通胀1.9%伴随5.5%增长；菲律宾是四倍的通胀率、不到一半的增长。PSEi 28%的跌幅和1,230亿比索的外资卖出是这一宏观恶化的资本市场表达。9.5倍的前瞻市盈率在历史上很便宜，但仅靠估值低廉并不能吸引资本——投资者需要可信的改善催化剂，而菲律宾目前没有提供任何催化剂。

未知事项

BSP将在12月17日会议上加息25还是50个基点，以及将提供什么前瞻性指引。11月通胀是否会如巴克莱预测般突破8.0%。大米进口自由化的时间表——政府是否会降低进口大米关税以压低食品价格，以及考虑到国内稻农反对的政治可行性。标普的主权信用评级是否会跟随增长预测下调——菲律宾目前评级为BBB+（投资级），但持续的滞胀和财政恶化可能触发负面展望。前九个月占GDP 6.1%的财政赤字是否会在第四季度收窄，还是社会支出将继续扩大。

Malaysia Plans Separate Wholesale Electricity Market for Data Centres as Sector Heads Toward 21% of National Power Demand

4–24 November 2026. Bloomberg / Malay Mail / The Star / Eco-Business / Energy Commission of Malaysia.

Verified reporting

Malaysia is planning a separate wholesale electricity market specifically for data centre operators, targeted for launch by 2029 or 2030, according to Bloomberg reporting on 11 November 2026. The move is part of a broader government strategy to manage the rapidly growing power demands of the data centre sector without compromising electricity supply to households and other industries.

Data centres already account for 9.28 per cent of Malaysia's total electricity consumption as of September 2026, according to the Energy Commission, up from negligible levels just three years ago. Modelling by the government projects that data centres could consume up to 21 per cent of national power demand as approved facilities reach full operation — a figure that would make Malaysia's data centre power share one of the highest in the world relative to national grid capacity. The government has 117 approved data centre projects, primarily concentrated in Johor and Selangor.

The government has taken several policy steps to manage the energy impact. Putrajaya has moved to prevent "phantom demand" — where approved projects reserve grid capacity but delay construction — from blocking other users' access to electricity. States with insufficient water supply for cooling have been prohibited from hosting new data centres. Malaysia has stopped approving data centres that are not specifically designed for artificial intelligence workloads, effectively directing the pipeline toward higher-value computing rather than conventional cloud storage. Mandatory renewable energy quotas have been imposed on data centre operators, with the government requiring them to source an increasing share of their electricity from solar and other clean sources.

The Economy Minister and government officials have assured industries that power consumed by data centres will not affect supply to other sectors. The separate wholesale market is designed to allow data centre operators to procure electricity through direct contracts with generators at market prices, rather than through the subsidised tariff structure available to residential and industrial users. This would insulate the broader economy from the fiscal cost of powering data centres at below-market rates.

Our analysis

Malaysia's data centre energy challenge is the most consequential infrastructure policy question facing any ASEAN economy today, and the government's response — a separate wholesale electricity market, mandatory renewable quotas, AI-only approvals, and phantom demand prevention — represents the most sophisticated regulatory framework for data centre power management in the region. The 9.28 per cent current share of national electricity consumption heading toward 21 per cent illustrates the speed and scale of the transformation. For context, the entire residential sector in Malaysia consumes approximately 20 per cent of national electricity — data centres are on track to match household consumption within two to three years. The separate wholesale market is the most significant structural reform. By creating a parallel procurement channel at market prices, Malaysia is effectively segmenting its electricity market into two tiers: a subsidised tier for households and traditional industry, and a market-priced tier for data centres. This protects the fiscal cost of electricity subsidies — which are already politically sensitive — while allowing data centre operators to pay the full cost of their power consumption. It also creates a price signal that incentivises data centre operators to invest in energy efficiency and on-site renewable generation, since they bear the full cost of their electricity rather than free-riding on subsidised rates. The decision to stop approving non-AI data centres is a strategic filter that prioritises higher-value computing workloads. Conventional cloud storage and content delivery facilities generate lower revenue per megawatt than AI training and inference clusters, so Malaysia is effectively curating its data centre pipeline for maximum economic return per unit of electricity consumed. The phantom demand prevention measure addresses a real problem: when companies secure grid capacity through approved projects but delay construction, they can block other users from accessing the grid even though no power is being consumed. This creates artificial scarcity that drives up costs and delays for manufacturing and other industrial users. Malaysia's challenge is execution — building generation and transmission capacity fast enough to serve both the existing 117 approved projects and the growing needs of other sectors, while transitioning an increasing share to renewable sources.

What we don't know yet

The specific structure of the separate wholesale electricity market — whether it will be a bilateral contract market, a spot exchange, or a hybrid. The timeline for bringing sufficient new generation capacity online to serve 21 per cent of national demand without supply disruptions. Whether the mandatory renewable energy quotas are achievable given Malaysia's solar generation capacity pipeline and the intermittency challenges of tropical weather patterns. The fiscal impact of the market segmentation — how much revenue the government expects from market-priced data centre electricity versus the cost of maintaining subsidised rates for other sectors. Whether the AI-only approval policy will deter conventional cloud operators from investing in Malaysia and redirect them to neighbouring countries with less restrictive policies. The impact on Tenaga Nasional Berhad's investment plans and profitability from the transition to a dual-tier market structure.

马来西亚计划为数据中心设立独立批发电力市场，该行业电力需求将达全国的21%

2026年11月4–24日。彭博社 / 马来邮报 / 星报 / Eco-Business / 马来西亚能源委员会。

经核实的报道

据彭博社11月11日报道，马来西亚正计划专门为数据中心运营商设立独立的批发电力市场，目标在2029年或2030年启动。此举是政府更广泛战略的一部分，旨在管理数据中心行业快速增长的电力需求，同时不影响家庭和其他行业的电力供应。

根据能源委员会数据，截至2026年9月，数据中心已占马来西亚总电力消耗的9.28%，而三年前这一比例几乎可以忽略不计。政府模型预测，当已批准设施全面运营时，数据中心可能消耗高达21%的全国电力需求——这一数字将使马来西亚数据中心电力占比相对于国家电网容量成为世界上最高的之一。政府已批准117个数据中心项目，主要集中在柔佛和雪兰莪。

政府已采取多项政策措施管理能源影响。布城已采取行动防止"幽灵需求"——已批准项目预留电网容量但延迟建设——阻止其他用户获取电力。水资源不足的州已被禁止承接新数据中心。马来西亚已停止批准非专门用于人工智能工作负载的数据中心，实际上将管线引导向更高价值的计算而非传统云存储。数据中心运营商已被施加强制性可再生能源配额，政府要求它们从太阳能和其他清洁能源获取越来越多的电力份额。

经济部长和政府官员已向各行业保证，数据中心消耗的电力不会影响其他行业的供应。独立批发市场旨在允许数据中心运营商通过与发电商的直接合同以市场价格采购电力，而非通过向住宅和工业用户提供的补贴电价结构。这将使更广泛的经济免受以低于市场价格为数据中心供电的财政成本影响。

原创分析

马来西亚的数据中心能源挑战是当今任何东盟经济体面临的最重要的基础设施政策问题，政府的应对——独立批发电力市场、强制性可再生能源配额、仅限AI审批和幽灵需求防范——代表了该地区最成熟的数据中心电力管理监管框架。当前9.28%的全国电力消耗占比向21%迈进，说明了转型的速度和规模。作为背景，马来西亚整个住宅部门消耗约20%的全国电力——数据中心有望在两到三年内追平家庭用电量。独立批发市场是最重要的结构性改革。通过创建以市场价格运作的平行采购渠道，马来西亚实际上将其电力市场分为两个层级：为家庭和传统工业提供的补贴层级，以及为数据中心提供的市场定价层级。这保护了电力补贴的财政成本——已具政治敏感性——同时允许数据中心运营商支付其电力消耗的全部成本。它还创造了激励数据中心运营商投资能源效率和现场可再生能源发电的价格信号，因为它们承担全部电力成本而非搭补贴的便车。停止批准非AI数据中心的决定是一个优先考虑更高价值计算工作负载的战略过滤器。传统云存储和内容分发设施每兆瓦产生的收入低于AI训练和推理集群，因此马来西亚实际上在筛选其数据中心管线以实现每单位电力消耗的最大经济回报。幽灵需求防范措施解决了一个现实问题：当企业通过已批准项目获得电网容量但延迟建设时，即使没有消耗电力，它们也可以阻止其他用户接入电网。这造成人为稀缺，推高了制造业和其他工业用户的成本和延误。马来西亚的挑战在于执行——足够快地建设发电和输电能力以服务现有117个已批准项目和其他行业不断增长的需求，同时将越来越多的份额过渡到可再生能源。

未知事项

独立批发电力市场的具体结构——是双边合同市场、现货交易所还是混合模式。为服务21%的全国需求而在线引入足够新发电能力的时间表，且不产生供应中断。考虑到马来西亚的太阳能发电能力管线和热带气候模式的间歇性挑战，强制性可再生能源配额是否可实现。市场分割的财政影响——政府预期从市场定价的数据中心电力获得多少收入，与维持其他行业补贴费率的成本相比。仅限AI的审批政策是否会阻止传统云运营商在马来西亚投资，并将其重定向到政策限制较少的邻国。向双层市场结构过渡对马来西亚国家能源公司（TNB）投资计划和盈利能力的影响。
