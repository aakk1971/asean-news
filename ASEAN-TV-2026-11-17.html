English presenter script

Lead presenter: Good evening. Our lead story tonight — extraordinary trade numbers out of Singapore. Non-oil domestic exports surged 79.8 per cent year-on-year in October, smashing the Reuters median forecast of 37.7 per cent and marking the strongest growth since 1988. The trade surplus ballooned to a record S$19.3 billion — more than double September's figure.

Analyst: These numbers are genuinely historic. Electronics NODX grew 73.9 per cent, with disk media products nearly tripling — up 189.6 per cent to approximately S$1.1 billion — personal computers rising 81 per cent to about S$700 million, and integrated circuits climbing 53.1 per cent. This is the AI infrastructure buildout showing up directly in Singapore's trade data. Non-electronics NODX surged 81.9 per cent as well, boosted by a 253.1 per cent spike in pharmaceuticals and ships, though that partly reflects a low base from last year. The geographic breakdown is telling: shipments to the United States soared 214.6 per cent, underscoring American tech companies' enormous appetite for AI components routed through Singapore's semiconductor ecosystem.

Lead presenter: How does this compare with the government's own forecast?

Analyst: Singapore is now dramatically outperforming its own projections. Year-to-date NODX growth stands at 33.6 per cent, more than double the government's raised full-year forecast of 14 to 16 per cent. Total merchandise trade grew 50.7 per cent in October, with exports up 55.8 per cent and imports rising 44.9 per cent. The trade surplus of S$19.3 billion — roughly US$15 billion in a single month — is extraordinary for an economy Singapore's size. But we should note the MAS warning from last month: AI-driven growth is expected to ease in late 2027. The higher the peak, the more visible the eventual normalisation will be. For now, though, Singapore sits at the absolute epicentre of the global AI hardware supply chain, and these numbers prove it.

English presenter script

Lead presenter: Turning to Malaysia, where the investment picture continues to strengthen. Approved investments jumped 20.7 per cent to RM350.4 billion in the first nine months of 2026 — roughly US$78 billion — with a dramatic shift in who is investing.

Analyst: The headline within the headline is this: the United States has displaced China as Malaysia's number one foreign investor. American investment surged fivefold to RM60 billion, up from RM11.3 billion in the same period last year. China came second at RM38.6 billion, Singapore third at RM38.5 billion, Japan fourth at RM24.4 billion, and Australia fifth at RM12.6 billion. These five countries account for 82 per cent of all foreign investment. Total foreign investment of RM212.3 billion — 60.6 per cent of the overall figure — jumped 40.7 per cent year-on-year. A total of 4,387 projects were approved, expected to create over 152,000 jobs.

Lead presenter: And data centres continue to dominate the pipeline?

Analyst: Absolutely. Data centre and cloud computing investments reached RM123.5 billion — up 44.5 per cent — now accounting for 35.2 per cent of all approved investments. That is more than a third of Malaysia's entire investment pipeline concentrated in a single technology category. Johor attracted the most investment at RM99.7 billion, boosted by its proximity to Singapore and its emerging data centre corridor, followed by Selangor at RM87.9 billion. The important nuance is that this is not a substitution story — American investment quintupling does not mean Chinese investment is falling. China's RM38.6 billion also grew. Malaysia is attracting more capital from both superpowers simultaneously, which is a remarkable feat of strategic positioning. The challenge now is execution: MIDA reports that 87.4 per cent of manufacturing projects approved since 2021 have reached production or construction, which is a creditable conversion rate. But with RM123.5 billion in data centre approvals, questions about energy and water supply capacity in key corridors will only intensify.

English presenter script

Lead presenter: Thailand's official third-quarter GDP data is now in. Growth came in at 2.9 per cent year-on-year — confirming the Bank of Thailand's earlier signal that revised manufacturing statistics would lift the number. The NESDC has raised its full-year forecast to 2.5 per cent.

Analyst: What matters more than the headline is the composition. Investment grew at its fastest pace in 55 quarters — nearly 14 years — with total investment up 11.8 per cent and private investment surging 16.2 per cent, driven by a 20.5 per cent increase in machinery and equipment spending. This tells us companies are building capacity, not merely selling into one-off demand. Merchandise exports surged 23.6 per cent — the strongest growth in 21 quarters — worth US$106.4 billion, led by computer components, communications equipment, and integrated circuits linked to AI and data centre demand. Manufacturing output grew 3.7 per cent, the fastest in 16 quarters.

Lead presenter: But the NESDC has flagged risks for the fourth quarter?

Analyst: Yes. NESDC chief Danucha Pichayanan acknowledged that property, services, and tourism sectors weighed on growth in Q3, and the fourth quarter is expected to slow on a high base effect and weaker private investment. The K-shaped recovery persists — strong exporters and large investors are thriving while households and small businesses face pressure. Unemployment is a healthy 1.02 per cent and headline inflation is manageable at 2.4 per cent, but high household debt continues to constrain domestic consumption. Tourism arrivals fell 5 per cent to 7.06 million in Q3, though receipts rose 11.7 per cent, suggesting higher spending per visitor. The central bank held its key rate at 1.0 per cent, with the next review on 23 December. Thailand is clearly benefiting from the AI-driven export boom sweeping ASEAN, but the benefits are concentrated in manufacturing and investment corridors — not yet broadly shared across the economy.

English presenter script

Lead presenter: The Philippines now. Citigroup's Asia economics chief has publicly declared the country in stagflation — a combination of stagnating growth and rising inflation that is the most difficult macroeconomic condition to manage. GDP growth of 2.1 per cent and inflation of 7.8 per cent — the numbers speak for themselves.

Analyst: When one of the Philippines' largest foreign banking operations uses the word stagflation, it carries real weight. Citi's Johanna Chua called it "slowflation" — a term that may soften the blow but describes the same condition. The data is stark: five consecutive quarters of deceleration, GDP at a 16-year low excluding the pandemic, inflation nearly four times the central bank's target midpoint, and unemployment rising from 3.8 to 5.4 per cent in a single year with 2.86 million Filipinos out of work. The collapse in public investment is particularly alarming — infrastructure spending plunged 34.6 per cent from January to August as investigations into flood-control projects paralysed government capital outlays. Public construction has fallen for a sixth straight quarter.

Lead presenter: And the central bank is still tightening?

Analyst: That is the impossible bind. The BSP raised rates by 25 basis points to 5 per cent in August, then by 50 basis points to 5.5 per cent in October. Citi expects two more quarter-point hikes to 6.0 per cent, with no easing until 2028. Barclays has warned inflation could reach 8 per cent in November and also projected a jumbo rate hike. S&P Global separately reinforced the stagflation assessment. Economic Planning Secretary Balisacan disagreed with the label, saying it was "too early to judge" — but the statistical case against him grows with each data release. The Philippines is now the clearest outlier in ASEAN: the only major economy simultaneously raising rates, experiencing rising unemployment, and decelerating toward 2 per cent growth while its neighbours ride an AI-driven export boom that the Philippines' domestic-demand-oriented economy simply cannot capture at scale.

English presenter script

Lead presenter: Vietnam continues to generate remarkable investment numbers. Registered foreign direct investment in the first ten months of 2026 hit US$54.85 billion — surging 74 per cent year-on-year to a 17-year high.

Analyst: The scale of this surge is worth unpacking. New project registrations numbered 3,478 with US$30.73 billion in capital — project count up 4.7 per cent but capital more than doubling. Additional capital for existing projects totalled US$16.13 billion across 1,079 projects, up 33.2 per cent. Disbursed FDI — the money actually hitting the ground — reached an estimated US$24.08 billion, up 13.1 per cent and the highest for the same period in five years. The source-country breakdown is interesting: Singapore leads with US$9.52 billion or 31 per cent, followed by South Korea at US$5.81 billion, Luxembourg at US$4.99 billion — which likely reflects European holding company structures — Hong Kong at US$3.57 billion, mainland China at US$2.77 billion, and Japan at US$1.58 billion.

Lead presenter: Where is this investment concentrated?

Analyst: Manufacturing and processing lead newly registered capital at US$14.61 billion — 47.5 per cent of the total. Ho Chi Minh City attracted over US$10.3 billion with 1,761 new projects, while Thai Nguyen province ranked second with over US$5.8 billion, reflecting the northern electronics manufacturing cluster anchored by Samsung's massive operations. The gap between registered and disbursed FDI — US$54.85 billion versus US$24.08 billion — underscores the persistent challenge: converting commitments to operating factories takes two to four years and can be disrupted by policy shifts and infrastructure bottlenecks. Despite Section 301 tariffs imposed by the United States in July, global manufacturers are still choosing Vietnam as a production base. The 74 per cent surge is a resounding vote of confidence — but Vietnam's US$140 billion surplus with the United States makes it a prominent target for further trade rebalancing, and that strategic risk remains the most significant overhang on the investment story.

中文主播稿

主播：越南继续创造令人瞩目的投资数据。2026年前十个月注册外国直接投资达548.5亿美元——同比飙升74%至17年新高。

分析师：这一飙升的规模值得细究。新注册项目3,478个，资本307.3亿美元——项目数量增长4.7%但资本翻倍以上。现有项目追加资本161.3亿美元，涉及1,079个项目，增长33.2%。实际到位FDI——真正落地的资金——估计达240.8亿美元，增长13.1%，为五年来同期最高。来源国分布很有意思：新加坡以95.2亿美元或31%领先，其次是韩国58.1亿美元，卢森堡49.9亿美元——可能反映了欧洲控股公司结构——香港35.7亿美元，中国大陆27.7亿美元，日本15.8亿美元。

分析师：制造加工业在新注册资本中领先，达146.1亿美元——占总额的47.5%。胡志明市吸引超过103亿美元，新注册1,761个项目，太原省以超过58亿美元排名第二，反映了以三星大型运营为支撑的北部电子制造集群。注册与到位FDI之间的差距——548.5亿美元对240.8亿美元——凸显了持续的挑战：将承诺转化为运营工厂需要两到四年，可能被政策转变和基础设施瓶颈打断。尽管美国7月实施了301条款关税，全球制造商仍在选择越南作为生产基地。74%的飙升是响亮的信任票——但越南对美1,400亿美元顺差使其成为进一步贸易再平衡的显著目标，这一战略风险仍是投资故事上最重大的阴影。

English presenter script

Lead presenter: Indonesia's equity market story continues to deteriorate. MSCI has removed eight Indonesian stocks from its Global Standard Indexes in its November rebalancing — with no additions. The number of Indonesian constituents has shrunk from 43 to 35.

Analyst: This is the most tangible financial market consequence yet of the governance concerns that prompted MSCI's reclassification consultation in October. Among the removed stocks are Barito Pacific and Bumi Resources Minerals. No Indonesian stocks were added, which is particularly telling — in a normal market environment, a growing economy with 5.47 per cent GDP growth would typically see index additions, not exclusively deletions. The changes take effect after the close on 30 November, and passive funds tracking MSCI indexes will adjust holdings ahead of that date, creating additional mechanical selling pressure on top of the already-severe foreign capital outflows.

Lead presenter: And the reclassification consultation is still running?

Analyst: Yes. The consultation on potentially reclassifying Indonesia from Emerging Market to Standalone Market status runs until 15 January 2027, with a decision expected in February. The timeline is now critical: Indonesia has approximately two months to demonstrate sufficient governance reforms to persuade MSCI to maintain its current status. The rebalancing was part of a broader Asia-Pacific adjustment — China had 38 deletions, Japan 5, Taiwan 4 — but Indonesia's situation is unique because it faces the existential threat of reclassification. The irony is extraordinary: the real economy has never been stronger — 5.47 per cent GDP growth, robust manufacturing, solid fiscal position — but the equity market is pricing in a worst-case scenario because capital market governance has not kept pace. The IHSG is trading at valuations last seen in 2017, down approximately 45 per cent from its January peak, despite corporate earnings broadly in line with expectations. This divergence between economic fundamentals and market reality cannot persist indefinitely.

主播：印尼股市的故事继续恶化。MSCI在11月调整中从其全球标准指数移除了八只印尼股票——没有新增。印尼成分股从43只缩减至35只。

分析师：这是MSCI 10月启动重新分类磋商所引发的治理担忧迄今最切实的金融市场后果。被移除的股票包括Barito Pacific和Bumi Resources Minerals。没有印尼股票被新增，这尤为说明问题——在正常市场环境下，一个GDP增长5.47%的增长型经济体通常会看到指数增补，而非纯粹的删除。变更将于11月30日收盘后生效，追踪MSCI指数的被动基金将在此日期前调整持仓，在本已严重的外资流出之上产生额外的机械性抛售压力。

主播：重新分类磋商仍在进行中？

分析师：是的。将印尼从新兴市场重新分类为独立市场的磋商将持续至2027年1月15日，预计2月作出决定。时间线现在至关重要：印尼大约有两个月的时间来展示足够的治理改革以说服MSCI维持其当前地位。此次调整是更广泛的亚太区域调整的一部分——中国有38只股票被删除，日本5只，台湾4只——但印尼的情况独特，因为它面临着重新分类的生存威胁。讽刺至极：实体经济从未如此强劲——第三季度GDP增长5.47%、制造业强劲、财政状况稳健——但股票市场正在定价最坏情景，因为资本市场治理未能跟上步伐。IHSG以2017年以来的估值水平交易，较1月高点下跌约45%，尽管企业盈利大致符合预期。经济基本面与市场现实之间的这种割裂不可能无限期持续。

English presenter script

Lead presenter: And finally, the 49th ASEAN Summit has concluded in Manila, with the Philippines formally handing the chairmanship to Singapore for 2027. It was a summit that produced over 100 outcomes — but the substance lies in a few key achievements.

Analyst: The most consequential deliverable of the Philippine chairship was the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement — DEFA — which we covered in detail yesterday. It is the world's first legally binding regional digital economy pact, covering cross-border data flows, electronic payments, cybersecurity, and AI governance for a market of 670 million people. Beyond DEFA, progress was made toward an ASEAN–Canada free trade agreement, upgrades to FTAs with South Korea and India, and preliminary work on an ASEAN Green Economy Framework Agreement. The ASEAN Power Grid memorandum was also formally enhanced.

Lead presenter: What does Singapore bring to the chairmanship?

Analyst: This handover is more than routine rotation. It marks a transition from a chair country experiencing severe economic distress — the Philippines with 2.1 per cent growth and 7.8 per cent inflation — to one at the peak of its economic strength, with Singapore delivering 5.4 per cent GDP growth, record trade surpluses, and genuine global leadership in digital economy governance. Prime Minister Lawrence Wong has indicated that Singapore's 2027 chairmanship will focus on unity, integration, and regional resilience, building on DEFA's momentum. Singapore's diplomatic weight and economic credibility give it a stronger platform than most chairs to tackle the unresolved tensions that carry over — South China Sea disputes, the Myanmar crisis, and trade policy uncertainty with the United States. But ASEAN's consensus-based decision-making ensures progress will remain incremental. President Marcos closed by saying that changing course "is not the same as losing direction" — a fitting reflection for a bloc navigating an increasingly complex geopolitical landscape.

中文主播稿

主播：最后，第49届东盟峰会在马尼拉闭幕，菲律宾正式将主席国移交给新加坡，迎接2027年。峰会产出了100多项成果——但实质在于几项关键成就。

分析师：菲律宾主席国任期最具影响力的成果是《东盟数字经济框架协议》——DEFA——我们昨天已详细报道。它是全球首个具有法律约束力的区域数字经济协定，涵盖跨境数据流通、电子支付、网络安全和AI治理，面向6.7亿人口的市场。除DEFA外，东盟在推进与加拿大的自贸协定、升级与韩国和印度的自贸协定，以及东盟绿色经济框架协议的初步工作方面也取得了进展。东盟电网谅解备忘录也得到正式强化。

主播：新加坡将为主席国角色带来什么？

分析师：这次交接不仅仅是例行轮换。它标志着从一个正经历严重经济困境的主席国——菲律宾2.1%增长和7.8%通胀——过渡到一个处于经济实力巅峰的主席国，新加坡交出5.4%的GDP增长、创纪录的贸易顺差，以及在数字经济治理方面真正的全球领导地位。黄循财总理表示，新加坡2027年主席国任期将聚焦团结、一体化和区域韧性，延续DEFA的势头。新加坡的外交分量和经济信誉为其提供了比大多数主席国更强的平台来解决延续的未决紧张局势——南海争端、缅甸危机和与美国的贸易政策不确定性。但东盟基于共识的决策机制确保进展将保持渐进。马科斯总统在闭幕致辞中表示，改变方向"不等于迷失方向"——对一个在日益复杂的地缘政治格局中航行的集团来说，这是恰如其分的反思。
