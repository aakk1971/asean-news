PHILIPPINES · SUMMIT / 菲律宾 · 峰会

Manila set to host 49th ASEAN Summit as DEFA signing approaches and Trump confirms late arrival

13 November 2026

Verified reporting

The Philippines will host the 49th ASEAN Summit and Related Summits from 15 to 18 November 2026 at the Philippine International Convention Center (PICC) in Pasay City, Manila. The summit, held under the theme "Navigating Our Future, Together," will focus on peace and security, economic prosperity, and people empowerment. More than 17 world leaders have confirmed attendance, including US President Donald Trump and Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva.

The landmark Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA) — described as the world's first binding regional digital economy pact — is targeted for signing at the summit. ASEAN's Secretary-General has projected that the bloc's digital economy could reach US$2 trillion by 2030, and DEFA is the regulatory architecture intended to harmonise rules on cross-border data flows, digital payments, e-commerce, cybersecurity and AI governance across the now 11 member states, following Timor-Leste's formal admission in October 2025.

The Department of Foreign Affairs confirmed on 13 November that Trump's late arrival — expected half an hour before the closing ceremonies — will not affect the summit programme. ASEAN leaders have agreed to extend the summit schedule to accommodate bilateral meetings with the US President. The Philippines will formally hand over the ASEAN chairship to Singapore on 17 November. President Marcos is scheduled to hold bilateral meetings with the leaders of Australia, Brazil, Japan and South Korea on the summit sidelines.

Analysis · Desk assessment

The DEFA signing would represent the summit's most consequential economic deliverable, but the gap between aspiration and implementation is vast. ASEAN's current digital economy is estimated at roughly US$300 billion; reaching the US$2 trillion target by 2030 requires not just a signed agreement but harmonised regulations, cross-border payment infrastructure, and digital identity systems that do not yet exist at scale across the region's diverse economies. The presence of 17-plus world leaders — particularly Trump and Lula, representing the world's largest and fifth-largest economies — elevates this Manila summit into a venue for high-stakes bilateral diplomacy. Trump's tariff policies have reshaped trade flows across Southeast Asia, and the summit provides a meeting opportunity with ASEAN leaders navigating the US-China trade realignment in real time. The energy security agenda has taken on new urgency as oil prices remain elevated, squeezing ASEAN's net energy importers. The handover to Singapore — ASEAN's most economically advanced member and a hub for the AI hardware cycle — positions the bloc's 2027 agenda around digital and technology governance. Whether Manila delivers substance alongside spectacle will define the Philippines' legacy as ASEAN chair.

What we do not know

The final DEFA text and its binding provisions. Whether bilateral meetings at the summit margins will produce specific announcements. What language member states will agree on regarding the South China Sea. The security arrangements and potential protests in Manila. Whether all announced leaders will attend in person or send representatives.

马尼拉即将举办第49届东盟峰会，DEFA签署在即，特朗普确认延迟抵达

2026年11月13日

核实报道

分析 · 编辑部评估

INDONESIA · MARKETS / 印尼 · 市场

IHSG extends rally to 5,339 as IDX expands high-shareholder-concentration reform to 383 stocks

13 November 2026

Verified reporting

The Jakarta Composite Index (IHSG) closed 1.69 per cent higher at 5,339.4 on 13 November, extending the prior session's 2.66 per cent rally to mark a second consecutive day of gains. The LQ45 blue-chip index advanced 1.3 per cent. All sectors finished in positive territory on 12 November, led by the industrial sector at 4.53 per cent, and the momentum carried into Friday's session with the index opening at 5,265.67. Trading value on 12 November reached Rp11.98 trillion.

Separately, the Indonesia Stock Exchange (IDX) expanded its High Shareholder Concentration (HSC) reform, increasing the number of stocks classified as HSC from 83 to 383 — representing approximately 6.16 per cent of IHSG's market capitalisation. The expanded list took effect on 13 November. The HSC Committee revised its methodology by dropping the Rp5 trillion (approximately US$275.6 million) market capitalisation screening threshold, meaning all listed stocks are now assessed. A stock is classified as HSC when 10 or fewer shareholders control 92.5 per cent or more of its shares. IDX President Director Jeffrey Hendrik stated that sanctions will apply if inaccurate shareholding reports are found.

The Indonesian rupiah weakened 0.26 per cent to Rp18,216 per US dollar, near its record low of Rp18,279. Market analysts cited the positive MSCI rebalancing outcome, resolution of political uncertainty surrounding VP Gibran's resignation, and expectations of a guaranteed 4.5 million tonnes of rice supply before year-end as supporting factors. However, analysts also flagged expectations of a December US Federal Reserve rate hike and fears of further foreign capital outflows.

Analysis · Desk assessment

The two-day rally of approximately 4.4 per cent from the 5,115 level represents the IHSG's strongest consecutive-day performance in months, but context matters: the index is still down approximately 37 per cent year-to-date from its all-time high above 8,500, and the rally has not yet been accompanied by a decisive shift in foreign fund flows. The HSC expansion to 383 stocks is the IDX's most aggressive governance intervention in its history and is explicitly designed to address MSCI's transparency concerns ahead of the broader reclassification consultation that continues into 2027. By removing the market-cap threshold, the exchange is signalling that no stock is too large to be scrutinised for ownership concentration — a direct response to criticism that Indonesia's blue-chip index was inflated by thinly traded stocks with concentrated ownership. The risk is that the aggressive reform pace creates short-term liquidity disruption in the affected names, though the 6.16 per cent market-cap impact suggests the most systemically important stocks are not on the HSC list. The rupiah's continued weakness near record lows, despite the equity rally, indicates that currency markets are pricing in broader macroeconomic risks that the stock market's technical bounce has not yet addressed.

What we do not know

Whether the two-day rally represents a sustainable trend reversal or a technical bounce within a longer downtrend. The exact magnitude and timing of passive fund rebalancing flows associated with the MSCI changes effective 1 December. Whether the expanded HSC classification will trigger forced selling by institutional investors in the newly designated stocks. The impact of the rupiah's weakness on corporate earnings and import costs. Whether MSCI will view the HSC expansion favourably in its ongoing reclassification consultation.

VIETNAM · TRADE / 越南 · 贸易

Vietnam's trade turnover surpasses US$1 trillion for the first time in 10 months

13 November 2026

Verified reporting

Vietnam's total merchandise trade reached approximately US$1.01 trillion in the first 10 months of 2026, surpassing the trillion-dollar mark for the first time in the country's history — a tenfold increase from just over US$100 billion when Vietnam joined the WTO in 2007. Total trade grew 32.3 per cent year-on-year. Exports totalled US$499.24 billion, up 27.5 per cent, while imports reached US$511.3 billion, up 37.4 per cent. The trade balance shifted to a deficit of US$12.07 billion, compared with a surplus of approximately US$19.5 billion in the same period of 2025.

In October alone, total trade was US$122 billion, up 47.4 per cent year-on-year, with exports of US$64.67 billion and imports of US$57.36 billion, producing a monthly surplus of US$7.3 billion — the second consecutive monthly surplus. The foreign-invested sector dominated exports at US$405.3 billion (81.2 per cent of the total), up 33 per cent, while the domestic sector contributed US$93.9 billion (18.8 per cent), up 8.2 per cent. Thirty-seven export items exceeded US$1 billion, and eight exceeded US$10 billion.

The United States remained Vietnam's largest export market at US$159.4 billion, while China was the largest import source at US$212.46 billion. Registered foreign direct investment surged 76.4 per cent to US$50.36 billion, with disbursed FDI rising 12.1 per cent to US$21.07 billion. Deputy Prime Minister Nguyen Van Thang stated that achieving the full-year GDP growth target of 10 per cent would require approximately 12.5 per cent growth in the fourth quarter.

Analysis · Desk assessment

Vietnam's trillion-dollar trade milestone cements its position as Southeast Asia's most trade-intensive economy relative to GDP, but the composition reveals structural vulnerabilities that the headline obscures. The foreign-invested sector accounts for 81 per cent of exports, meaning Vietnam's trade success is overwhelmingly a story of multinational factory output rather than domestic industrial capacity. The shift from a US$19.5 billion surplus to a US$12 billion deficit reflects the rising cost of the manufacturing inputs that fuel the export machine — particularly from China, where Vietnam's import bill surged to US$212 billion, creating a deficit of nearly US$135 billion with its northern neighbour alone. This dependency means Vietnam's trade performance is essentially a function of the global electronics supply chain that runs through Chinese components into Vietnamese assembly plants and onward to US consumers. The FDI surge of 76 per cent is encouraging but also suggests that investors are front-loading commitments to lock in Vietnam's tariff advantages before potential US trade policy shifts. The 10 per cent GDP target requiring 12.5 per cent Q4 growth appears extremely ambitious, suggesting the government may be signalling aspirational rather than achievable goals.

What we do not know

Whether the trade deficit will persist or reverse in the final two months of 2026. The composition of the US$159.4 billion in US-bound exports and how much may be subject to tariff action. Whether the 76 per cent FDI surge reflects genuine new investment or re-registered commitments. The extent to which Vietnam's export growth is driven by volume increases versus price effects from the weaker dong. Whether the 10 per cent full-year GDP target is achievable.

越南10个月贸易额首次突破1万亿美元

2026年11月13日

核实报道

越南2026年前10个月商品贸易总额约达1.01万亿美元，历史上首次突破万亿美元大关——较2007年越南加入WTO时的1,000多亿美元增长了十倍。贸易总额同比增长32.3%。出口总计4,992.4亿美元，增长27.5%；进口达5,113亿美元，增长37.4%。贸易余额转为逆差120.7亿美元，而2025年同期为顺差约195亿美元。

仅10月份，贸易总额就达1,220亿美元，同比增长47.4%，其中出口646.7亿美元，进口573.6亿美元，月度顺差73亿美元——连续第二个月实现顺差。外资部门主导出口，达4,053亿美元（占总量的81.2%），增长33%；国内部门贡献939亿美元（18.8%），增长8.2%。37个出口商品超过10亿美元，8个超过100亿美元。

美国仍是越南最大出口市场，达1,594亿美元；中国是最大进口来源国，达2,124.6亿美元。注册外国直接投资飙升76.4%至503.6亿美元，实际利用外资增长12.1%至210.7亿美元。副总理阮文胜表示，实现全年10%的GDP增长目标需要第四季度约12.5%的增长。

分析 · 编辑部评估

越南的万亿美元贸易里程碑巩固了其作为东南亚贸易密集度最高经济体（相对于GDP）的地位，但结构揭示了标题所掩盖的脆弱性。外资部门占出口的81%，意味着越南的贸易成功在很大程度上是跨国工厂产出的故事，而非国内工业能力的故事。从195亿美元顺差转为120亿美元逆差，反映了为出口机器提供动力的制造业投入品成本上升——特别是来自中国的进口激增至2,124亿美元，仅与北方邻国就产生了近1,350亿美元的逆差。这种依赖意味着越南的贸易表现本质上是全球电子供应链的函数——中国零部件进入越南组装工厂，再出口到美国消费者。FDI飙升76%令人鼓舞，但也暗示投资者正在抢先锁定越南的关税优势，以应对潜在的美国贸易政策转变。10%的GDP目标需要第四季度12.5%的增长，这一目标看起来极为雄心勃勃，表明政府可能是在传递愿景性而非可实现的目标。

未知事项

贸易逆差是否会在2026年最后两个月持续或逆转。1,594亿美元对美出口的构成及其中有多少可能受到关税行动的影响。76%的FDI飙升反映的是真正的新投资还是重新登记的承诺。越南出口增长在多大程度上由数量增长驱动，而非越南盾走弱的价格效应。全年10%的GDP目标是否可以实现。

MALAYSIA · ECONOMY / 马来西亚 · 经济

Malaysia's economy grows 5.3% in Q3 2026, beats expectations as manufacturing and services lead

13 November 2026

Verified reporting

Malaysia's economy expanded 5.3 per cent year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026, according to advance estimates from the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM), moderating from 6.0 per cent in Q2 but coming in at the upper end of Bloomberg's 4.5 to 5.4 per cent forecast range and above UOB's 4.5 per cent projection. On a quarter-on-quarter basis, growth accelerated to 4.6 per cent from 1.9 per cent in Q2. Year-to-date growth for the first three quarters of 2026 stood at 5.5 per cent, compared with 4.8 per cent in the same period of 2025.

Manufacturing grew 7.5 per cent, driven by electrical, electronic and optical products, and petroleum, chemical, rubber and plastic products. Services expanded 5.4 per cent, led by wholesale and retail trade, information and communication, and finance and insurance. Construction grew 6.0 per cent, driven by non-residential buildings and specialised construction. Agriculture expanded 1.3 per cent on the back of oil palm and livestock, while mining and quarrying contracted 4.9 per cent due to lower crude oil and condensate output.

Malaysia's trade was projected to surge 27.2 per cent to RM3.9 trillion in 2026, after recording a historic RM3 trillion in 2025 — the 28th consecutive year of trade surplus. MATRADE has set a target to endeavour RM3 trillion in trade despite global turbulence. The IMF forecast Malaysia's GDP growth at 5.2 per cent for 2026, moderating to 4.7 per cent in 2027. Official Q3 GDP figures are due on 18 November.

Analysis · Desk assessment

Malaysia's 5.3 per cent Q3 growth confirms it as one of ASEAN's strongest performers this year, trailing only Vietnam's near-double-digit pace and comfortably ahead of Thailand's 2.4 to 2.7 per cent range. The manufacturing sector's 7.5 per cent expansion, driven by electronics and semiconductor-related products, underscores Malaysia's position as a key node in the global AI hardware supply chain — the same cycle that is lifting Singapore and, to a lesser extent, Thailand. The quarter-on-quarter acceleration to 4.6 per cent from 1.9 per cent is notable: it suggests the moderation in year-on-year growth reflects base effects from an exceptionally strong Q3 2025 rather than genuine deceleration. The mining contraction of 4.9 per cent is a structural headwind that Malaysia's diversification into electronics is progressively offsetting. The trade projection of RM3.9 trillion — a 27 per cent surge — is ambitious but consistent with the semiconductor supercycle that has boosted Malaysia's E&E exports. The critical vulnerability remains Malaysia's exposure to a potential US tariff escalation targeting ASEAN's role in China-plus-one supply chain rerouting, which could quickly dampen the trade outlook.

What we do not know

The official Q3 GDP figures due 18 November may revise the advance estimate. The impact of elevated oil prices on Malaysia's fiscal position given the mining sector contraction. Whether the electronics-driven manufacturing surge will sustain into Q4 as the global AI investment cycle matures. The extent to which Malaysia's trade growth is driven by re-exports of Chinese-origin goods, which could attract US tariff scrutiny.

马来西亚第三季度经济增长5.3%，制造业和服务业领涨，超出预期

2026年11月13日

核实报道

据马来西亚统计局（DOSM）预估数据，马来西亚经济2026年第三季度同比增长5.3%，较第二季度的6.0%有所放缓，但位于彭博4.5%至5.4%预测区间的上端，并高于大华银行4.5%的预测。环比增长加速至4.6%，第二季度为1.9%。2026年前三季度累计增长5.5%，2025年同期为4.8%。

分析 · 编辑部评估

马来西亚5.3%的第三季度增长确认其为今年东盟表现最强的经济体之一，仅落后于越南接近两位数的增速，舒适地领先于泰国2.4%至2.7%的区间。制造业7.5%的扩张由电子和半导体相关产品驱动，凸显了马来西亚作为全球AI硬件供应链关键节点的地位——同一周期也在提振新加坡以及在较小程度上提振泰国。环比增长从1.9%加速至4.6%值得注意：这表明同比增速放缓反映的是2025年第三季度超常强劲的基数效应，而非真正的减速。矿业4.9%的收缩是结构性逆风，马来西亚向电子行业的多元化正在逐步抵消这一影响。贸易预测3.9万亿令吉——飙升27%——雄心勃勃，但与提振马来西亚电子电气出口的半导体超级周期一致。关键脆弱性仍然是马来西亚面临美国可能升级关税的风险，目标是东盟在中国加一供应链重新路由中的角色，这可能迅速抑制贸易前景。

未知事项

THAILAND · ECONOMY / 泰国 · 经济

Thailand's GDP data overhaul lifts growth baseline to 2.7% as AI cycle brightens outlook but K-shaped risks persist

13 November 2026

Verified reporting

Thailand's Office of Industrial Economics updated the Manufacturing Production Index (MPI) to incorporate high-technology categories including laptops, optical transceivers and server UPS units, and adopted chain-linking methodology with annual weight updates. The revision resolved a longstanding discrepancy between strong export values and contracting manufacturing indicators, raising the 2026 baseline GDP growth projection from 2.4 per cent to 2.7 per cent. Growth in 2027 is forecast at 3.0 per cent, and ttb analytics projects that GDP could reach 3.7 to 4.0 per cent over the next five years under favourable policy execution.

Global demand for AI-related electronics and IT equipment has grown nearly fourfold over five to six years, and Thailand has positioned itself as a production hub for printed circuit boards, power supply units, hard disk drives and semiconductor parts, supported primarily by Taiwanese foreign direct investment. High-tech electronics production indices rose from 100 to above 150. The Joint Standing Committee on Commerce, Industry and Banking (JSCCIB) lifted its 2026 growth outlook to 2.4 to 2.6 per cent.

However, the Bank of Thailand governor warned that the economy exhibits a pronounced K-shaped recovery. While AI-linked electronics export at record volumes, textiles, traditional manufacturing, tourism businesses dependent on Chinese visitors, and regional SMEs continue to struggle. Approximately 76,000 registered firms — 16 per cent of active companies — are classified as zombie SMEs with stagnant revenues unable to cover their debt servicing costs. SMEs collectively employ roughly 12 million workers. The Thai-US Treasury yield spread is at its widest in two decades, creating capital outflow pressure and baht volatility.

Analysis · Desk assessment

Thailand's GDP revision is essentially a statistical correction that makes the official data match a reality the market had already priced in: the AI electronics cycle is generating genuine economic value that the old MPI — weighted toward legacy industries — was not capturing. The upgrade from 2.4 to 2.7 per cent is meaningful for Thailand's credibility with international investors, but it does not change the fundamental story, which is that Thailand's growth — even at the revised level — trails every major ASEAN peer except Myanmar and Laos. The K-shaped divergence is the most concerning feature of the Thai economy: a thin slice of high-tech manufacturing plugged into global supply chains is performing exceptionally well, while the broad domestic economy that employs most workers is stagnating or contracting. The 76,000 zombie SMEs representing 16 per cent of active firms and employing 12 million workers represent a structural crisis that monetary policy cannot solve — the Bank of Thailand has already cut rates to 1 per cent with limited effect on domestic demand. ttb analytics' expectation of rate increases to 1.50 to 1.75 per cent by end-2027 would squeeze the zombie SMEs further, potentially triggering a wave of closures that would test Thailand's social safety net. The AI bubble risk flagged by analysts is real: a sharp correction in global AI investment would hit Thailand's best-performing sector disproportionately hard.

What we do not know

Whether the MPI revision will prompt upward revisions to other macroeconomic indicators. The sustainability of Taiwanese FDI in electronics if geopolitical tensions escalate. The timeline for the zombie SME problem to translate into a broader credit event. Whether the Bank of Thailand will begin raising rates in 2027 as ttb analytics projects. The impact on Thai tourism of the shift in Chinese outbound travel patterns.

SINGAPORE · MONETARY POLICY / 新加坡 · 货币政策

MAS holds S$NEER policy band unchanged in dovish surprise as core inflation eases to 1.5–2.0%

13 November 2026

Verified reporting

The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) kept the rate of appreciation, width and centre of the Singapore dollar nominal effective exchange rate (S$NEER) policy band unchanged at its October 2026 review, surprising all 10 analysts polled by Reuters who had expected tightening. The policy band retains its appreciating slope, which the central bank characterised as a pause in tightening rather than an easing.

MAS narrowed its 2026 core inflation forecast to 1.5 to 2.0 per cent, with core and headline inflation expected to remain slightly elevated in the coming months before easing more noticeably from mid-2027 as import costs ebb. Risks to inflation remain tilted to the upside, mainly from a possible sharp, sustained rise in energy or food prices. Singapore's advance Q3 2026 GDP came in at 5.4 per cent year-on-year (beating the 5.0 per cent consensus) and 1.1 per cent quarter-on-quarter seasonally adjusted.

MAS expects the economy to expand at a firm pace in the near term, then moderate as the AI cycle slows and global financial conditions tighten. It projects AI-related activity to slow in the second half of 2027, with the positive output gap narrowing through that year. Downside risks include a larger-than-expected rise in interest rates or a sharper pullback in AI investment. The government is expected to publish its full 2026 and 2027 growth forecasts in November.

Analysis · Desk assessment

The MAS decision to hold steady — against unanimous expectations of tightening — is the most significant dovish surprise in Singapore monetary policy in recent years. It signals that the central bank is choosing patience over pre-emption, betting that the AI-driven inflation pressures will prove transitory as the cycle matures. This is a calculated gamble: Singapore's economy is growing at 5.4 per cent with a positive output gap, conditions that would normally warrant tighter policy. MAS is essentially making a forward-looking call that the AI boom will cool naturally by the second half of 2027, removing the need for additional tightening now. The upgraded full-year 2026 forecast of 4.5 to 5.5 per cent represents growth roughly double the rate initially anticipated — a remarkable outcome driven by the global AI and electronics hardware cycle that has supercharged Singapore's precision manufacturing and trade intermediation sectors. The weakness in accommodation and food services — at just 2.1 per cent in Q3 — is a reminder that the domestic consumption economy has not kept pace with export-oriented sectors. Energy remains the key swing factor: a renewed jump in crude linked to Gulf escalation could bring tightening back at the April 2027 review. The dovish hold may leave the Singapore dollar open to some softening as positions built for tightening unwind.

What we do not know

Whether MAS will tighten at the next review in April 2027 if inflation proves stickier than projected. The exact timeline for AI-related activity to moderate. Whether the dovish surprise will lead to meaningful Singapore dollar weakness. The full 2026 and 2027 government growth forecasts due in November. The impact of elevated oil prices on Singapore's inflation trajectory given MAS's explicit flagging of energy as a risk.
