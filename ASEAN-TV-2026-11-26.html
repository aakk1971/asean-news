Malaysia Smashes Trade Record as October Exports Surge 61.1% to RM236 Billion on Semiconductor Supercycle

马来西亚出口创纪录——10月出口飙升61.1%至2360亿令吉，半导体超级周期驱动

English presenter script

Lead presenter: Good evening. Our lead story tonight — Malaysia has shattered its trade records. October total trade hit RM436.61 billion, the first time the country has ever breached the RM400 billion threshold in a single month. Exports surged 61.1 per cent year-on-year to RM236.22 billion, while imports rose 23.5 per cent to RM200.39 billion. The trade surplus widened dramatically to RM35.83 billion. For the year through October, total trade stands at RM3.17 trillion, already exceeding the full-year 2025 total of RM2.86 trillion with two months still to go.

Analyst: These are genuinely extraordinary numbers. A 61.1 per cent year-on-year export surge for a US$400 billion economy is remarkable by any standard, and the composition tells the story: electrical and electronic products accounted for the lion's share, driven by the global semiconductor supercycle and AI-related demand. Malaysia produces roughly 13 per cent of the world's semiconductor test and packaging output, and the country has become a critical node in the AI chip supply chain — advanced packaging, back-end testing, and data centre equipment all flow through Malaysian facilities. The E&E sector's exports have been accelerating quarter after quarter as hyperscale operators expand their infrastructure. What makes this particularly significant is the breadth: it is not just one product category or one destination. Exports to China, the United States, Singapore, and the EU all posted strong gains. The RM35.83 billion trade surplus in a single month provides a substantial current account cushion and supports the ringgit at a time when several regional currencies are under pressure.

Lead presenter: Is this sustainable, or is it a one-off spike?

Analyst: There are arguments on both sides. In favour of sustainability: global AI capital expenditure continues to accelerate, with hyperscale operators committed to multi-year buildout programmes. TSMC, Intel, and Infineon are all expanding their Malaysian operations, suggesting structural rather than cyclical demand. The government's National Semiconductor Strategy, which aims to capture US$120 billion in semiconductor investment by 2030, is gaining traction. Against sustainability: a 61 per cent growth rate inevitably includes some front-loading effects and base-year distortions. If the AI cycle moderates or if global trade tensions escalate — particularly any expansion of US export controls on advanced chips — Malaysia's concentrated exposure to the semiconductor supply chain becomes a vulnerability. Investment and Trade Minister Tengku Zafrul has set a target of RM3.5 trillion in total trade for 2026. With RM3.17 trillion already in the books through October, that target now looks conservative. The critical question is whether Malaysia can convert this trade windfall into permanent industrial upgrading — moving from packaging and testing into higher-value design and fabrication activities.

主播：这种增长可持续吗，还是一次性飙升？

分析师：正反两方面都有论据。支持可持续的一面：全球AI资本支出继续加速，超大规模运营商已承诺多年建设计划。台积电、英特尔和英飞凌均在扩大马来西亚业务，表明这是结构性而非周期性需求。政府旨在到2030年吸引1,200亿美元半导体投资的国家半导体战略正在取得进展。不利的一面：61%的增长率不可避免地包含一些前置效应和基期扭曲。如果AI周期放缓或全球贸易紧张升级——特别是美国对先进芯片出口管制的任何扩展——马来西亚对半导体供应链的集中暴露将成为脆弱性。投资贸易部长东姑扎弗鲁设定了2026年贸易总额3.5万亿令吉的目标。截至10月已有3.17万亿令吉入账，该目标现在看来保守。关键问题是马来西亚能否将这一贸易红利转化为永久性产业升级——从封装测试向更高价值的设计和制造活动迈进。

English presenter script

Lead presenter: To Vietnam now — where foreign direct investment is on a record-breaking trajectory. Through the first ten months of 2026, registered FDI reached US$54.85 billion, surging 74 per cent year-on-year. This is the highest level since 2008 and puts Vietnam on course to comfortably exceed its full-year target. Disbursed FDI — the money actually spent on the ground — reached US$19.6 billion, up 3.1 per cent. The gap between registered and disbursed capital reflects the multi-year nature of large industrial projects.

Analyst: The 74 per cent surge in registered FDI is a headline grabber, but the underlying dynamics are what matter. Vietnam is capturing an outsized share of the China-plus-one reallocation that has been reshaping Asian supply chains since 2018. The country offers a compelling combination: labour costs roughly one-third of coastal China, a young and increasingly skilled workforce, proximity to the Chinese supply base, and an extensive network of free trade agreements including the CPTPP and RCEP. The manufacturing sector dominates inflows, with electronics, semiconductors, and EV battery components leading. Samsung alone accounts for a significant share — its Vietnamese operations now produce approximately half of all Samsung smartphones globally. What is new in 2026 is the emergence of AI-related investment: data centre construction, cloud infrastructure, and semiconductor packaging facilities are attracting capital from the United States, South Korea, Japan, and increasingly from China as Chinese firms seek to establish production outside China.

Lead presenter: How does this connect to Vietnam's 10 per cent GDP growth target for 2027?

Analyst: This is the critical link. The National Assembly's unprecedented 10 per cent growth target for 2027 requires total social investment of US$228 to 243 billion, of which FDI disbursement of US$30 to 32 billion is a core component. If registered FDI continues at the current pace through the remainder of 2026, Vietnam will enter 2027 with a substantial pipeline of committed projects waiting to disburse. The conversion rate from registered to disbursed FDI historically runs at approximately 35 to 40 per cent in any given year, so US$54 billion in registrations should translate into roughly US$19 to 22 billion in annual disbursements over the following two to three years. That gets Vietnam most of the way to the US$30 billion target, but achieving 10 per cent GDP growth requires much more than FDI alone — it requires domestic private investment, infrastructure spending, and productivity gains all firing simultaneously. The risk is that the gap between ambitious targets and execution capacity widens, particularly in infrastructure where land clearance, permitting, and construction bottlenecks have historically slowed project delivery. Vietnam's absorption capacity — its ability to actually convert capital inflows into productive output — will be tested as never before.

主播：转向越南——外商直接投资正以破纪录的轨迹增长。2026年前十个月，注册外资达548.5亿美元，同比飙升74%。这是2008年以来的最高水平，使越南有望轻松超过全年目标。实际到位外资——实际投入的资金——达196亿美元，增长3.1%。注册资本与实际到位资本之间的差距反映了大型工业项目的多年期性质。

分析师：注册外资74%的飙升是个引人注目的标题，但真正重要的是其背后的动态。越南正在获取自2018年以来重塑亚洲供应链的中国加一战略中不成比例的份额。该国提供了引人注目的组合：劳动力成本约为中国沿海地区的三分之一、年轻且技能日益提高的劳动力、靠近中国供应基地，以及包括CPTPP和RCEP在内的广泛自贸协定网络。制造业主导资金流入，电子、半导体和电动车电池组件领先。仅三星就占据了相当大的份额——其越南工厂现在生产全球约一半的三星智能手机。2026年的新变化是AI相关投资的兴起：数据中心建设、云基础设施和半导体封装设施正吸引来自美国、韩国、日本，以及越来越多来自中国的资本，因为中国企业寻求在中国以外建立生产基地。

主播：这与越南2027年10%的GDP增长目标有何关联？

分析师：这是关键联系。国会史无前例的2027年10%增长目标需要2,280亿至2,430亿美元的社会投资总额，其中FDI到位300亿至320亿美元是核心组成部分。如果注册外资在2026年剩余时间保持当前速度，越南将带着大量已承诺等待到位的项目储备进入2027年。注册到到位外资的转化率历来在任何一年约为35%至40%，因此548亿美元的注册额应在未来两到三年转化为约190亿至220亿美元的年度到位额。这使越南接近300亿美元的目标，但实现10%的GDP增长不仅需要外资——还需要国内私人投资、基础设施支出和生产率提升同时发力。风险在于雄心勃勃的目标与执行能力之间的差距扩大，特别是在基础设施领域，征地、审批和建设瓶颈历来拖累项目交付。越南的吸收能力——将资本流入实际转化为生产性产出的能力——将面临前所未有的考验。

English presenter script

Lead presenter: To the Philippines now — where a rare public intervention from the central bank governor has underscored the severity of the country's economic predicament. BSP Governor Eli Remolona has explicitly called for fiscal stimulus to support the weakening economy, stating that monetary policy alone cannot resolve what he described as a supply-side inflation problem combined with slowing demand. This comes as October CPI remains at 7.8 per cent while Q3 GDP growth decelerated to 2.1 per cent year-on-year — the worst combination of high inflation and low growth in ASEAN.

Analyst: Governor Remolona's statement is extraordinary because central bankers almost never publicly call for fiscal stimulus — it crosses the conventional boundary between monetary and fiscal policy. The fact that he has done so signals that the BSP has concluded its own toolkit is insufficient. And the data supports that conclusion. The BSP has raised rates by 125 basis points in 2026, bringing the benchmark to 5.50 per cent, yet inflation continues to accelerate — from 4.3 per cent in January to 7.8 per cent in October. This confirms that the inflation is primarily supply-driven: rice prices are up 20.7 per cent, transport fares are surging from fuel cost pass-throughs, and typhoon damage has disrupted agricultural supply chains. Rate hikes cannot increase rice production or repair typhoon-damaged infrastructure. Meanwhile, the rate hikes are suppressing the demand side of the economy — consumer spending is weakening, business investment is contracting, and the PSEi has fallen approximately 28 per cent year-to-date as capital flees to higher-yielding or higher-growth markets.

Lead presenter: What kind of fiscal stimulus would help?

Analyst: Governor Remolona pointed specifically to supply-side interventions: agricultural productivity investment, irrigation and cold chain infrastructure, food import facilitation, and targeted subsidies for transport operators. These address the root causes of inflation rather than trying to suppress its symptoms through higher borrowing costs. The challenge is fiscal space. The Philippine government's debt-to-GDP ratio stands at approximately 61 per cent, and the fiscal deficit is running at about 5.7 per cent of GDP. Rating agencies have warned that sustained fiscal expansion without a credible consolidation plan could trigger a credit outlook revision. The Philippines is now the clearest example in ASEAN of a policy dilemma: the central bank cannot cut rates because inflation is too high, cannot raise rates further without crushing an already weak economy, and is now asking the government to do what monetary policy cannot. If the fiscal response is too slow or too small, the stagflation trap deepens. The World Bank and IMF have both emphasised that structural reform in agriculture — not monetary tightening — is the solution to Philippine food price inflation. Whether the Marcos administration can deliver those reforms before the 2028 midterm elections is the key political-economic question.

中文主播稿

主播：转向菲律宾——央行行长罕见的公开干预凸显了该国经济困境的严重性。BSP行长伊莱·雷莫洛纳明确呼吁财政刺激以支撑疲弱的经济，表示货币政策无法单独解决他所描述的供给侧通胀问题叠加需求放缓的局面。此时10月CPI仍高达7.8%，而第三季度GDP同比增长降至2.1%——东盟中高通胀与低增长的最糟糕组合。

分析师：雷莫洛纳行长的声明非同寻常，因为央行行长几乎从不公开呼吁财政刺激——这跨越了货币政策和财政政策之间的传统界限。他这样做表明BSP已经得出结论：自身工具箱不足。数据支持这一结论。BSP在2026年已加息125个基点，将基准利率升至5.50%，但通胀继续加速——从1月的4.3%升至10月的7.8%。这确认通胀主要是供给驱动的：大米价格上涨20.7%，交通费用因燃料成本传导而飙升，台风破坏了农业供应链。加息无法增加大米产量或修复台风损坏的基础设施。与此同时，加息正在抑制经济的需求端——消费支出走弱、商业投资收缩、PSEi年初至今下跌约28%，资本逃向更高收益或更高增长的市场。

主播：什么样的财政刺激会有帮助？

分析师：雷莫洛纳行长特别指向供给侧干预：农业生产力投资、灌溉和冷链基础设施、食品进口便利化，以及对运输经营者的定向补贴。这些措施针对通胀的根源，而非试图通过提高借贷成本来压制其症状。挑战在于财政空间。菲律宾政府债务占GDP比率约为61%，财政赤字约为GDP的5.7%。评级机构警告称，如果没有可信的整顿计划而持续财政扩张，可能触发信用展望修订。菲律宾现在是东盟中政策困境最明显的例子：央行因通胀过高而无法降息，不能在不压垮本已疲弱经济的情况下进一步加息，现在要求政府做货币政策无法做到的事。如果财政反应太慢或力度太小，滞胀陷阱将加深。世界银行和国际货币基金组织都强调，农业结构性改革——而非货币紧缩——才是菲律宾食品价格通胀的解决方案。马科斯政府能否在2028年中期选举前推动这些改革，是关键的政治经济问题。

English presenter script

Lead presenter: To Indonesia now — where the Daya Anagata Nusantara sovereign wealth fund, known as Danantara, has disclosed the deployment of Rp162 trillion — approximately US$10 billion — across energy, food security, and hajj pilgrimage infrastructure. The fund, which was established under President Prabowo to consolidate state-owned enterprise investments, has moved rapidly from formation to deployment, with projects spanning waste-to-energy facilities, agricultural partnerships, and pilgrim accommodation. CEO Rosan Roeslani outlined the fund's strategy at a recent investor briefing.

Analyst: Danantara represents the most ambitious restructuring of Indonesian state capitalism since the Suharto era. By consolidating the government's stakes in major SOEs — including Bank Mandiri, BRI, Telkom, and Pertamina — under a single holding entity, the fund aims to unlock value through coordinated investment and professional management. The Rp162 trillion deployment figure signals that Danantara is moving faster than many analysts expected. The energy allocation is the largest component, centred on waste-to-energy projects that serve a dual purpose: addressing Indonesia's urban waste crisis — Jakarta alone generates approximately 7,500 tonnes of waste daily — while generating baseload electricity. The food security pillar includes a partnership with Brazil's JBS, the world's largest meat processor, to develop domestic protein production capacity. This is strategically significant because Indonesia imports approximately US$3 billion in food annually, and reducing that dependency is a core policy objective.

Lead presenter: What about the hajj component?

Analyst: The hajj investment is uniquely Indonesian. As the world's largest Muslim-majority nation, Indonesia sends approximately 220,000 pilgrims to Saudi Arabia annually, and the hajj waiting list exceeds 4 million people with wait times of 20 to 30 years. Danantara is investing in accommodation and logistics infrastructure in Saudi Arabia to reduce per-pilgrim costs and potentially increase the annual quota. This is both economically rational — hajj-related spending is a significant foreign exchange outflow — and politically strategic, given the importance of hajj access to Indonesian voters. The broader question is governance. Sovereign wealth funds succeed or fail based on investment discipline, transparency, and independence from political interference. Singapore's GIC and Temasek are the regional benchmarks, and both took decades to build their reputations. Danantara is deploying capital at scale while simultaneously building its institutional framework — a sequencing that creates execution risk. The fund's credibility will ultimately depend on whether its investments generate commercial returns or become vehicles for politically directed spending. International investors are watching closely, particularly given the MSCI reclassification consultation that is running in parallel and has raised questions about Indonesian capital market governance.

中文主播稿

主播：转向印尼——主权财富基金Daya Anagata Nusantara（即Danantara）披露已部署162万亿印尼盾——约100亿美元——用于能源、粮食安全和朝觐基础设施。该基金由普拉博沃总统领导下成立，旨在整合国有企业投资，已从组建迅速进入部署阶段，项目涵盖垃圾焚烧发电设施、农业合作和朝觐住宿。首席执行官罗桑·鲁斯拉尼在近期投资者简报会上概述了基金战略。

分析师：Danantara代表了苏哈托时代以来印尼国家资本主义最雄心勃勃的重组。通过将政府在主要国企——包括曼迪利银行、BRI、电信公司Telkom和国家石油公司Pertamina——的股份整合到单一控股实体下，该基金旨在通过协调投资和专业管理释放价值。162万亿印尼盾的部署数字表明Danantara的行动速度快于许多分析师的预期。能源配置是最大组成部分，以垃圾焚烧发电项目为核心，具有双重目的：解决印尼城市垃圾危机——仅雅加达每天产生约7,500吨垃圾——同时产生基荷电力。粮食安全支柱包括与巴西JBS（全球最大肉类加工商）的合作，以发展国内蛋白质生产能力。这具有战略意义，因为印尼每年进口约30亿美元的食品，减少这一依赖是核心政策目标。

主播：朝觐部分呢？

分析师：朝觐投资具有独特的印尼特色。作为世界上最大的穆斯林占多数的国家，印尼每年向沙特阿拉伯派遣约22万名朝觐者，朝觐等候名单超过400万人，等待时间为20至30年。Danantara正在投资沙特的住宿和物流基础设施，以降低每位朝觐者的成本并可能增加年度配额。这既在经济上合理——朝觐相关支出是重大外汇流出——又具有政治战略意义，鉴于朝觐对印尼选民的重要性。更广泛的问题是治理。主权财富基金的成败取决于投资纪律、透明度和独立于政治干预。新加坡的GIC和淡马锡是区域基准，两者都花了数十年建立声誉。Danantara在大规模部署资本的同时建设制度框架——这种顺序安排带来执行风险。该基金的信誉最终取决于其投资是否产生商业回报，还是成为政治导向支出的工具。国际投资者正密切关注，特别是鉴于同时进行的MSCI重新分类磋商已对印尼资本市场治理提出质疑。

HDB Resale Market Hits All-Time Record of 238 Million-Dollar Flats in October as Highest Price Reaches S$1.818 Million

新加坡组屋转售市场10月创历史纪录——238套百万组屋成交，最高价达181.8万新元

English presenter script

Lead presenter: To Singapore now — where the public housing market has reached a milestone that would have been unthinkable a decade ago. In October 2026, a record 238 HDB resale flats changed hands at prices of one million Singapore dollars or more. The highest transaction was a five-room flat in Queenstown that sold for S$1.818 million. These numbers shatter the previous monthly record and confirm a structural shift in Singapore's public housing market — once the emblem of affordable homeownership.

Analyst: The 238 million-dollar transactions in a single month represent an extraordinary acceleration. To put this in context, the entire year of 2020 saw only 82 million-dollar HDB resale transactions — October 2026 alone nearly tripled that full-year figure in 30 days. Several factors are converging. First, the BTO supply lag: new Build-to-Order flats take four to five years to complete, and the pipeline shortfall from COVID-era construction delays means buyers who cannot wait are competing in the resale market. Second, the AI-driven economic boom has lifted household incomes, particularly in the technology and financial services sectors, pushing purchasing power higher. Third, the upgrader dynamic: families who bought BTO flats a decade ago at S$300,000 to 400,000 are selling into a market where comparable units fetch S$700,000 to over a million, then rolling profits into larger or better-located resale units. The S$1.818 million Queenstown flat is in a mature estate with excellent MRT connectivity — location premiums for well-connected estates are compressing toward private condo prices.

Lead presenter: What does this mean for housing affordability?

Analyst: This is the central policy tension. HDB was designed as Singapore's universal affordable housing programme — approximately 80 per cent of the resident population lives in HDB flats. When public housing routinely trades above a million dollars, the affordability proposition is fundamentally challenged for younger Singaporeans entering the market, particularly those without parental financial support. The government has responded with measures: higher income ceilings for BTO eligibility, increased grants for first-time buyers, cooling measures including longer minimum occupation periods, and a ramp-up of BTO supply to 23,000 units per year. But these supply-side responses take years to materialise. The demand-side pressure is immediate: with Singapore's GDP growing at 6 per cent, employment at record levels, and the AI sector generating high-salary jobs, the structural demand for housing near employment centres will continue to push prices higher. The risk is a generational divide: existing homeowners benefit from capital appreciation while younger entrants face progressively higher barriers. National Development Minister Desmond Lee has signalled that additional cooling measures are under consideration if the trend continues. The question is whether any politically acceptable measure can meaningfully restrain prices when the fundamental driver is genuine economic prosperity and limited land supply.

中文主播稿

主播：转向新加坡——公共住房市场达到了十年前难以想象的里程碑。2026年10月，创纪录的238套HDB转售组屋以100万新元或以上的价格成交。最高交易价为皇后镇一套五房式组屋的181.8万新元。这些数字打破了此前的月度纪录，确认了新加坡公共住房市场的结构性转变——曾经是可负担住房的象征。

分析师：单月238宗百万交易代表着非凡的加速。作为对比，2020年全年仅有82宗百万HDB转售交易——2026年10月一个月就几乎是那个全年数字的三倍。多个因素正在汇聚。首先是BTO供应滞后：预购组屋需要四到五年才能完工，新冠时期建设延误造成的管线缺口意味着等不了的买家正在转售市场竞争。其次，AI驱动的经济繁荣提高了家庭收入，特别是在科技和金融服务行业，推动购买力上升。第三是升级动态：十年前以30万至40万新元购买BTO的家庭正以70万至超过100万新元的价格出售，然后将利润投入更大或更好地段的转售单位。181.8万新元的皇后镇组屋位于成熟社区，MRT连接优越——交通便利社区的地段溢价正在向私人公寓价格靠拢。

主播：这对住房可负担性意味着什么？

分析师：这是核心政策张力。HDB被设计为新加坡的全民可负担住房计划——约80%的居民人口居住在组屋中。当公共住房交易价格经常超过百万新元时，对进入市场的年轻新加坡人来说，可负担性主张受到根本挑战，特别是那些没有父母财务支持的人。政府已出台措施：提高BTO资格收入上限、增加首次购房者补助金、包括延长最短居住期在内的降温措施，以及将BTO供应量提升至每年23,000套。但这些供给侧应对需要数年才能实现。需求端压力是即时的：随着新加坡GDP增长6%、就业处于历史最高水平、AI行业产生高薪工作，对就业中心附近住房的结构性需求将继续推高价格。风险在于代际分化：现有业主从资本增值中受益，而年轻进入者面临逐渐升高的门槛。国家发展部长李智陞暗示，如果趋势持续，正在考虑额外的降温措施。问题是，当根本驱动力是真正的经济繁荣和有限的土地供应时，是否有任何政治上可接受的措施能有效抑制价格。

Bank of Thailand Projects Inflation Briefly Turning Negative in 2027 as GDP Forecast Cut to 2.4% and Rate Held at 1.00%

English presenter script

Lead presenter: And finally to Thailand — where the Bank of Thailand has delivered a sobering assessment of the economic outlook. At its November meeting, the monetary policy committee voted 6 to 1 to hold the benchmark rate at 1.00 per cent, but the accompanying projections contained a striking forecast: headline inflation is expected to briefly turn negative in 2027, pushing Thailand to the edge of deflation. The 2026 GDP growth forecast was maintained at 2.7 per cent, but the 2027 projection was cut from 2.4 per cent to 2.1 per cent, citing weaker tourism, sluggish domestic consumption, and the impact of global trade uncertainty.

Analyst: The deflation projection is extraordinary for an ASEAN economy and highlights just how far Thailand has diverged from its regional peers. While the Philippines is battling 7.8 per cent inflation and Malaysia is enjoying growth-with-price-stability, Thailand is confronting the opposite problem: insufficient demand to sustain even modest price increases. The drivers are structural. Domestic consumption has been persistently weak, weighed down by elevated household debt — now approximately 90 per cent of GDP, the highest in ASEAN and among the highest in emerging markets globally. Thai consumers are over-leveraged, particularly in auto loans and credit cards, and the debt overhang is suppressing spending even as nominal incomes slowly recover. Tourism, which historically contributed 15 to 20 per cent of GDP through direct and indirect channels, has not recovered to pre-pandemic levels and the revised 2026 target of 31.5 million arrivals falls short of the 40 million peak in 2019.

Lead presenter: Why is the BOT not cutting rates further?

Analyst: This is the central dilemma. The 6 to 1 hold vote — with only one member dissenting in favour of a cut — reveals that the majority of the committee believes further easing carries more risk than reward. At 1.00 per cent, the policy rate is already historically low. Cutting further would widen the rate differential with the United States — currently approximately 3 percentage points — accelerating capital outflows and putting additional pressure on the baht, which has already depreciated 6.7 per cent against the dollar year-to-date. It could also fuel asset bubbles in property and equities without translating into productive investment. The BOT has consistently argued that Thailand's problems are structural, not cyclical: low productivity growth, an ageing population, inadequate investment in education and technology, and an agricultural sector that employs 30 per cent of the workforce but generates only 8 per cent of GDP. Governor Vitai Ratanakorn has stated that interest rates alone cannot solve these problems. The uncomfortable reality is that Thailand needs fiscal reform and structural transformation — the kind of deep changes that require political consensus and years of implementation. At 2.1 per cent projected growth for 2027, Thailand risks falling into a low-growth trap while its ASEAN neighbours accelerate ahead at 5 to 6 per cent.

中文主播稿

主播：最后转向泰国——泰国央行对经济前景作出了令人警醒的评估。在11月会议上，货币政策委员会以6比1投票维持基准利率在1.00%不变，但附带预测包含了一个引人注目的判断：预计2027年整体通胀将短暂转负，将泰国推至通缩边缘。2026年GDP增长预测维持在2.7%，但2027年预测从2.4%下调至2.1%，理由是旅游业疲弱、国内消费低迷以及全球贸易不确定性的影响。

分析师：通缩预测对东盟经济体来说非同寻常，凸显了泰国与区域同行的巨大分化。在菲律宾与7.8%的通胀作斗争、马来西亚享受增长与价格稳定并存之际，泰国正面临相反的问题：需求不足以维持哪怕温和的物价上涨。驱动因素是结构性的。国内消费持续疲弱，被高企的家庭债务拖累——目前约占GDP的90%，东盟最高且在全球新兴市场中名列前茅。泰国消费者过度负债，特别是在汽车贷款和信用卡方面，债务过重正在抑制支出，即使名义收入缓慢恢复。旅游业通过直接和间接渠道历来贡献GDP的15%至20%，但尚未恢复至疫情前水平，修订后的2026年目标3,150万人次低于2019年4,000万的峰值。

主播：为什么泰国央行不进一步降息？

分析师：这是核心困境。6比1的维持投票——只有一名成员持降息异议——显示委员会多数认为进一步宽松的风险大于收益。在1.00%的水平上，政策利率已处于历史低位。进一步降息将扩大与美国的利差——目前约3个百分点——加速资本外流并对泰铢施加额外压力，泰铢年初至今已兑美元贬值6.7%。这还可能助长房地产和股市资产泡沫而不会转化为生产性投资。泰国央行一直认为泰国的问题是结构性的，而非周期性的：生产率增长低下、人口老龄化、教育和技术投资不足，以及一个雇用30%劳动力但仅产生8%GDP的农业部门。行长维泰·拉达纳功表示利率无法解决这些问题。令人不安的现实是，泰国需要财政改革和结构性转型——这种深层变革需要政治共识和多年实施。在2027年预计2.1%的增长率下，泰国面临陷入低增长陷阱的风险，而其东盟邻国正以5%至6%的速度加速前进。
