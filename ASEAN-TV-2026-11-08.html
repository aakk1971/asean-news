Good evening. We begin in Singapore with this report. The Monetary Authority of Singapore on 3 November formally established the Future of Finance Institute (FFI), a dedicated body tasked with governing the deployment of artificial intelligence across the city-state's financial sector. The institute will operate under three core pillars — PathFin.ai for industry roadmapping, BuildFin.ai for technology development, and SafeFin.ai for risk governance — each designed to ensure that Singapore's financial institutions adopt AI responsibly while maintaining competitive advantage.

MAS said the FFI will initially focus on agentic AI governance, addressing the rapid proliferation of autonomous AI systems that can execute multi-step financial transactions without continuous human oversight. The institute plans to issue its first set of guidelines for agentic AI deployment in banking by mid-2027, covering areas such as algorithmic accountability, model explainability requirements, and consumer protection safeguards for AI-driven advisory services.

Our analysis: The FFI represents Singapore's attempt to stay ahead of both innovation and regulation simultaneously — a difficult balance that few jurisdictions have managed. By creating dedicated pillars for development and safety, MAS avoids the common trap of regulation lagging technology by years. The agentic AI focus is particularly forward-looking; most regulators worldwide have not yet addressed autonomous AI systems in finance. This positions Singapore to export its governance framework regionally, potentially becoming the de facto standard for ASEAN financial AI regulation.
