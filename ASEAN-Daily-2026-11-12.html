MSCI rebalancing reshapes Indonesia index as IHSG rallies

12 November 2026

Verified reporting

MSCI's November 2026 rebalancing removed Barito Pacific (BRPT) from the Global Standard Index and downgraded Bumi Resources Minerals (BRMS) to the Small Cap Index. Seven stocks exited the Small Cap tier entirely: Avia Avian (AVIA), Ciputra Development (CTRA), Dayamitra Telekomunikasi (MTEL), Elang Mahkota Teknologi (EMTK), Jasa Marga (JSMR), Rukun Raharja (RAJA) and Smartfren Telecom (WIFI).

Indonesia's four largest banks — BCA (BBCA), BRI (BBRI), Bank Mandiri (BMRI) and BNI (BBNI) — were retained and together accounted for 71.81% of the MSCI Indonesia index weight. Across all tiers, 35 Indonesian stocks remained in MSCI indices. Changes take effect 1 December 2026; the next scheduled review is 9 February 2027.

On the session following the announcement, the Jakarta Composite Index (IHSG) closed at 5,250.89, rising 135.86 points or 2.66%. Foreign investors recorded a net buy of Rp 510.51 billion. Estimated passive fund outflows linked to the rebalancing were approximately Rp 980 billion.

Year to date, the IHSG has fallen roughly 39%, and foreign investors have recorded cumulative net selling of approximately Rp 127 trillion.

Analysis · Desk assessment

The retention of the big-four banks at nearly three-quarters of index weight means the MSCI Indonesia benchmark is now more concentrated than before. If passive funds tracking the index mechanically sell the removed names, liquidity in mid-cap stocks could thin further — particularly for those seven that left the Small Cap tier entirely and lose index-linked demand altogether. The estimated Rp 980 billion in passive outflows is modest relative to IHSG daily turnover, but concentrated selling in individual small-cap names could move prices disproportionately. The 2.66% rally in IHSG the same session suggests the market read the retention of the big four as the dominant signal, but whether that bounce holds depends on whether foreign selling — running at Rp 127 trillion year to date — pauses or continues.

What we do not know

The exact timeline and magnitude of passive fund rebalancing flows. Whether active managers will follow passive benchmarks in exiting removed names. How the affected companies' capital-raising plans may be impacted by index exclusion. Whether MSCI will apply additional changes at the February 2027 review.

被动基金再平衡资金流的确切时间表和规模。主动型基金经理是否会跟随被动基准退出被移除的股票。受影响公司的融资计划可能如何受到指数剔除的影响。MSCI是否会在2027年2月审查中作出进一步调整。

Vice-President Gibran offers resignation amid diploma dispute

12 November 2026

Verified reporting

Vice-President Gibran Rakabuming Raka offered his resignation to President Prabowo Subianto on 10–11 November 2026 amid an ongoing dispute over his academic qualifications. The State Secretary confirmed receipt of the offer. Approximately 50 lawmakers had been pushing a parliamentary motion related to the diploma controversy.

Gibran, the eldest son of former President Joko Widodo (Jokowi), became Vice-President in October 2024. Indonesia's constitutional process for replacing a vice-president has never been tested in practice.

Analysis · Desk assessment

The resignation offer opens constitutional territory Indonesia has not navigated before. The 1945 Constitution provides a replacement mechanism — the MPR (People's Consultative Assembly) would select a new vice-president from candidates nominated by the president — but the process has never been exercised. The parliamentary motion involving roughly 50 lawmakers suggests the political pressure was building beyond what could be managed quietly, though 50 out of 580 DPR members is well short of a formal majority. Whether Prabowo accepts the resignation, and how quickly the MPR would convene if he does, will determine whether this becomes a drawn-out institutional test or a contained political transition. Markets may price in uncertainty if the process stalls.

What we do not know

Whether President Prabowo has accepted or will accept the resignation. The precise constitutional steps and timeline for replacing a vice-president, given no precedent exists. Whether the parliamentary motion will proceed regardless of the resignation offer. The political implications for Jokowi's broader network and coalition dynamics.

Singapore GDP growth eases to 5.4% in Q3 as MTI lifts full-year forecast

12 November 2026

Verified reporting

Singapore's economy grew 5.4% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, easing from 6.2% in Q2. On a quarter-on-quarter seasonally adjusted basis, growth was 1.1%, down from 1.5% in the preceding quarter.

By sector: construction expanded 8.8%, trade and transport 8.0%, manufacturing 7.5%, information and communications and finance 4.3%, and accommodation and food services 2.1%.

The Ministry of Trade and Industry (MTI) upgraded its full-year 2026 GDP growth forecast to 4.5–5.5%, from the previous range of 2–4%. The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) held the S$NEER policy band unchanged at its October review. Brent crude oil was trading at approximately US$106 per barrel.

Analysis · Desk assessment

The sequential slowdown from 6.2% to 5.4% year on year, and from 1.5% to 1.1% quarter on quarter, suggests the growth pace is moderating but remains well above trend. The MTI's decision to narrow and raise the forecast range to 4.5–5.5% signals confidence that full-year performance will land comfortably above the original 2–4% band. Construction at 8.8% likely reflects ongoing public infrastructure and housing projects, while trade and transport at 8.0% benefits from the global trade cycle. MAS holding the S$NEER band unchanged at the October review suggests the central bank sees no urgency to tighten or ease, consistent with growth that is strong but decelerating. Oil at $106 per barrel adds to imported cost pressures but has not yet prompted a policy response.

What we do not know

Whether the Q4 data will confirm a continued deceleration or a rebound. How the elevated oil price will feed through to consumer inflation in coming quarters. Whether MAS will adjust the S$NEER band at its next review. The breakdown of trade and transport growth between re-exports, domestic exports and logistics services.

THAILAND · MONETARY POLICY / 泰国 · 货币政策

Bank of Thailand holds policy rate at 1% for fourth consecutive meeting

12 November 2026

Verified reporting

The Bank of Thailand's Monetary Policy Committee held the policy interest rate at 1.00% at its November meeting, the fourth consecutive hold. The decision was unanimous among the six members attending; one member was absent.

The committee revised its GDP growth forecasts: 2026 was raised to 2.4% from 2.3%, and 2027 was raised to 2.1% from 1.8%. Headline inflation forecasts were lowered: 2026 to 2.1% from 2.8%, and 2027 to 1.3% from 1.4%. Core inflation was projected at 1.2% for 2026 and 1.3% for 2027.

Exports grew 24% year on year in September. Private consumption remained weak and SME credit was contracting. The next MPC meeting is scheduled for 23 December 2026.

Analysis · Desk assessment

The combination of a unanimous hold at 1%, upgraded GDP forecasts and downgraded inflation forecasts suggests the MPC sees the current rate as appropriate for an economy growing modestly with contained price pressures. The 24% export growth in September is striking but has not translated into broad domestic momentum — private consumption remains weak and SME credit is contracting, which points to an uneven recovery where export-oriented sectors outperform domestic demand. The inflation downgrade from 2.8% to 2.1% for 2026 may reduce political pressure for rate cuts, but the weak SME credit picture could renew calls for easing if consumption does not improve. The December meeting will have an additional quarter of data to assess whether the export strength is filtering through to the broader economy.

What we do not know

The specific factors behind the absent MPC member. Whether the export surge is sustainable or reflects front-loading ahead of tariff changes. The extent to which SME credit contraction reflects supply-side tightening versus demand weakness. Whether the government will increase fiscal stimulus to compensate for weak private consumption.

VIETNAM-MALAYSIA · TRADE / 越南-马来西亚 · 贸易

Vietnam-Malaysia bilateral trade on track to surpass $18 billion

12 November 2026

Verified reporting

Bilateral trade between Vietnam and Malaysia reached US$16.33 billion in 2025, having doubled from US$7.4 billion in 2015. In the first half of 2026, trade totalled US$10.8 billion, comprising US$3.4 billion in Vietnamese exports and US$7.4 billion in imports from Malaysia. At this pace, full-year 2026 trade is on track to exceed US$18 billion, against a stated target of US$20 billion and a longer-term goal of US$25 billion.

Vietnam ran a trade deficit of US$5.67 billion with Malaysia in 2025. Malaysia's domestic halal market was valued at approximately US$50 billion. Over 80% of Vietnam's agricultural products were described as inherently halal-compatible, though JAKIM certification requirements remained a noted challenge for Vietnamese exporters.

Analysis · Desk assessment

The doubling of bilateral trade from $7.4 billion to $16.33 billion over a decade, and the H1 2026 run rate pointing above $18 billion, suggests structural rather than cyclical growth in the relationship. However, the trade is heavily imbalanced: Vietnam's $5.67 billion deficit in 2025 and the H1 2026 split ($3.4 billion exports versus $7.4 billion imports) show Vietnam is buying significantly more from Malaysia than it sells. The halal opportunity could help narrow that gap — if 80% of Vietnamese agricultural products are inherently halal-compatible, the binding constraint is certification rather than product suitability. JAKIM certification costs, processing times and compliance requirements are practical barriers that trade agreements alone cannot remove. Whether the $20 billion target is met depends less on headline trade growth and more on whether Vietnam can diversify its export mix beyond the current commodity-heavy composition.

What we do not know

The commodity composition of bilateral trade and which product categories are driving the growth. How many Vietnamese exporters have obtained or are pursuing JAKIM halal certification. The specific timeline and mechanisms for reaching the $25 billion long-term target. Whether supply-chain shifts related to US-China trade tensions are contributing to the bilateral trade increase.

分析 · 编辑部评估

未知事项

Manila prepares to host 49th ASEAN Summit with 17 leaders expected

12 November 2026

Verified reporting

核实报道

菲律宾将于2026年11月15日至17日在马尼拉举办第49届东盟峰会。预计超过17位国家元首和政府首脑出席，包括美国总统特朗普和巴西总统卢拉。

峰会主题为"坚韧与崛起"。预计将签署东盟数字经济框架协议（DEFA），目标是到2030年实现区域数字经济规模达2万亿美元。议程还包括能源安全、气候韧性及南海问题。

分析 · 编辑部评估

DEFA到2030年2万亿美元的目标对于一个数字经济估值目前在3,000亿至6,000亿美元之间（取决于定义）的地区而言颇为雄心勃勃。该框架的价值将取决于它能否在数据流通、数字支付互操作性和监管协调方面产生具有约束力的承诺，还是仅停留在愿景层面。特朗普的出席增加了地缘政治分量——他上次出席东盟活动是2017年第一任期内的马尼拉峰会，其间美中在该地区的竞争已显著加剧。南海议程项目将考验峰会能否产生所有十个成员国都接受的措辞，鉴于声索国与非声索国立场各异。菲律宾的东道主角色赋予其议程设定权，但东盟基于共识的决策机制意味着任何联合声明都将反映最低公约数。

未知事项

DEFA的最终文本和具有约束力的条款。峰会间隙的双边会晤是否会产生具体公告。成员国将就南海问题达成何种措辞。所有宣布的领导人是否会亲自出席还是派代表。马尼拉的安保安排及潜在抗议活动。
