IHSG Surges 2.66% to 5,251 as MSCI Rebalancing Spares Indonesia's Top Four Banks

IHSG飙升2.66%至5,251，MSCI再平衡保留印尼四大银行

English presenter script

Good evening. We open tonight's bulletin with breaking market news from Indonesia. The Jakarta Composite Index surged 2.66 per cent on 12 November, closing at 5,250.89 — up 135.86 points — after MSCI's November rebalancing announcement proved less severe than feared. The rally brought turnover to 5.83 trillion rupiah, with foreign investors recording net purchases of 510.51 billion rupiah on the day. MSCI removed Barito Pacific from its Global Standard Indexes and downgraded Bumi Resources Minerals to the Small Cap Indexes. Seven further stocks — AVIA, CTRA, MTEL, EMTK, JSMR, RAJA and WIFI — were dropped from the Small Cap tier. No Indonesian stocks were added. Critically, however, MSCI spared Indonesia's four major banks: Bank Central Asia, Bank Rakyat Indonesia, Bank Mandiri and Bank Negara Indonesia. These four now constitute 71.81 per cent of MSCI Indonesia's weighting. Indonesia retains 35 stocks across both tiers — 7 in the Global Standard and 28 in the Small Cap indexes. The changes take effect on 1 December 2026, with the next review scheduled for 9 February 2027. Mirae Asset estimated total passive outflows at approximately 980 billion rupiah, including roughly 700 billion from Barito Pacific's exit and 280 billion from the Bumi Resources Minerals downgrade.

Our analysis: The market's relief rally — the strongest single-day gain in weeks — underscores how deeply investors had feared a harsher outcome. The survival of the four major banks was the key variable: had they been removed, Indonesia's emerging market weighting would have collapsed from 0.41 per cent to 0.12 per cent, triggering an estimated 290 million US dollars in passive outflows. Instead, the actual impact is an order of magnitude smaller at roughly 980 billion rupiah. However, the celebration should be tempered by three realities. First, the IHSG remains down approximately 39 per cent year-to-date and cumulative foreign net selling stands at nearly 127 trillion rupiah. Second, the broader reclassification consultation — which could see Indonesia downgraded from Emerging Market to Standalone Market status — runs into 2027 and remains the far larger threat. Third, the pattern of steady constituent attrition means MSCI Indonesia is shrinking with each review cycle. Analysts at Panin Sekuritas see the IHSG trading in a 4,700 to 5,300 range through year-end, with an optimistic scenario of 5,400. The rally is relief, not reversal.

Good evening. We continue in Indonesia with a developing political story that has market implications. Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka has submitted his resignation to President Prabowo Subianto amid an escalating dispute over his educational credentials. The State Secretary confirmed on 10 November that Gibran had expressed his intent to step down, and by 11 November he had formally offered his resignation. The move came as approximately 50 lawmakers pushed a parliamentary motion questioning the Vice President's eligibility on the basis of his diploma qualifications. Gibran, the eldest son of former President Joko Widodo, had been a controversial figure since his rapid political ascent from mayor of Surakarta to the vice presidency. The resignation, if accepted by President Prabowo, would trigger a constitutional process for selecting a replacement — a procedure that has never been tested in Indonesia's post-reform democratic era.

Our analysis: The timing of Gibran's resignation offer — coinciding with the MSCI rebalancing announcement — created an unusual test of market sentiment. The fact that the IHSG rallied 2.66 per cent on 12 November despite the political uncertainty suggests investors view the resignation as potentially resolving an overhang rather than creating a new crisis. The diploma dispute had been a persistent source of governance concern, and its resolution — even through resignation — removes one element of unpredictability from the Prabowo administration. For business, the key question is whether the political transition disrupts the economic policy continuity that Indonesia's market badly needs during the MSCI reclassification consultation period. The constitutional replacement process could take weeks to months, during which the vice presidential office's role in economic coordination would be diminished. Foreign investors, already nervous about Indonesia's institutional credibility, will watch whether the transition is handled through proper constitutional channels or becomes a vehicle for further political manoeuvring. The market's initial verdict is cautiously positive, but sustained confidence will depend on execution.

English presenter script

Good evening. We turn now to Singapore with this report. Singapore's economy expanded 5.4 per cent year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026, according to the Ministry of Trade and Industry's advance estimate — a slight moderation from the 6.2 per cent growth recorded in Q2 but comfortably above market expectations. On a quarter-on-quarter seasonally adjusted basis, growth came in at 1.1 per cent, compared with 1.5 per cent in the preceding quarter. Construction led sectoral growth at 8.8 per cent, accelerating from 6.1 per cent in Q2. Wholesale and retail trade, transportation and storage grew 8.0 per cent, up from 6.9 per cent. Manufacturing expanded 7.5 per cent, moderating from 12.9 per cent in Q2 as base effects normalised, though growth remained broad-based across electronics, precision engineering and transport engineering. The information and communications, finance and insurance, and professional services cluster grew 4.3 per cent, while accommodation, food services, real estate and administrative support were the weakest at 2.1 per cent. On the strength of these numbers, MTI upgraded its full-year 2026 GDP growth forecast to 4.5 to 5.5 per cent, up from the previous range of 2.0 to 4.0 per cent. The Monetary Authority of Singapore held its S-dollar nominal effective exchange rate policy band unchanged in October, citing oil prices near 106 US dollars per barrel and geopolitical uncertainty.

Our analysis: Singapore's Q3 moderation from 6.2 to 5.4 per cent represents a deceleration from exceptional strength rather than any sign of weakness. The upgraded full-year forecast of 4.5 to 5.5 per cent signals that the government now expects growth roughly double the rate initially anticipated — a remarkable outcome driven by the global AI and electronics cycle that has supercharged Singapore's precision manufacturing and trade intermediation sectors. The sectoral composition tells an important story: construction's acceleration to 8.8 per cent reflects both public infrastructure spending and the private building pipeline, while the wholesale-trade surge captures Singapore's role as ASEAN's logistics and trading hub during a period of elevated global goods flows. The weakness in accommodation and food services — at just 2.1 per cent — is a reminder that Singapore's domestic consumption economy has not kept pace with its export-oriented sectors. The MAS decision to hold its exchange rate band steady, despite growth well above trend, reflects the external risks that could reverse momentum quickly: Middle East instability, elevated oil prices and the possibility of renewed US trade measures. Singapore is performing as the ASEAN economy best positioned to capture the AI hardware cycle, but policymakers are wisely avoiding premature declarations of victory.

English presenter script

Good evening. We turn now to Thailand with this report. The Bank of Thailand's Monetary Policy Committee held its benchmark interest rate at 1.00 per cent per annum on 28 October, the fourth consecutive meeting without a change and the lowest rate since September 2022. The vote was unanimous among the six members attending, with one member absent. All 26 economists surveyed by Reuters had expected the hold. The committee raised its 2026 GDP growth forecast to 2.4 per cent from 2.3 per cent, and upgraded 2027 growth to 2.1 per cent from 1.8 per cent. Headline inflation forecasts were revised down sharply to 2.1 per cent for 2026 from 2.8 per cent, and to 1.3 per cent for 2027 from 1.4 per cent. Core inflation is projected at 1.2 per cent in 2026 and 1.3 per cent in 2027. The committee acknowledged that Thailand's economy presents a tale of two realities: merchandise exports surged 24 per cent year-on-year in September, powered by electronics and AI-related equipment, while private consumption remains weak due to high household debt, rising unemployment and eroding purchasing power. SME credit continues to contract across almost every sector. The next MPC meeting is scheduled for 23 December.

Our analysis: Thailand's 1 per cent policy rate — having been cut 1.5 percentage points since October 2024 — is already at levels that would normally signal deep economic distress, yet the economy continues to underperform. The upgraded GDP forecast of 2.4 per cent for 2026 matches the 2025 outcome, meaning Thailand is essentially treading water while neighbours accelerate: Vietnam is growing at nearly 10 per cent, Singapore at 5.4 per cent, and Malaysia at 5.3 per cent. The tale of two economies is becoming Thailand's defining economic narrative. The export sector — particularly electronics and AI hardware — is booming because it plugs into global supply chains that are independent of domestic demand. But the household sector, weighed down by debt that the central bank describes as high, is unable to translate low interest rates into spending. SME credit contraction across nearly every sector is an alarm signal: it means the monetary transmission mechanism is broken at precisely the level where most Thai employment resides. The MPC's decision to hold rather than cut further suggests it recognises that more rate reductions would not fix structural problems — and could worsen financial stability risks that the committee explicitly flagged. The uncomfortable truth is that Thailand's economy needs fiscal and structural reform more than it needs cheaper money, but the political will for such reform remains unclear.

English presenter script

Good evening. We turn now to a cross-border trade story connecting Vietnam and Malaysia. Two-way trade between Vietnam and Malaysia reached 16.33 billion US dollars in 2025, more than double the 7.4 billion recorded in 2015, and is on track to exceed 18 billion US dollars in 2026 — putting the 20 billion dollar milestone within reach and the longer-term target of 25 billion dollars in sight. In the first half of 2026, bilateral trade totalled 10.8 billion US dollars, comprising 3.4 billion in Vietnamese exports and 7.4 billion in imports from Malaysia. Vietnam's main exports to Malaysia are machinery, electrical equipment, mechanical products and production materials, while Malaysia ships machinery, equipment, petroleum products, computers, electronics and components in return. Vietnam runs a persistent trade deficit with Malaysia — 5.67 billion US dollars in 2025 and nearly 4 billion in the first half of 2026 alone. A key growth opportunity lies in Malaysia's halal market. Malaysia's domestic halal food market is estimated at 50 billion US dollars, and its Halal Industry Master Plan targets 113 billion in domestic halal consumption by 2030. Vietnam, as one of the world's top ten exporters of rice, coffee, cashew nuts, pepper and medicinal herbs, has natural advantages: more than 80 per cent of its plant-based agricultural products inherently meet basic halal requirements. However, businesses face significant certification challenges, including limited experience with JAKIM's strict procedures and the cost of mandatory on-site inspections.

Our analysis: The Vietnam-Malaysia trade corridor illustrates an underappreciated dynamic within ASEAN: intra-regional trade growing faster than either country's trade with China or the United States. The doubling of bilateral trade from 7.4 billion to 16.33 billion dollars in a decade reflects the deepening integration of supply chains within Southeast Asia itself, not merely the China-plus-one story that dominates headlines. Vietnam's persistent deficit with Malaysia is driven by the structure of their respective manufacturing sectors — Malaysia exports higher-value components and petroleum products that Vietnamese factories consume as inputs, while Vietnam ships back semi-finished goods. The halal market opportunity is the most strategically interesting angle: Vietnam's agricultural export base is inherently halal-compatible, but converting that natural advantage into certified market access requires institutional infrastructure that does not yet exist at scale. JAKIM certification is rigorous and expensive, and Vietnam's halal certification authority is still in the early stages of direct cooperation with its Malaysian counterpart. If Vietnam can crack the halal certification barrier, it opens not just Malaysia's 50 billion dollar market but the broader 2.3 trillion dollar global halal economy that Malaysia serves as a gateway to. The 20 billion dollar bilateral target is achievable on current trajectory; whether the relationship deepens beyond commodity trade into higher-value collaboration depends on whether the certification and standards gap can be closed.

各位晚上好。接下来请看一则连接越南和马来西亚的跨境贸易报道。越南与马来西亚双边贸易2025年达163.3亿美元，较2015年的74亿美元翻了一番以上，2026年有望突破180亿美元——200亿美元的里程碑触手可及，250亿美元的长期目标也进入视野。2026年上半年，双边贸易总额108亿美元，其中越南出口34亿美元，从马来西亚进口74亿美元。越南对马主要出口机械、电气设备、机械产品和生产材料，马来西亚则向越南出口机械设备、石油产品、电脑、电子产品和零部件。越南对马来西亚持续存在贸易逆差——2025年为56.7亿美元，仅2026年上半年就接近40亿美元。一个关键增长机遇在于马来西亚的清真市场。马来西亚国内清真食品市场估计达500亿美元，其《清真产业总体规划》目标是到2030年国内清真消费达1,130亿美元。越南作为全球十大大米、咖啡、腰果、胡椒和药用植物出口国之一，拥有天然优势：超过80%的植物性农产品本身就符合基本清真要求。然而，企业面临重大认证挑战，包括对马来西亚伊斯兰发展局严格程序经验不足以及强制性现场检查的费用。

原创分析：越马贸易走廊揭示了东盟内部一个被低估的动态：区域内贸易增速快于两国与中国或美国的贸易增速。双边贸易十年内从74亿翻倍至163.3亿美元，反映了东南亚内部供应链的深化整合，而不仅仅是主导新闻标题的"中国加一"故事。越南对马来西亚的持续逆差源于两国制造业结构差异——马来西亚出口更高价值的零部件和石油产品作为越南工厂的投入品，而越南回运半成品。清真市场机遇是战略上最有趣的角度：越南的农产品出口基础天然具有清真兼容性，但将这一天然优势转化为经认证的市场准入，需要目前尚不具规模的制度基础设施。马来西亚伊斯兰发展局认证严格且昂贵，越南的清真认证机构与马方的直接合作仍处于早期阶段。如果越南能突破清真认证壁垒，打开的不仅是马来西亚500亿美元的市场，还有马来西亚作为门户所通向的2.3万亿美元全球清真经济。按当前轨迹，200亿美元的双边目标可以实现；双边关系能否从大宗商品贸易深化为更高价值的合作，取决于认证和标准差距能否弥合。

English presenter script

Good evening. We close tonight's bulletin with a look ahead to next week's ASEAN Summit. Manila is in final preparations to host the 49th ASEAN Summit from 15 to 17 November, with more than 17 world leaders confirming their attendance, including US President Donald Trump and Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. The summit, held under the Philippines' 2026 ASEAN chairship theme of "Resilient and Rising," is expected to deliver several landmark outcomes. Chief among them is the signing of the Digital Economy Framework Agreement — DEFA — described as the world's first binding regional digital economy pact. ASEAN's Secretary-General has projected that the bloc's digital economy could reach 2 trillion US dollars by 2030, and DEFA is the regulatory architecture intended to support that ambition by harmonising rules on cross-border data flows, digital payments, e-commerce, cybersecurity and AI governance across the ten member states. Beyond DEFA, the summit agenda is expected to cover energy security, food security, climate action and the South China Sea. President Marcos has positioned the Philippines' chairship around practical, inclusive and measurable initiatives, with the Department of Finance pushing an ASEAN climate finance agenda ahead of COP31. The Philippine government has described the summit as showcasing the country's leadership and readiness for new investments.

Our analysis: The confirmed attendance of 17-plus world leaders — including both the US President and the Brazilian President, representing the world's largest and fifth-largest economies — elevates this Manila summit above a routine ASEAN gathering. Trump's presence is particularly significant: his administration's tariff policies have reshaped trade flows across Southeast Asia, and the summit provides a bilateral meeting opportunity with ASEAN leaders who are navigating the US-China trade realignment in real time. The DEFA signing would be the summit's most consequential economic deliverable, but its impact depends entirely on implementation. The 2-trillion-dollar digital economy target by 2030 is aspirational — ASEAN's current digital economy is estimated at roughly 300 billion dollars — and closing that gap requires not just a signed agreement but harmonised regulations, cross-border payment infrastructure, and digital identity systems that do not yet exist at scale. The energy security agenda has taken on new urgency as oil prices remain near 106 US dollars per barrel, squeezing ASEAN's net energy importers — particularly the Philippines and Thailand. President Marcos's framing of the summit around "practical" and "measurable" outcomes is an implicit acknowledgment that ASEAN's track record of translating summit declarations into binding action has been mixed at best. Whether Manila delivers substance alongside spectacle will define the Philippines' legacy as ASEAN chair.

各位晚上好。今晚最后一篇报道展望下周的东盟峰会。马尼拉正在为11月15日至17日举行的第49届东盟峰会做最后准备，超过17位世界领导人确认出席，包括美国总统特朗普和巴西总统卢拉。本次峰会以菲律宾2026年东盟轮值主席国主题"韧性崛起"举行，预计将产生几项里程碑式成果。首要成果是签署《数字经济框架协议》（DEFA）——被描述为全球首个具约束力的区域数字经济协定。东盟秘书长预计该集团的数字经济到2030年可达2万亿美元，DEFA正是旨在支撑这一雄心的监管架构，通过协调十个成员国在跨境数据流通、数字支付、电子商务、网络安全和AI治理方面的规则。除DEFA外，峰会议程预计涵盖能源安全、粮食安全、气候行动和南海问题。马科斯总统将菲律宾的主席任期定位于务实、包容和可衡量的倡议，财政部在COP31之前推动东盟气候融资议程。菲律宾政府表示，峰会展示了该国的领导力和吸引新投资的准备就绪状态。

原创分析：17位以上世界领导人确认出席——包括分别代表全球第一和第五大经济体的美国总统和巴西总统——使马尼拉峰会超越了例行性东盟会议的层级。特朗普的出席尤为重要：其政府的关税政策已重塑东南亚贸易格局，峰会为正在实时应对美中贸易重组的东盟领导人提供了双边会晤机会。DEFA签署将是峰会最具实质意义的经济成果，但其影响完全取决于实施。2030年数字经济2万亿美元的目标是愿景性的——东盟当前数字经济估计约为3,000亿美元——弥合这一差距不仅需要签署协议，还需要尚不具规模的统一监管、跨境支付基础设施和数字身份系统。随着油价维持在每桶约106美元附近，挤压东盟净能源进口国——特别是菲律宾和泰国——能源安全议程获得了新的紧迫性。马科斯总统将峰会定位于"务实"和"可衡量"的成果，隐含承认东盟将峰会宣言转化为具约束力行动的记录充其量是参差不齐的。马尼拉能否在盛况之外交出实质成果，将定义菲律宾作为东盟轮值主席国的遗产。
