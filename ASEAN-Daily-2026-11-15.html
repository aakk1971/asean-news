49th ASEAN Summit Opens in Manila with Global Leaders as Marcos Declares Bloc Can 'Weather Global Headwinds'

15 November 2026. Rappler / Manila Times / Al Jazeera / Inquirer.

Verified reporting

The 49th ASEAN Summit formally opened in Manila on 15 November 2026 with all ten ASEAN heads of state present alongside dialogue partners including US President Donald Trump, Chinese Premier Li Qiang, Japanese Prime Minister Shigeru Ishiba, and South Korean President. Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., serving as ASEAN chair, declared that ASEAN is now "a strong regional bloc that can weather global headwinds." Day 1 addressed three major issues: a conditional pathway for Myanmar's engagement — with ASEAN setting conditions for the military junta to meet before full re-engagement — an acknowledgment that the self-imposed 2026 deadline for concluding the South China Sea Code of Conduct with China had been missed, and celebration of the Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA) signed the previous day. The Philippines had previously pitched US$1.5 billion in investment-ready projects at the ASEAN Investment Forum held on 10 November, positioning the summit as a platform for economic commitments.

Our analysis

The convergence of global powers in Manila underscores ASEAN's growing geopolitical centrality. The bloc's combined GDP of approximately US$4 trillion and population of 670 million make it impossible for any major power to ignore. Marcos's "weather global headwinds" framing is deliberate — it positions ASEAN not as a passive recipient of great-power competition but as an active economic force capable of shaping regional outcomes. The Myanmar conditional pathway represents ASEAN's strongest stance yet, moving beyond the failed Five-Point Consensus toward measurable conditions that the junta must meet. The missed South China Sea Code of Conduct deadline is diplomatically significant: ASEAN and China have been negotiating for over two decades, and the failure to meet even a self-imposed timeline suggests fundamental disagreements remain on binding enforcement mechanisms. The summit's economic deliverables — DEFA, multiple free trade agreement conclusions, and investment pledges — are designed to demonstrate that ASEAN's relevance extends far beyond security discussions. For Marcos, the successful convening of this many world leaders in Manila validates the Philippines' ASEAN chairship and strengthens the narrative that the country is a serious player in regional economic integration.

What we don't know yet

The specific conditions set for Myanmar's re-engagement have not been fully disclosed. Whether a new timeline for the South China Sea Code of Conduct will be agreed upon at this summit remains unclear. The total value of new investment commitments from dialogue partners has not been tallied.

Malaysia's Economy Expected to Grow Above 5% in 2026, Making It Three Consecutive Years of Strong Expansion

15 November 2026. Business Today Malaysia / The Star / Malay Mail.

Verified reporting

Malaysia's economy is expected to grow above 5 per cent in 2026 for the third consecutive year, according to government projections reported on 15 November. Finance Minister II Amir Hamzah said the government is confident of exceeding the 5 per cent threshold, citing strong domestic demand, rising investment flows, and robust export performance. Third-quarter 2026 GDP came in at 5.3 per cent, beating market expectations, following first-quarter growth of 5.4 per cent. Malaysia posted 5.2 per cent growth in 2025 and 5.1 per cent in 2024. The ringgit has strengthened over the course of 2026, headline inflation remains the lowest in ASEAN at 1.8 per cent, and the Madani Economy framework has attracted significant foreign direct investment in semiconductors, data centres, and green energy — with major commitments from global technology companies including Microsoft, Google, and AWS.

Our analysis

Three consecutive years of 5 per cent or higher GDP growth would represent Malaysia's most sustained strong performance since the period before the 2008 global financial crisis. The achievement is particularly notable given the challenging global backdrop — US-China trade tensions, higher global interest rates, and supply chain disruptions have weighed on many peer economies. The composition of growth matters: Malaysia has benefited enormously from the semiconductor and data centre investment wave sweeping Southeast Asia, positioning itself alongside Singapore as the region's digital infrastructure hub. The risk is that growth becomes over-reliant on a single technology cycle — if global AI investment moderates, the pipeline of new data centre projects could slow. The ringgit's stabilisation and low inflation environment give Bank Negara Malaysia policy flexibility, but the World Bank's recent call for "decisive reforms" — particularly on fuel subsidies — is a reminder that strong headline growth is masking structural issues that could erode competitiveness over time. The 5 per cent figure also has political significance: Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's government has staked its economic credibility on delivering growth that translates into improved living standards, and three years of strong GDP numbers strengthen that narrative.

What we don't know yet

Full fourth-quarter 2026 data is not yet available and will determine whether the annual figure crosses the 5 per cent threshold. The extent to which data centre and semiconductor investment will sustain into 2027 is uncertain amid signs of cooling in global technology capital expenditure. Whether the government will use this strong growth period to accelerate fuel subsidy reform remains to be seen.

Singapore's Economy Grows 5.4% in Q3 2026 Amid AI-Driven Boom, Slowing from 6.2% in Q2

October 2026 (advance estimates). Ministry of Trade and Industry (Singapore) / SingStat / Al Jazeera / The Star.

Verified reporting

Singapore's economy grew 5.4 per cent year on year in the third quarter of 2026, down from 6.2 per cent in Q2, according to advance estimates released by the Ministry of Trade and Industry. On a quarter-on-quarter seasonally adjusted basis, GDP rose 1.1 per cent. The AI boom has been a key growth driver, with Singapore positioning itself as Southeast Asia's AI infrastructure hub — second only to Taiwan in Asia for AI-related economic benefits. Manufacturing, information and communications, and financial services were the main sectoral contributors. DBS and UOB described growth as "running above potential." The Monetary Authority of Singapore noted that 2026 overall growth may moderate from 2025's robust pace but remain above the economy's long-run trend. Data centre construction and semiconductor-related services are expanding rapidly across the island, with hyperscale cloud providers investing billions of dollars in Singapore-based capacity.

Our analysis

Singapore's 5.4 per cent growth rate, while a deceleration from Q2's 6.2 per cent, remains well above the economy's long-run potential of 2 to 3 per cent. The AI infrastructure boom is the dominant narrative — Singapore has become the Southeast Asian epicentre for data centre construction, AI research laboratories, and cloud computing infrastructure, attracting billions from global hyperscalers. This has created a two-speed economy: technology-adjacent sectors including semiconductor services, cloud infrastructure, and fintech are growing rapidly, while more traditional sectors such as retail, food services, and tourism-dependent hospitality face more modest conditions. The MAS's signal that 2026 overall growth may moderate reflects concerns that the base effects from the 2025 rebound are fading and that external demand may soften as global trade conditions evolve. However, the structural positioning of Singapore as the region's AI and digital economy hub suggests that the city-state's growth premium over ASEAN peers will persist into the medium term. The risk is that Singapore's economy becomes increasingly correlated with the global AI investment cycle, making it vulnerable to any correction in technology capital expenditure.

What we don't know yet

Final Q3 GDP figures may be revised when the Ministry of Trade and Industry releases confirmed data. The breakdown between manufacturing sub-sectors — especially AI-related versus traditional electronics — has not been fully detailed. The impact of potential US technology export controls on Singapore's semiconductor services sector remains uncertain.

Indonesia Records US$13.23 Billion in Foreign Portfolio Inflows Through October 2026 as High-Yield Instruments Attract Global Capital

November 2026. Bank Indonesia / Indonesia Business Post / Antara News.

Verified reporting

Indonesia recorded US$13.23 billion in foreign portfolio inflows through October 2026, according to Bank Indonesia data, driven primarily by demand for the central bank's high-yield Rupiah Securities (SRBI) and sovereign bonds (SBN). Bank Indonesia has deployed rate hikes and the creation of SRBI instruments as a dual strategy to attract foreign capital and defend the rupiah. The trajectory of inflows has accelerated throughout the year: BI reported US$3.3 billion through May, US$9 billion through June, and the figure has now risen to US$13.23 billion through October. Foreign investors poured US$1.8 billion into Indonesian instruments in Q3 alone. Indonesian government bonds attracted foreign inflows for the first time in over seven years in 2026, marking a significant reversal of the persistent outflows experienced during the global rate-hiking cycle of 2023–2024. Indonesia's official reserve assets remain solid, and the country's GDP growth picked up pace in the third quarter, providing a supportive macroeconomic backdrop.

Our analysis

The US$13.23 billion in portfolio inflows through October represents a remarkable turnaround for Indonesia's capital markets, which had experienced persistent foreign outflows in 2023–2024 as the US Federal Reserve's rate hikes drew capital toward dollar-denominated assets. Bank Indonesia's strategy of creating high-yield SRBI instruments has proven effective — these securities offer attractive real yields that compensate investors for currency risk, drawing in yield-seeking global capital at a time when many emerging-market central banks are competing for the same pool of funds. The return of foreign investors to Indonesian government bonds after a seven-year absence signals improving confidence in the country's macroeconomic management and fiscal trajectory. However, portfolio inflows are inherently volatile — they can reverse rapidly if global risk sentiment shifts, if BI signals the beginning of a rate-cutting cycle, or if the rupiah comes under renewed pressure from a strengthening US dollar. The composition of inflows matters: if they are concentrated in short-duration central bank bills rather than long-dated sovereign bonds, the capital base is less sticky and more prone to sudden withdrawal. Indonesia's broader challenge is to convert portfolio investor interest into more permanent foreign direct investment, which is less subject to sudden reversal and contributes directly to productive capacity.

What we don't know yet

The precise split between SRBI and SBN inflows has not been broken down in published data. Whether Bank Indonesia will begin cutting rates in 2027, which could reduce the yield attraction, remains uncertain. The impact of potential US Federal Reserve policy shifts on Indonesian capital flows in 2027 has not been formally assessed.

Philippines Awaits US Response on Pax Silica Semiconductor Hub Framework as Trump Arrives for ASEAN Summit

November 2026. Asian News Network / PhilStar / Gulf News.

Verified reporting

The Philippines is awaiting a formal response from the United States on the Pax Silica framework — a proposed bilateral agreement to develop a major semiconductor, artificial intelligence, and advanced manufacturing hub at New Clark City in Tarlac province. The draft framework was finalised between Philippine and US officials earlier in 2026, with Philippine officials saying in July that the agreement could be signed by November. The Pax Silica zone covers approximately 4,000 acres at New Clark City and envisions a "Silicon Valley"-style economic security zone anchored by major technology firms. Our analysis

Pax Silica represents the Philippines' most ambitious technology and industrial policy initiative in decades — an attempt to leverage its geographic position, alliance with the United States, and existing electronics manufacturing base to capture a share of the global semiconductor supply chain restructuring. The strategic logic is compelling: the US seeks to diversify semiconductor supply chains away from concentrated dependence on Taiwan and China, Japan brings advanced manufacturing expertise, and the Philippines offers competitive labour costs, an English-speaking workforce, and existing OSAT (outsourced semiconductor assembly and test) capabilities that already underpin a US$54-billion export industry. New Clark City's 4,000-acre footprint is designed at a scale that signals serious intent — this is not a small special economic zone but a city-sized industrial development that could fundamentally reshape the Philippines' position in the global electronics value chain. Foxconn's anchor commitment provides industrial credibility and ecosystem benefits. However, several challenges remain: the Philippines faces power generation constraints that could limit energy-intensive chip fabrication, the skills pipeline for advanced semiconductor manufacturing needs significant development, and the country has historically struggled to translate ambitious special economic zone concepts into fully operational industrial clusters. The timing of Trump's Manila visit creates a natural diplomatic window, though ASEAN officials have cautioned that the summit itself may not be the venue for signing bilateral agreements.

What we don't know yet

Whether the United States will formally sign or announce the Pax Silica framework during Trump's Manila visit has not been confirmed. The specific investment commitments beyond Foxconn have not been publicly detailed. The timeline for construction and first production at New Clark City has not been set.

Bank of Thailand Raises Growth Forecast to 2.4% but Warns Recovery Remains 'Concentrated' in Exports

November 2026. Bank of Thailand / Thailand Business News / Kaohoon International / Nation Thailand.

Verified reporting

The Bank of Thailand raised its 2026 GDP growth forecast to 2.4 per cent, up from its previous projection, while maintaining the policy rate at 1.00 per cent for the fourth consecutive meeting. The central bank warned that the economic recovery remains "concentrated" in exports and certain manufacturing sectors, while domestic demand, household consumption, and small business activity remain subdued. Separately, the Joint Standing Committee on Commerce, Industry and Banking (JSCCIB) — Thailand's top private-sector business group — raised its own 2026 growth forecast to 2.4–2.6 per cent. At the ASEAN Summit in Manila, Thai Prime Minister Anutin Chanvirakul pushed a three-point strategy for ASEAN to boost regional trade and investment. The ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office (AMRO) released its annual consultation report on Thailand in November, flagging risks to the domestic outlook. Thailand's household debt remains elevated at approximately 90 per cent of GDP, weighing on consumer spending and small business confidence.

Our analysis

Thailand's "tale of two economies" is the defining feature of its current economic cycle. The export sector is booming — driven by AI-related electronics demand, automotive parts, and agricultural commodities — while the domestic economy remains under pressure from high household debt, weak consumer confidence, and a struggling SME sector. The BOT's decision to hold rates at 1 per cent for the fourth consecutive meeting reflects this tension: cutting rates could boost domestic activity but would widen the interest rate differential with the US and put pressure on the baht; holding rates protects currency stability but does little for cash-strapped households and small businesses. The 2.4 per cent growth forecast, while an upgrade, places Thailand significantly below ASEAN peers — Vietnam at 8.2 per cent, Malaysia above 5 per cent, Singapore at 5.4 per cent, and Indonesia at approximately 5 per cent. This underperformance relative to the bloc is politically sensitive for Prime Minister Anutin, whose government has faced criticism for failing to deliver on economic promises. AMRO's risk flagging adds external validation to concerns that Thailand's recovery, while statistically positive, is not broad-based enough to lift living standards for the majority of the population.

What we don't know yet

Whether the BOT will begin cutting rates in early 2027 has not been signalled. The full impact of the government's digital wallet stimulus programme on consumer spending has not been quantified. How Thailand's "concentrated" recovery will interact with potential global trade disruptions in 2027 remains uncertain.
