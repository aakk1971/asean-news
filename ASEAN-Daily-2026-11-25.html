Singapore Raises 2026 GDP Forecast to About 6% After Q3 Growth Hits 6.1% on AI and Export Boom

24–25 November 2026. Ministry of Trade and Industry / Department of Statistics / AsiaOne / Reuters / VnExpress.

Verified reporting

Singapore's Ministry of Trade and Industry (MTI) raised the country's 2026 GDP growth forecast to "around 6 per cent" on 24 November, up from the previous range of 4.5 to 5.5 per cent set in August, following a third-quarter expansion of 6.1 per cent year-on-year. The Q3 figure beat the advance estimate of 5.4 per cent and the median market expectation of 5.6 per cent. On a quarter-on-quarter seasonally adjusted basis, the economy grew 1.5 per cent in Q3, easing from 1.8 per cent in Q2.

This marks the third upward revision to Singapore's growth forecast in 2026 — from 1 to 3 per cent in November 2025, to 2 to 4 per cent in February, to 4.5 to 5.5 per cent in August, and now to around 6 per cent. Growth in the first three quarters of 2026 averaged 6.2 per cent year-on-year, with Q2 registering 6.4 per cent.

Q3 growth was driven primarily by wholesale trade — specifically the machinery, equipment and supplies segment reflecting strong AI-related demand — along with manufacturing, where the electronics and precision engineering clusters expanded on AI demand, and by the finance and insurance sector through credit growth and fee-generating activity. Retail trade and food and beverage services contracted in Q3, which the MTI attributed to a sustained shift in local spending abroad as outbound travel picked up.

For 2027, the MTI forecast growth of 2.5 to 4.5 per cent, citing expectations that manufacturing and wholesale trade will continue to expand but at a slower pace. Downside risks include a correction in the global AI cycle, further Middle East escalation pushing up energy and input prices, and economic and political uncertainty in external economies. Permanent Secretary Dr Beh Swan Gin noted that concerns about the sustainability of the global AI cycle could trigger sharper corrections in global financial markets.

Our analysis

Singapore's 6 per cent full-year growth forecast is extraordinary for a mature, high-income economy — this is the kind of growth rate that developing economies aspire to, and it confirms Singapore's position as perhaps the single largest beneficiary of the global AI infrastructure buildout among developed economies. The composition of growth is revealing: the dominant drivers are wholesale trade in AI-related machinery, electronics manufacturing, and precision engineering — all directly connected to the global data centre and AI chip supply chain. Singapore produces approximately 10 per cent of the world's chips and about 20 per cent of global semiconductor manufacturing equipment, according to industry estimates, which explains why the AI boom translates so directly into GDP growth. The serial forecast upgrades — from 1–3 per cent to 6 per cent in a single year — illustrate how dramatically the AI demand surge exceeded initial expectations. As recently as November 2025, the MTI was projecting growth that would have been considered normal for Singapore; the AI boom has added approximately 3 to 4 percentage points above what would otherwise have been expected. The 2027 forecast of 2.5 to 4.5 per cent represents a significant moderation, and the MTI's explicit flagging of AI cycle correction risk as a downside scenario suggests the ministry is aware that the current pace is unsustainable. If AI-related capital expenditure by hyperscale operators slows — whether due to overbuilding, shifting to inference from training workloads, or a reassessment of AI returns — Singapore's growth could decelerate sharply. The contraction in retail and F&B from outbound travel is an interesting structural shift: Singaporeans are spending money abroad rather than domestically, which is a form of leakage that offsets some of the GDP gains from exports. The comparison with ASEAN peers is instructive: at 6 per cent, Singapore is growing faster than Malaysia (5.5 per cent), Indonesia (approximately 5 per cent), and Vietnam (approximately 7 per cent in recent quarters), and dramatically faster than Thailand (2.5 per cent) and the Philippines (2.1 per cent). For a US$500 billion economy, this is a remarkable performance.

What we don't know yet

Whether the AI-driven demand that powered three consecutive quarters of above-6 per cent growth will sustain into 2027 or whether the current pace reflects front-loading of data centre equipment orders. How much of the Q3 growth in wholesale trade and manufacturing represents genuine end-demand versus inventory building in the semiconductor supply chain. The composition of Q4 growth — whether the retail and F&B contraction will deepen as outbound travel continues to accelerate. Whether the 2027 forecast of 2.5 to 4.5 per cent adequately captures the downside risk from an AI cycle correction, given the economy's heavy dependence on this single demand driver. The fiscal implications of rapid growth — whether the government will deploy additional fiscal measures or allow the stronger revenue position to build reserves.

新加坡在第三季度GDP增长6.1%后将2026年全年预测上调至约6%，AI和出口热潮推动增长

2026年11月24–25日。贸工部 / 统计局 / AsiaOne / 路透社 / VnExpress。

经核实的报道

新加坡贸工部（MTI）于11月24日将2026年GDP增长预测上调至"约6%"，高于8月设定的4.5%至5.5%区间，此前第三季度同比增长6.1%。第三季度数据超过了5.4%的预估值和5.6%的市场预期中位数。经季节性调整的环比基础上，第三季度增长1.5%，低于第二季度的1.8%。

这是2026年新加坡增长预测的第三次上调——从2025年11月的1%至3%，到2月的2%至4%，到8月的4.5%至5.5%，现在上调至约6%。2026年前三个季度增长平均为6.2%，其中第二季度为6.4%。

第三季度增长主要由批发贸易驱动——特别是反映强劲AI相关需求的机械、设备和供应品部分——以及制造业（电子和精密工程集群在AI需求推动下扩张）和金融保险业（信贷增长和收费活动）。零售贸易和餐饮服务在第三季度收缩，贸工部将其归因于出境旅游增加导致本地消费持续向海外转移。

对于2027年，贸工部预测增长2.5%至4.5%，预期制造业和批发贸易将继续扩张但速度放缓。下行风险包括全球AI周期的修正、中东局势进一步升级推高能源和投入品价格，以及外部经济体的经济和政治不确定性。常任秘书贝宣勤博士指出，对全球AI周期可持续性的担忧可能触发全球金融市场更大幅度的修正。

原创分析

新加坡6%的全年增长预测对于一个成熟的高收入经济体而言非同寻常——这是发展中经济体渴望的增长率，确认了新加坡作为发达经济体中全球AI基础设施建设最大受益者的地位。增长构成揭示性强：主要驱动力是AI相关机械的批发贸易、电子制造业和精密工程——全部直接连接全球数据中心和AI芯片供应链。据行业估计，新加坡生产全球约10%的芯片和约20%的半导体制造设备，这解释了为何AI热潮如此直接地转化为GDP增长。连续的预测上调——从1-3%到6%在一年之内——说明AI需求激增超出初始预期的程度之大。就在2025年11月，贸工部的增长预测对新加坡来说还属正常水平；AI热潮在原本预期之上增加了约3至4个百分点。2027年2.5%至4.5%的预测代表显著放缓，贸工部明确将AI周期修正风险列为下行情景，表明该部门意识到当前速度不可持续。如果超大规模运营商的AI相关资本支出放缓——无论是由于过度建设、从训练向推理工作负载转变还是对AI回报的重新评估——新加坡的增长可能急剧减速。零售和餐饮因出境旅游而收缩是一个有趣的结构性转变：新加坡人在海外而非国内消费，这是一种抵消部分出口GDP收益的泄漏形式。与东盟同行的比较具有启示性：以6%的增速，新加坡增长快于马来西亚（5.5%）、印尼（约5%）和越南（近几个季度约7%），且远快于泰国（2.5%）和菲律宾（2.1%）。对于一个5,000亿美元的经济体，这是卓越的表现。

未知事项

驱动连续三个季度超6%增长的AI需求能否延续至2027年，还是当前速度反映了数据中心设备订单的前置。第三季度批发贸易和制造业增长中有多少代表真正的终端需求而非半导体供应链的库存积累。第四季度增长的构成——零售和餐饮收缩是否会随出境旅游继续加速而加深。2.5%至4.5%的2027年预测是否充分反映了AI周期修正的下行风险，考虑到经济对这一单一需求驱动力的高度依赖。快速增长的财政影响——政府是否会部署额外的财政措施还是让更强的收入状况积累储备。

Source: MTI / Department of Statistics / AsiaOne / Reuters

Vietnam's National Assembly Sets Unprecedented 10% GDP Growth Target for 2027 with US$228 Billion Investment Plan

7–25 November 2026. National Assembly / VnExpress / VietNamNet / Vietnam+ / VNA.

Verified reporting

Vietnam's 16th National Assembly adopted a resolution on 7 November 2026 setting a GDP growth target of "at least 10 per cent" for 2027, with 467 of 469 deputies present voting in favour. The resolution also sets a GDP per capita target of approximately US$6,100, a CPI inflation target of about 4.5 per cent, and describes 2027 as "a year of acceleration and breakthroughs" linked to the 14th Party Congress resolution.

The sector targets are ambitious across the board: industry and construction at 12.2 per cent, manufacturing and processing at 12.3 per cent (reaching about 25.4 per cent of GDP), services at approximately 9.8 per cent, and agriculture, forestry and fisheries at 3.8 per cent. The industrial production index is targeted at 11.5 to 12.5 per cent, retail sales growth at 14 per cent, merchandise export growth at 15 per cent, and import growth at 12 per cent. Labour productivity is targeted to rise 8.6 per cent, and the digital economy is projected to reach approximately 23.5 per cent of GDP.

The investment plan is massive. Total social investment is projected at 36 to 38 per cent of GDP — approximately VND6 to 6.4 quadrillion (US$228 to 243 billion). State-budget development investment accounts for about VND1.76 quadrillion (US$67 billion), or 27 to 29 per cent of the total, leaving a non-budget gap of approximately VND4.2 to 4.6 quadrillion (US$160 to 175 billion) that must come from private domestic and foreign sources. FDI disbursement is targeted at US$30 to 32 billion. State-owned enterprise group disbursement is set above VND416 trillion (US$15.8 billion), up 40 per cent. Stock market mobilisation is targeted at approximately VND950 trillion (US$36.1 billion).

Thirty of Vietnam's 34 provinces and cities are targeting GRDP growth of at least 10 per cent, with Hai Phong at 13 per cent, Quang Ninh at 12 per cent, Hanoi at 11.1 per cent, and Ho Chi Minh City at 10.4 per cent. Major infrastructure priorities include completing the eastern and western North-South Expressway sections, accelerating the Lao Cai-Hanoi-Hai Phong railway and the North-South high-speed railway, and progressing Long Thanh International Airport in Dong Nai and Gia Binh International Airport in Bac Ninh.

Our analysis

Vietnam's 10 per cent GDP growth target for 2027 is the most ambitious macroeconomic objective set by any ASEAN economy in recent memory — and potentially in the country's modern economic history. For context, the IMF's October 2026 forecast projects Vietnam growing at 7.5 per cent in 2027, which means the National Assembly is targeting growth approximately 2.5 percentage points above what the international community considers achievable. Vietnam has not achieved 10 per cent growth since the early 1990s, during the doi moi reform boom, when the economy was a fraction of its current size. The investment plan reveals how Hanoi intends to reach the target: by mobilising US$228 to 243 billion in total social investment, representing 36 to 38 per cent of GDP. This is an enormous capital commitment. The state budget covers only US$67 billion — the remaining US$160 to 175 billion must come from private domestic enterprises, foreign direct investment, and the stock market. The FDI disbursement target of US$30 to 32 billion represents a significant acceleration from the US$24.08 billion disbursed in the first nine months of 2026 (up 13.1 per cent). The question is whether the global investment environment and Vietnam's absorptive capacity can sustain this pace. The savings-investment dynamics deserve scrutiny. National savings would need to jump from 34.9 per cent of GDP to 39.4 per cent in a single year to finance the investment without inflationary pressure, according to IMF figures. If savings do not keep pace, the gap will need to be filled by foreign capital inflows or domestic credit expansion, either of which carries risks — the former to exchange rate stability, the latter to inflation and financial system health. The 4.5 per cent CPI target alongside 10 per cent growth is also ambitious, given that rapid investment-driven expansion typically generates inflationary pressure through demand for labour, materials, and energy. The infrastructure pipeline — high-speed rail, two new international airports, expressway completion — is transformative but will require years of execution. The political significance should not be underestimated: the near-unanimous 467-to-469 vote reflects the Party's determination to demonstrate that Vietnam's development model can deliver growth rates comparable to China's peak years.

What we don't know yet

Whether the 10 per cent target is achievable or aspirational — and whether the government will adjust policy if growth falls short, or pursue stimulus measures that could generate inflation. How the US$160 to 175 billion non-budget investment gap will be financed — whether the stock market can mobilise VND950 trillion when Vietnam's market capitalisation is relatively small by regional standards. Whether the FDI pipeline is sufficient to deliver US$30 to 32 billion in disbursements, and whether global trade tensions — particularly US tariffs on Vietnamese exports — will deter investment. The timeline for the North-South high-speed railway and whether the US$67 billion public investment budget is sufficient for the infrastructure ambitions outlined. Whether the provincial growth targets of 10 per cent or above for 30 of 34 provinces are realistic, given the wide variation in economic development across Vietnam. How Vietnam will manage the inflation risk from simultaneous acceleration in infrastructure spending, credit growth, and industrial expansion.

越南国会为2027年设定史无前例的10%以上GDP增长目标，配套2,280亿美元投资计划

2026年11月7–25日。国会 / VnExpress / VietNamNet / Vietnam+ / 越通社。

经核实的报道

越南第16届国会于2026年11月7日通过决议，为2027年设定"至少10%"的GDP增长目标，出席的469名代表中有467名投票赞成。决议还设定了人均GDP约6,100美元、CPI通胀约4.5%的目标，并将2027年描述为与第14次党代会决议相关的"加速和突破之年"。

各部门目标全面雄心勃勃：工业和建筑12.2%，制造和加工12.3%（达GDP的约25.4%），服务业约9.8%，农林渔业3.8%。工业生产指数目标11.5%至12.5%，零售额增长14%，商品出口增长15%，进口增长12%。劳动生产率目标增长8.6%，数字经济预计达GDP的约23.5%。

投资计划规模庞大。社会投资总额预计占GDP的36%至38%——约6至6.4京越南盾（2,280亿至2,430亿美元）。国家预算发展投资约1.76京越南盾（670亿美元），占总额的27%至29%，剩余的非预算缺口约4.2至4.6京越南盾（1,600亿至1,750亿美元）需来自国内私人和外国来源。外商直接投资到位目标300亿至320亿美元。国有企业集团支出设定在416万亿越南盾（158亿美元）以上，增长40%。股市融资目标约950万亿越南盾（361亿美元）。

越南34个省市中有30个将地区GDP增长目标设定为至少10%，海防13%、广宁12%、河内11.1%、胡志明市10.4%。主要基础设施优先事项包括完成南北高速公路东西段、加快老街-河内-海防铁路和南北高速铁路，以及推进同奈省的隆城国际机场和北宁省的嘉平国际机场。

原创分析

越南2027年10%的GDP增长目标是近年来任何东盟经济体设定的最雄心勃勃的宏观经济目标——也可能是该国现代经济史上最大胆的。作为背景，国际货币基金组织2026年10月预测越南2027年增长7.5%，这意味着国会的目标比国际社会认为可实现的高出约2.5个百分点。越南自1990年代初革新开放改革繁荣期以来未曾实现10%的增长，当时经济体量仅为现在的零头。投资计划揭示了河内如何打算达到目标：动员2,280亿至2,430亿美元的社会投资总额，占GDP的36%至38%。这是巨大的资本承诺。国家预算仅覆盖670亿美元——其余的1,600亿至1,750亿美元必须来自国内私人企业、外商直接投资和股市。300亿至320亿美元的FDI到位目标代表着从2026年前九个月240.8亿美元（增长13.1%）的显著加速。问题是全球投资环境和越南的吸收能力能否维持这一速度。储蓄-投资动态值得审视。根据IMF数据，国民储蓄需要在一年内从GDP的34.9%跃升至39.4%才能在不产生通胀压力的情况下为投资提供资金。如果储蓄跟不上，缺口需要由外国资本流入或国内信贷扩张来填补，两者都有风险——前者对汇率稳定，后者对通胀和金融体系健康。10%增长伴随4.5%的CPI目标也颇具雄心，因为快速的投资驱动扩张通常会通过劳动力、材料和能源需求产生通胀压力。基础设施管线——高速铁路、两个新国际机场、高速公路完工——具有变革性但需要多年执行。政治意义不容低估：467票对469票的近乎一致投票反映了党证明越南发展模式能够实现与中国巅峰时期相当增长率的决心。

未知事项

10%的目标是可实现的还是愿景性的——如果增长不及预期，政府是否会调整政策，还是追求可能产生通胀的刺激措施。1,600亿至1,750亿美元的非预算投资缺口将如何融资——鉴于越南市值按区域标准相对较小，股市能否融资950万亿越南盾。FDI管线是否足以实现300亿至320亿美元的到位目标，以及全球贸易紧张——特别是美国对越南出口的关税——是否会阻碍投资。南北高速铁路的时间表以及670亿美元的公共投资预算是否足以支撑概述的基础设施雄心。34个省市中30个省市10%或以上的增长目标是否现实，考虑到越南各地经济发展水平差异巨大。越南将如何管理基础设施支出、信贷增长和工业扩张同时加速带来的通胀风险。

MSCI Denies Reprieve for Indonesia as Reclassification Consultation Threatens Emerging-Market Status

23–25 November 2026. MSCI / Jakarta Post / Jakarta Globe / Bloomberg.

Verified reporting

MSCI issued a formal statement on 24 November 2026 denying reports that it had softened its stance on Indonesia's potential reclassification from Emerging Markets status. The denial came after Bloomberg reported private talks between MSCI CEO Henry Fernandez and President Prabowo Subianto in which Fernandez was said to have indicated MSCI "did not intend to downgrade" Indonesia. MSCI stated there has been no change to its consultation process for the reclassification proposal announced on 15 October, which remains in effect.

The consultation runs through 30 April 2027, with MSCI's final decision on Indonesia's reclassification due on or before 30 June 2027. If enacted, the reclassification would move Indonesia from the MSCI Emerging Markets Index to standalone market status — a move that would force index-tracking funds to sell Indonesian equities and significantly reduce passive capital flows to the Jakarta market.

MSCI's concerns centre on opaque shareholding structures and suspected coordinated trading, which it has described as creating "profound investability concerns." In June 2026, MSCI gave Indonesian authorities until the November 2026 MSCI Index Review to demonstrate meaningful progress on three issues: coordinated trading, concentrated share ownership, and limited free float. Indonesian regulators — including the Financial Services Authority (OJK), Indonesia Stock Exchange (IDX), and the Indonesian Central Securities Depository (KSEI) — have implemented several reforms: enhanced disclosure requirements for holders of more than 1 per cent of a listed company, more detailed investor classification, a High Shareholding Concentration (HSC) framework, and a roadmap to raise the minimum public free float to 15 per cent.

The Jakarta Composite Index closed at 5,215.9 on 23 November (up 1.35 per cent) amid hopes of a reprieve, then opened higher on 24 November before turning negative after the MSCI denial, before rebounding to close at approximately 5,241.6. A growing number of listed companies are reportedly seeking to go private under the tighter regulatory framework, which the Jakarta Post described as undermining the exchange's appeal. Separately, foreign investors pulled US$900 million from Indonesian equities in November amid global uncertainty, though Indonesia maintained US$4.8 billion in net portfolio inflows for the fourth quarter.

Our analysis

The MSCI reclassification threat is the single most consequential event facing Indonesian capital markets since the Asian Financial Crisis of 1997-98. A downgrade from Emerging Markets to standalone status would not merely be a symbolic demotion — it would mechanically force billions of dollars in passive fund outflows, as the hundreds of billions of dollars in assets tracking the MSCI Emerging Markets Index would no longer include Indonesian equities. The denial of a reprieve, following what appeared to be direct diplomatic intervention by President Prabowo, is a significant escalation. MSCI is signalling that political engagement at the highest level will not substitute for market structure reforms. The 30 April 2027 consultation deadline and 30 June 2027 decision date create a seven-month window during which Indonesian regulators must demonstrate that the reforms they have announced are producing tangible results — not just on paper, but in measurable improvements to shareholding transparency, trading integrity, and free float levels. The reforms undertaken — enhanced disclosure, the HSC framework, the 15 per cent free float roadmap — are directionally correct but face a credibility gap. MSCI's concern is not about the existence of rules but their consistent implementation and sustained effect. Indonesia was upgraded to Emerging Markets status in 2010 after demonstrating improved market accessibility; losing that status sixteen years later would be a devastating reversal. The trend of companies seeking to go private is an underappreciated risk factor. If the tighter regulatory environment causes more companies to delist, it reduces the breadth and depth of the Jakarta market, potentially reinforcing the very concentration concerns that MSCI has flagged. This creates a perverse dynamic where reforms designed to improve market quality could, in the short term, reduce market attractiveness. What we don't know yet

Whether the reforms implemented by OJK, IDX, and KSEI will be judged sufficient by MSCI during the consultation period. How much passive capital would be forced to exit if Indonesia is reclassified — estimates range from US$3 billion to US$8 billion depending on the transition timeline. Whether the government will implement additional reform measures before the April 2027 consultation deadline. How the MSCI decision will interact with Indonesia's broader effort to attract foreign investment, including the US$86.8 billion in Chinese MoUs signed during President Prabowo's recent visit. 未知事项

OJK、IDX和KSEI实施的改革在磋商期间是否会被MSCI认为充分。如果印尼被重新分类，有多少被动资本将被迫退出——估计从30亿美元到80亿美元不等，取决于过渡时间表。是否会有更多公司寻求私有化，进一步减少交易所的深度并可能加剧集中度问题。政府是否会在2027年4月磋商截止前实施额外改革措施。MSCI的决定将如何与印尼更广泛的吸引外资努力互动，包括普拉博沃总统近期访华期间签署的868亿美元中国备忘录。如果MSCI降级实施且资本外流加速，印尼能否维持其投资级主权信用评级。

Our analysis

The private investment surge of 9.4 per cent is particularly significant because it suggests that the AI-driven demand is translating into physical capacity expansion, not just order fulfilment from existing facilities. 泰国投资委员会报告2026年1月至9月投资申请1.71万亿泰铢（约503亿美元），增长28%，其中约70%与AI供应链相关。MUFG指出泰国的贸易条件处于27年来最弱水平，且科技出口热潮无法完全抵消疲弱的国内增长。

原创分析

泰国的经济图景正在成为东盟内部结构性分化的案例研究。泰铢兑美元6.7%的跌幅——19个月来最弱——是货币政策本身无法解决的更深层问题的症状。与美国3个百分点的利差使泰国资产对寻求收益的全球资本缺乏吸引力，但泰国银行无法在不进一步抑制本已疲弱的国内经济的情况下加息。这是K型复苏所造成的陷阱：与AI相关的出口部门正在增长并吸引503亿美元的投资申请（其中70%与AI相关），但这种增长集中在经济的狭窄切片中，产生有限的国内就业和收入溢出效应。旅游业下滑尤其令人担忧，因为旅游历来是泰国最可靠的增长引擎，也是与国内就业和消费联系最直接的行业。入境游客4.59%的下降以及中国游客在最后两个月可能减少20%的警告表明，泰国银行所标记的下行风险——中国放缓减少可自由支配旅行——正在成为现实。修订后的3,150万游客目标与2025年的3,297万相比，政府已隐性承认该行业正在收缩。助理行长唐·纳空塔坦率地评估货币立场"非常非常宽松"且这是"必要的因为经济一直相当疲弱"，对于央行官员来说异常直白，信号表明泰国银行认为进一步放松的空间有限。在1.00%的水平上，政策利率已处于历史低位，泰铢疲弱意味着进一步降息将加速货币贬值并可能输入通胀。MUFG指出的27年来最差贸易条件具有结构性重要性：这意味着即使泰国在出口量上增加，其收到的价格相对于进口支付的价格正在恶化。这直接转化为贸易带来的实际收入收益降低，解释了为何强劲的出口量增长未能转化为更广泛的繁荣。行长维泰观察到利率无法解决结构性问题是准确的，但引发了什么能解决的问题——以及泰国政治建制是否有意愿进行将经济潜在增长率重新提升至3%以上所需的结构性改革（在教育、生产率、中小企业融资和农业现代化方面）。

未知事项

泰国银行是否会在2027年进一步降息还是维持在1.00%，鉴于支持增长和防止泰铢进一步贬值之间的紧张关系。1.71万亿泰铢BOI投资申请的构成——有多少代表约束性承诺而非意向表达，以及实际资本部署的时间表。11月-12月中国游客20%的下降是否会实现以及对旅游收入的全年影响。政府是否会采取财政刺激措施支持国内需求，以及有多大的财政空间。提高泰国潜在增长率所需的中小企业融资、教育和生产率结构性改革的时间表。27年来贸易条件低点是周期性现象还是反映了泰国出口组合的永久性结构恶化。

ING Expects BSP to Downshift to 25bp Hike in December as Philippine Inflation Heads Toward 8% and Rice Prices Surge 20.7%

5–25 November 2026. Philippine Statistics Authority / Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas / ING / Philippine Star / Manila Bulletin.

Verified reporting

ING's regional head of research Deepali Bhargava forecasts that the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) will raise its benchmark rate by 25 basis points at its 17 December meeting, downshifting from the 50-basis-point hike delivered in October. The BSP's benchmark rate currently stands at 5.50 per cent following cumulative tightening of 125 basis points in 2026. ING's base case is that the larger October hike was delivered in anticipation of the inflation surge, and that a second consecutive jumbo hike is less likely if oil prices continue easing and Q3 GDP prints weak.

October 2026 headline CPI rose to 7.8 per cent year-on-year, up from 7.2 per cent in September, in line with ING's forecast but above the consensus expectation of 7.6 per cent. Core inflation — excluding volatile food and energy — accelerated to 5.4 per cent from 4.8 per cent, marking six consecutive months above the BSP's target. Rice inflation remained at 20.7 per cent year-on-year, roughly unchanged from September. Fuel and electricity prices added approximately 70 basis points to the headline figure, while non-rice food inflation eased modestly led by vegetables and seafood.

ING flagged upside risks to food inflation from recent rice price rises across Asia and elevated vegetable oil and corn prices relative to past severe El Nino episodes. Core inflation was driven higher by transport fares and restaurant and hotel services, indicating that price pressures are broadening beyond food and energy. A sharp fall in global oil prices in recent weeks — amid hopes of US-Iran de-escalation — and a weaker US dollar with firmer peso provide some offsetting relief.

Q3 2026 GDP growth came in at 2.1 per cent, the weakest quarterly result since the pandemic. Barclays has separately forecast that headline inflation could breach 8.0 per cent in November. The Philippine Stock Exchange Index has fallen 28 per cent year-to-date with net foreign selling of PHP 123 billion, trading at approximately 9.5 times forward earnings — the lowest valuation in over a decade.

Our analysis

The Philippines' stagflation is now entrenched rather than emerging. With 7.8 per cent headline inflation, 5.4 per cent core inflation, and 2.1 per cent GDP growth, the country occupies the worst macroeconomic quadrant — high and broadening inflation combined with decelerating growth. No other ASEAN economy is in this position, and the contrast with Malaysia (1.9 per cent inflation, 5.5 per cent growth) is now a factor of four on inflation and more than half on growth. ING's expectation of a 25-basis-point hike rather than a repeat of the October 50-basis-point move reflects a judgement that the BSP has front-loaded its tightening response. But the fundamental problem remains: the inflation-adjusted policy rate is still approximately minus 2.3 per cent, meaning that despite 125 basis points of cumulative hikes, real borrowing costs remain below inflation and monetary policy is effectively still accommodative. At 25 basis points per meeting, it would take several more hikes to bring the real rate to neutral, let alone restrictive territory. The rice inflation figure of 20.7 per cent is the single most politically sensitive number in the Philippines' economic data because rice is the staple food for the majority of the population and rice spending disproportionately affects lower-income households. The World Bank's earlier intervention — explicitly stating that food price inflation is a supply-side problem requiring supply-side solutions — was a pointed criticism of the policy mix. Rate hikes cannot increase rice production or fix typhoon-damaged supply chains. The broadening of core inflation to 5.4 per cent, driven by transport fares and hospitality services, suggests that the problem is evolving from a food-and-fuel price shock into a generalised inflation episode where businesses across the economy are adjusting prices upward. This is the stage at which inflation expectations risk becoming unmoored and wage-price spirals become self-reinforcing. The easing in global oil prices and the firmer peso provide some short-term relief, but these are external factors over which the Philippines has no control. The 28 per cent decline in the PSEi and PHP 123 billion in net foreign selling reflect capital markets' judgement that the Philippines lacks a credible near-term catalyst for improvement.

What we don't know yet

Whether the BSP will deliver a 25 or 50-basis-point hike at the 17 December meeting, and what forward guidance it will provide about the January 2027 meeting. Whether November inflation will breach 8.0 per cent as Barclays forecasts, and whether this would force the BSP to accelerate its tightening pace. The timeline for rice import liberalisation or tariff reductions that could bring down food prices, and the political feasibility given domestic rice farmer opposition. Whether the fiscal deficit — at 6.1 per cent of GDP through nine months — will narrow in Q4 or whether social spending will continue to expand to cushion the impact of inflation on lower-income households. Whether S&P's sovereign credit rating will follow the growth downgrade — the Philippines is currently rated BBB+ (investment grade), but sustained stagflation could trigger a negative outlook. Whether the government has specific structural reform plans for agricultural supply chains or is relying primarily on monetary policy to control inflation.

ING预期BSP将在12月缩减至加息25个基点，菲律宾通胀趋向8%且大米价格飙升20.7%

2026年11月5–25日。菲律宾统计局 / 菲律宾中央银行 / ING / 菲律宾星报 / 马尼拉公报。

经核实的报道

ING亚太区研究主管迪帕莉·巴尔加瓦预测菲律宾中央银行（BSP）将在12月17日会议上加息25个基点，从10月的50个基点加息缩减。BSP基准利率在2026年累计紧缩125个基点后目前为5.50%。ING的基本判断是10月的大幅加息是提前应对通胀飙升，如果油价继续回落且第三季度GDP疲弱，第二次连续大幅加息的可能性较低。

2026年10月整体CPI同比上升至7.8%，高于9月的7.2%，与ING预测一致但高于市场预期的7.6%。核心通胀——剔除波动性食品和能源——从4.8%加速至5.4%，连续六个月高于BSP目标。大米通胀维持在同比20.7%，与9月大致持平。燃料和电力价格为整体数据贡献了约70个基点，而非大米食品通胀在蔬菜和海鲜带动下温和回落。

ING标记了食品通胀的上行风险，来自亚洲近期大米价格上涨以及相对于过去严重厄尔尼诺事件的高位植物油和玉米价格。核心通胀被交通费和餐饮住宿服务推高，表明价格压力正在食品和能源之外扩大。近几周全球油价大幅下跌——受美伊缓和希望推动——以及美元走弱和比索走强提供了一些抵消性缓解。

2026年第三季度GDP增长为2.1%，疫情以来最弱的季度表现。巴克莱另外预测11月整体通胀可能突破8.0%。菲律宾证券交易所指数年初至今下跌28%，外资净卖出1,230亿比索，前瞻市盈率约9.5倍——十多年来最低估值。

原创分析

菲律宾的滞胀现已根深蒂固而非初露端倪。在7.8%的整体通胀、5.4%的核心通胀和2.1%的GDP增长下，该国占据着最糟糕的宏观经济象限——高且正在扩大的通胀与减速的增长并存。没有其他东盟经济体处于这一位置，与马来西亚（1.9%通胀、5.5%增长）的对比在通胀上现为四倍、增长上超过一半。ING对25个基点加息（而非重复10月50个基点）的预期反映了BSP已前置其紧缩应对的判断。但根本问题仍在：经通胀调整的政策利率仍约为负2.3%，意味着尽管累计加息125个基点，实际借贷成本仍低于通胀，货币政策实际上仍为宽松。按每次会议25个基点计算，需要再加息数次才能将实际利率带到中性水平，更不用说限制性领域。20.7%的大米通胀是菲律宾经济数据中政治上最敏感的数字，因为大米是大多数人口的主食，大米支出对低收入家庭影响最大。世界银行此前的干预——明确指出食品价格通胀是需要供给侧解决方案的供给侧问题——是对政策组合的尖锐批评。加息无法增加大米产量或修复台风破坏的供应链。核心通胀扩大至5.4%，受交通费和餐饮服务驱动，表明问题正从食品和燃料价格冲击演变为整个经济体企业普遍上调价格的全面通胀事件。这是通胀预期可能脱锚、工资-价格螺旋变得自我强化的阶段。全球油价回落和比索走强提供了一些短期缓解，但这些是菲律宾无法控制的外部因素。PSEi 28%的跌幅和1,230亿比索的外资净卖出反映了资本市场对菲律宾缺乏可信近期改善催化剂的判断。

未知事项

BSP将在12月17日会议上加息25还是50个基点，以及将对2027年1月会议提供什么前瞻性指引。11月通胀是否会如巴克莱预测般突破8.0%，以及这是否会迫使BSP加速紧缩步伐。大米进口自由化或关税减免以降低食品价格的时间表，以及考虑到国内稻农反对的政治可行性。前九个月占GDP 6.1%的财政赤字是否会在第四季度收窄，还是社会支出将继续扩大以缓冲通胀对低收入家庭的冲击。标普的主权信用评级是否会跟随增长预测下调——菲律宾目前评级为BBB+（投资级），但持续的滞胀可能触发负面展望。政府是否有针对农业供应链的具体结构性改革计划，还是主要依赖货币政策来控制通胀。
